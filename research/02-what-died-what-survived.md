## 3. Cross-cutting failure patterns, ranked by kill count

1. **Token emissions as the only loop → ponzi decay the moment deposits slow.** Axie (2.8M→99k DAU), StepN ($1,400→$90 sneakers, forced GMT halving Jan 2026), Hamster Kombat (300M→13M), Notcoin (−74%), Star Atlas (11.8M ATLAS/day vs an 8.5M target). If the payout comes from new entrants rather than from a sink funded by *consumption*, the curve is a countdown, not a chart.
2. **Ran out of money before finding a retaining loop.** The single most-cited stated cause across 2025 shutdowns: Nyan Heroes, Ember Sword, The Mystery Society, Kryptomon, Valeria, Anterris, Blast Royale, Ragnarok, Dragginz. Web3 budgets were AAA-sized; web3 revenue was indie-sized; VC dropped **93% YoY**.
3. **Selling assets before shipping a game.** Ember Sword's **$200M+ land pledges**, Star Atlas ship sales, every land sale. Selling to speculators creates a creditor class that must be paid in appreciation forever, which is a permanent tax on every future design decision.
4. **Financialization crowds out fun.** Once an item has a price, the optimal play is spreadsheet arbitrage, not play. Mojo Melee's team said crypto made the game "too big and complex"; Moonfrost and Jungle both *removed* crypto to survive.
5. **Speculators are not players — the funnel was always fake.** Nyan Heroes had 1M playtesters and 250k wishlists and still died. Goombles: **339 installs after 2+ years**. Airdrop-farm DAU converts to retained DAU at ~zero.
6. **Chain-hopping / migration as a substitute for product-market fit.** MetalCore (Immutable→Solana), Pirate Nation (Polygon→Arbitrum→Abstract), Realms of Alurya (Treasure→Ronin), Off The Grid (Avalanche→+Solana). Each migration resets tooling, splits liquidity, and buys a few weeks of grant money.
7. **Grant/publisher dependency.** Realms of Alurya died when Treasure cut its grant; Ragnarok died when the Sky Mavis partnership ended (Apr 2025); Loot Legends died when HYCHAIN consolidated publishing. Your runway is someone else's discretionary budget.
8. **Live-ops cost exceeds LTV of a tiny base.** Age of Dino: high live-ops cost + poor acquisition. OpenSeason: literally "server costs are too expensive."
9. **Smart-contract risk is an existence-level game risk.** RoboKiden: **$38.7M stolen**, project over. 120+ hacks across DeFi YTD 2026.
10. **Token launch scheduled before the game is fun.** Tokyo Beast: launched 9 Jun 2025, dead 24 Aug 2025. The TGE is the peak and everything after it is a distribution-schedule problem.
11. **Governance/insider structure poisons the community.** Blade of God X (allegedly abandoned the roadmap post-raise, withheld salaries), Rumble Kong League (celebrity hype, no delivery). Star Atlas: **34% of POLIS staked, predominantly whales.**

## 4. What crypto genuinely adds — and what it doesn't

### Genuinely adds (short, honest list)
1. **Permissionless, real-money wagering and markets at global scale, with no bank, no licence per jurisdiction, and instant settlement.** This is the *only* category with unambiguous product-market fit: $51B on-chain gambling volume 2025, $44.8B/month prediction volume, pump.fun at $1B cumulative revenue. Everything else on this list is secondary.
2. **A cap table for a game economy.** You can pay creators, ranked players, market makers, and track owners a *programmatic revenue share* without contracts or payment rails — Photo Finish LIVE's $9.7M in track earnings is the clearest real example.
3. **Genuine asset scarcity with credible, verifiable supply commitments.** Not "you own your items" — you never really do, the servers still gate utility. The real property is that the *issuer cannot secretly print more*, which matters for collectibles and for wagering integrity.
4. **Verifiable randomness and auditable settlement.** For anything with money on it, "you can check the RNG" is a real product feature (Zeebit's whole pitch). Web2 casinos require you to trust a regulator; here you check a hash.
5. **Instant, global, sub-cent payouts to unbanked players.** Axie's scholarship system was socially destructive but the payment rail worked; the LATAM/SEA payout use case is real.
6. **Composability as free distribution.** Your item shows up in Backpack, Jupiter, Magic Eden, Blinks without a BD deal. Modest, but real and cheap.

### Claimed but false
- **"True ownership."** You own a pointer. The studio owns the renderer, the servers, and the rules. Every 2025 shutdown proved this — Nyan Heroes NFT holders own JPEGs of a game that no longer exists.
- **"Interoperable assets across games."** Fifteen years of promises, effectively zero shipped examples anyone plays. Art style, balance, and engine constraints make it economically nonsensical.
- **"Player-owned economies are more fun."** They are more *financialized*. Adding a price to an item converts play into labor (Axie) or arbitrage (StepN).
- **"Web3 solves user acquisition."** It buys mercenaries. 1M Nyan Heroes playtesters → dead. 300M Hamster Kombat users → 13M.
- **"Tokens align incentives with players."** They align incentives with *sellers*. Any holder's optimal move is exit liquidity for the next holder.
- **"Blockchain reduces cheating / bots."** It's the opposite: monetary reward makes botting profitable and multi-accounting rational (documented as an explicit killer of the tap-to-earn genre).
- **"Cheaper infrastructure."** Moonfrost, Mojo Melee, and Jungle all removed crypto specifically because integration cost exceeded benefit.
- **"Trading is retention."** Trading is *engagement*. Photo Finish LIVE's $50M in race fees is real, but the category ceiling is still below a mid-tier DEX.

## 5. Player acquisition reality in 2026

**Where players actually come from now — in order of real effectiveness:**

1. **Existing crypto traders, recycled.** The addressable market is ~3.2M daily active Solana wallets, and it is largely the *same people* across pump.fun, Axiom, prediction markets, and games. Almost nobody in web3 gaming is acquiring a genuinely new gamer; they are competing for attention within a trading population.
2. **X/Twitter + quest-farming, still the default.** The 2026 playbook is unchanged: follow, join Discord, test the build, get a token/NFT allocation — which, per Bitmedia, "generates a financially motivated audience before there is even a product to critique." It works for wallet counts and fails for retention. Discord Quests do convert on the mechanical level (one crypto studio: **72% quest accept rate, 45% mint completion**) — but that measures a claim, not a session. ([Bitmedia](https://bitmedia.io/blog/user-acquisition-web3-games-crypto-exchanges), [TokenMinds](https://tokenminds.co/blog/tips-of-discord-marketing))
3. **Steam — still the biggest prize and still mostly closed.** Steam's rules have barred blockchain/NFT/crypto games since 2021 and have **not** been formally reversed. What changed is enforcement in practice: titles like **Off The Grid**, **The Bornless**, and **Sparkball** are on Steam with **optional or scaled-back** web3 features. The operative rule for 2026: *you can ship on Steam if the crypto is invisible and optional.* ([CCN](https://www.ccn.com/news/technology/steam-web3-game-experimental/), [PlayToEarn](https://playtoearn.com/blockchaingames/steam))
4. **Epic Games Store — open, permissive, weak conversion.** Allows blockchain games given legal compliance, disclosure, and age rating; **175+ blockchain titles listed**, ~20 launching in a single year. Aurory and Nyan Heroes both had EGS presence. Nyan Heroes died anyway — shelf space is not demand. ([BlockchainGamer](https://www.blockchaingamer.biz/news/34430/175-blockchain-games-epic-games-store/), [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/gaming-news/epic-games-store-changes-rules-to-allow-blockchain-games-3562838))
5. **Mobile app stores — usable, with a hard rule: never mention earning.** **Google Play** (since 2023) permits tokenized assets/NFTs with mandatory disclosure and an explicit ban on **promoting or glamorizing earnings** from play or trading. **Apple** loosened in **May 2025** post-antitrust-ruling: US storefront apps may now include buttons/external links to browse NFT collections, and may list, mint, transfer, and display NFTs — but Apple remains stricter on external transactions and IAP bypass. Practical consequence: Photo Finish LIVE and Honeyland ship as normal mobile games; the token is a back-room feature. **No major new 2026 policy change found — treat as current-through-mid-2025.** ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/317589/apple-loosens-nft-crypto-ios-app-rules), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/12/google-play-policy-blockchain-apps-gaming-nfts-digital-assets/))
6. **Solana Mobile dApp Store — small, high-intent, low-friction.** **200k+ Seekers shipped, $5B+ on-chain volume, 500+ dApps, $100M+ mobile activity in Seeker Season**, and SMS is now licensable to any Android OEM (MWC 2026). This is the only mobile channel with **no crypto restrictions and no 30% cut** — but the ceiling is a few hundred thousand devices, and its top-20 list is dominated by Jupiter and fitness apps, not games. Best used as a *launch* channel and a payments escape hatch, never as your growth plan. ([Solana Mobile](https://blog.solanamobile.com/post/sms-goes-global-and-season-2-wrapup), [@solanamobile](https://x.com/solanamobile/status/1993375627404058820))
7. **Backpack / wallet-native surfaces.** Backpack's points → BP airdrop (Mar 2026, 25% community, 0% insider float, 1% to Mad Lads) shows the model: the wallet is the funnel and the campaign is the game. Sanctum/Jupiter-style gamified campaigns work the same way — they acquire *farmers* extremely efficiently and retain them only as long as the emission lasts.
8. **Telegram mini-apps — a dead channel to copy, a live channel to use.** Tap-to-earn as a *genre* is finished (Notcoin's own team said so; Hamster Kombat 300M→13M). But Telegram as a **distribution and payments surface** — zero-install, native social graph — is still the cheapest onboarding on the internet. The lesson is to port the *distribution*, not the clicker. ([DailyGame](https://www.dailygame.net/telegram-mini-apps-and-the-new-wave-of-bot-based-games-how-ton-gaming-reshaped-mobile-play-in-2026/), [CryptoNews](https://cryptonews.com/news/hamster-kombat-loses-users-as-paws-mini-app-gains-ground/))
9. **Blinks/X-native games — marginal.** X never natively integrated Blink rendering; requires a Phantom/Solflare extension; mobile is weak. Fine as a viral tipping/mint primitive, not a customer-acquisition channel. **PARTIALLY UNVERIFIED for 2026.**

**The 2026 acquisition rule, stated plainly:** every channel that will let you talk about earning delivers speculators who leave; every channel that reaches actual gamers (Steam, Apple, Google) forbids you from talking about earning. That contradiction is the acquisition problem, and it has no clever workaround — only a choice about which audience you are building for.

## What the record actually recommends

- Build the **market** first and the game around it (Photo Finish LIVE, pump.fun, prediction markets, casinos), or build a **real game** and let crypto be an invisible optional rail (Off The Grid, Moonfrost's post-pivot, BattleFrens' no-native-token design).
- **Do not issue a native game token.** The 2026 evidence — stablecoins displacing native in-game currencies, BattleFrens paying prizes in SOL, 95% average token drawdown — is that the token is the liability, not the asset.
- **Do not sell land or assets pre-launch.** It converts your future players into creditors.
- **Budget indie, not AAA.** Nyan Heroes with 1M playtesters could not raise; assume you cannot either.
- **Assume the reward loop is the game.** If removing the token kills your retention, you never had retention.
