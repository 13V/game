# Wave 3 — concepts with the compliance gate removed
Same builder profile as wave 2, but regulatory structuring was explicitly out of scope. This recovers mechanics the wave-2 gate suppressed — chiefly **asymmetric, consequential randomness**, the engine behind poker, Balatro, XCOM, backgammon and Magic, where the skill *is* variance management.
Four lenses: `variance-strategy` (skill expressed through risk), `house-and-oracle` (player-vs-program with verifiable fairness and a stakeable bankroll), `persistent-stakes-world` (asynchronous territory/raiding with real spoils), `market-native-sim` (price discovery and speculation as core mechanics).
**These are unjudged and unranked** — the run was stopped after critique, before the judge panel. Each concept does carry a full hostile review from a trio including a professional advantage player. Note `ASSAY` was independently proposed by three of the four generators.

## DELVE
*Lens: house-and-oracle*

**Pitch.** A 4-minute dungeon run where the loot table, the odds, and the house's bank account are all public on-chain — and if you'd rather be the house, you can put money into the vault instead and earn the edge.

**Core loop.** Stake 0.02–0.67 SOL as your purse. You start with 10 Health, 12 Light, and a 1.00x multiplier. Each floor shows three doors, each marked with a glyph that is truthful 70% of the time (the 70% is a published on-chain constant). You can spend 1 Light to true-scout one door before choosing. Pick a door: outcomes are Vein (+0.15x to +0.90x multiplier), Hazard (−1 to −4 HP), Cache (+2 Light or +2 HP), or Collapse (−0.20x and −2 HP). Every floor also burns 1 Light passively; at 0 Light the glyphs stop showing entirely and you are choosing blind. At any point you press BANK and take stake × multiplier. At 0 HP you bust and lose the purse. Twelve floors max; the multiplier band ceiling rises 1.05x → 8.0x across the run. A run is 30–40 discrete decisions in 3–5 minutes, each one a real tradeoff between Light (information), HP (survivability), and depth (multiplier).

**Depth curve.** Day 1 you play on gut. Day 100 you are running a 12-deep dynamic program over the state (floor, HP, Light, multiplier) and you know the exact scout-vs-descend threshold at HP=4, Light=3, floor 8 — because that threshold is computable and the tables are on-chain, so the community will publish solvers. The house publishes two numbers every epoch: optimal-play RTP (97.0%) and realized population RTP (last epoch: 92.4%). Closing that 4.6-point gap is the entire mastery ladder and it is measured publicly per wallet. What keeps it from being solved-and-dead: every 7-day epoch, Switchboard VRF draws 3 of 24 published Vein modifiers (e.g. 'Hazards deal +1 but Caches give +1 Light', 'glyph accuracy 55% but scouting is free') and they change the solve. The re-solve race each Monday is the retention event. Honest caveat: at optimal play this is still −3% EV. Retention here is the retention of video poker and speedrunning — mastery that is scored, published, and paid via the ladder — not the retention of a game you'd play for free forever.

**Skill vs chance.** Per run: maybe 25% skill, 75% variance — a great player busts on floor 3 constantly. Across 500 runs: skill is nearly all of it, because skill is expressed as a 4.6-point RTP difference compounding over volume, not as win/loss on any single run. That ratio is correct for this design specifically because the LP bankroll needs high per-run variance to be exciting and low aggregate variance to be safe, and the only way to get both is a long-horizon skill signal on top of a fat-tailed short-horizon payout. If a strong player could reliably win any given run, the vault would be drainable in an afternoon.

**Why on-chain.** Two things break off-chain. First, the LP side: 'anyone can deposit into the house bankroll, see every liability, and withdraw pro-rata' is a permissionless capital market that a web server cannot offer without a prospectus and a custodian. Second, the audit: the pitch is literally 'we publish the loot table as program constants, the VRF proof for your seed, the vault balance, and the max-bet rule as an on-chain assertion — you never trust an RTP claim again.' The gameplay itself does not need a blockchain; MagicBlock is just fast infrastructure. The trust surface is the product.

**Solana primitives.**
  - MagicBlock Private ER (TEE-backed) — the run seed and unrevealed door contents live inside the TEE so the player provably cannot precompute the dungeon; the full transcript plus seed is published on undelegation for anyone to recompute
  - MagicBlock Ephemeral Rollup — ~10ms slots, 0 SOL base fee per tx, 0.0003 SOL/session, 0.0001 SOL/commit, so 40 in-run decisions cost nothing and only settlement costs money
  - Switchboard On-Demand VRF — seed commitment at run start, proof published at settle
  - Per-run PDA escrow (per-player writable accounts see near-zero priority fees under Solana's local fee markets), closed at settle for rent refund
  - 16 sharded vault PDAs so the bankroll is never one hot writable account bumping the 12M CU/writable-account/block ceiling or its own local fee market
  - Jito bundle (up to 5 tx, atomic, same slot) for stake-escrow + delegate + run-open so a run can never half-open
  - Token-2022 for optional USDC purses (165-byte token account, ~0.00204 SOL rent, recoverable)

**Money flow.** Value enters as player stakes and as LP deposits. It exits as payouts and LP withdrawals. Target steady state: 300 runs/day at 0.30 SOL average = 90 SOL/day wagered. House edge 3.0% of wagered = 2.70 SOL/day gross. Protocol fee is 25% of house gross = 0.675 SOL/day to the dev (~$54/day at SOL $80); LPs receive 2.03 SOL/day. On a 400 SOL bankroll that is ~0.5%/day gross yield to LPs before variance — and the variance is real, LP share price will visibly draw down 5–8% on a bad week. LP shares are a non-transferable position PDA, deliberately not a tradable token: no creditor class, no secondary market to pump. Withdrawals queue for 24h at the then-current share price so an LP cannot exit ahead of a bad streak or front-run their own losing run.

**House edge / rake.** 3.0% house edge against optimal play, calculated as 1 − RTP over the full decision tree; ~7.6% against actual population play. There is no separate rake — the edge IS the revenue, and it is asserted in the program: the payout table constants are public and a `verify_rtp` view instruction lets anyone recompute expected return. Players tolerate it because 3% is roughly a third of a slot machine's take and a tenth of a lottery's, they can see it is 3%, and because their personal RTP is a function of their own play — the marketing line is 'the house edge is a skill check, and we publish your score.'

**Bots.** Day one a bot plays optimal lines at 97% RTP and loses 3% of everything it wagers. That is the whole defense: a negative-EV reward cannot be farmed, and multi-accounting buys nothing because there are no signup bonuses, no per-wallet drops, and no rate-limited rewards. Bots are welcome and are in fact the best customers — highest volume, most predictable losses. The genuine threat is not a bot playing well but a bot finding a positive-EV line caused by a math error in my tables. Three defenses: (1) the hard on-chain max stake of bankroll/600 bounds the drain rate to ~0.17% of the bankroll per run, so an exploit takes thousands of runs to matter and shows up as an anomalous per-wallet RTP on a dashboard I watch; (2) VRF-drawn epoch modifiers are only ever drawn from a set I have Monte-Carlo'd to ≥96.5% optimal RTP; (3) a standing bounty of 10% of one epoch's house gross, paid from rake, to anyone who reports a positive-EV line — cheaper than being drained.

**Cold start plan.** This is player-versus-program, so it works with exactly one player online — there is no concurrency requirement anywhere in the design. Week one is 8 players and a 50 SOL bankroll I seed myself, which by the bankroll/600 rule caps stakes at 0.083 SOL. Small stakes are the honest week-one product and I say so. The cold start I actually have to solve is LP-side, not player-side, and that is a Crypto Twitter pitch rather than a game-design problem: 8 players × 20 runs/day × 0.2 SOL = 32 SOL/day wagered, 0.96 SOL/day of edge on a 50 SOL bankroll, ~1.9%/day. That number is what gets posted, and every LP who deposits becomes someone with a financial reason to recruit players. The max-stake rule creates the flywheel: more bankroll mechanically raises the stake ceiling, so players lobby for LPs and LPs lobby for players.

**First session.** Minute 0: a Crypto Twitter link opens a web page, no install, no download. Minute 0–1: 'Connect wallet' (Phantom/Backpack/Solflare); if they have no SOL, an embedded MoonPay/Coinbase Onramp widget or a Jupiter swap from any token they hold. Minute 1: they see the vault — current balance, current max stake, live population RTP, and the last 20 settled runs with seed hashes. Minute 2: a free practice run with play chips, identical mechanics, no VRF, so the first thing they experience is the decision tree and not a deposit. Minute 4: they finish the practice run, see 'you played at 88% RTP; optimal was 97%', and are annoyed. Minute 5: they stake 0.05 SOL. One transaction escrows the stake and delegates the run PDA to the ER; from then on every door click is an ER tx at 0 SOL base fee and ~10ms, so it feels like a normal web game. Minute 9: they bank at 2.4x, one commit + undelegate lands 0.12 SOL in their wallet in under a second, and the run PDA closes and refunds its rent. Minute 10: the settle screen shows the VRF proof and a 'recompute this run yourself' button.

**Viral mechanic.** Every settled run mints a shareable transcript card — floor-by-floor, banked multiplier, seed hash, and a one-click verifier — rendered as CSS/emoji with no art budget. The bigger one is the public per-wallet RTP ledger: because every run is on-chain, lifetime RTP is a real, uncheatable, doxxable number, and a leaderboard of 'who plays closest to optimal' is a status object that gamblers do not currently have anywhere. Second surface: LPs posting yield screenshots, which is a completely different audience reaching a completely different timeline for the same product.

**Four-week MVP.** Ship: 8 floors (not 12), 3 doors, HP + Light only, one fixed modifier set, glyph accuracy 70%, Private ER integration (this is the load-bearing piece and gets week 1–2), Switchboard VRF at run open, LP vault with deposit/queued-withdraw and the hard bankroll/600 max-stake assert, per-run PDA close-and-refund, free practice mode, and a public verifier page. Cut explicitly: the 24 epoch modifiers and their VRF rotation, the Deep Ladder payout, USDC purses, mobile layout, the 16-way vault sharding (one vault PDA handles 750 settles/sec at 400ms slots, so sharding is a scaling task not a launch task), and any leaderboard beyond a raw on-chain RTP query.

**Biggest risk.** That −3% EV plus a solved decision tree is not enough to bring anyone back on day 30. The mechanic has to be interesting with play chips, and if the practice mode has bad retention I have a casino with a dungeon skin and should say so out loud rather than shipping the vault. Secondary and more concrete: Private ER integration is a hard dependency for the hidden-information design — without a TEE there is no honest way for a program to keep a secret from the player it is playing against — so if PER integration slips, the game does not have a fallback that preserves push-your-luck tension.

### Hostile review

- **Verdict:** buildable-with-caveats
- **Solo-dev estimate:** 16 weeks
- **Degenerate strategy:** **Install the solver; then stop playing.** The decision tree is a finite MDP over (floor, HP, Light, multiplier) with ~595k states and no hidden opponent — it is exactly solvable by backward induction in under a second, and the tables are on-chain program constants by design, so the community publishes the solver in week one. There is no meta, no opponent, and no positive-EV state anywhere in the game: the best possible outcome of perfect play is −3% EV. Once solved, DELVE reduces to a lookup table plus a bank/continue threshold, and the only remaining decision is bet sizing, where the answer for any EV-aware player is zero. The epoch modifier rotation does not save this — 3-of-24 draws from a published set means 2,024 pre-solvable variants; the community solves all of them once and ships a dropdown. The 'Monday re-solve race' is a `cargo run` that finishes before coffee. Compare video poker, which the design invokes: video poker retained because full-pay paytables were genuinely +EV (Deuces Wild 100.76%) plus comps. DELVE has neither a +EV paytable nor comps. Its mastery ladder pays you by moving from −7.6% to −3%, i.e. it is a ladder whose top rung is still losing.
- **Technical blockers:**
  - **The Private ER dependency is unnecessary and it is eating 3-4 weeks of a 4-week budget.** The stated hidden-information requirement is 'the program must keep a secret from the player.' That is a solved 2013 problem: operator commits H(server_seed) at run open, player supplies client_seed, every door is HMAC(server_seed, client_seed || floor_index), server_seed is revealed at settle and anyone recomputes the transcript. The operator cannot change the seed (hash committed), cannot bias it (client seed), and the player cannot precompute (no seed). This is exactly what bustabit and Stake ship. PERs are real (Intel TDX, mainnet since March 2026) but you are buying a TEE to solve a problem a 32-byte hash already solves. Cutting PER + ER drops the build to ~8 weeks and removes the single dependency the design itself names as fatal-if-slipped.
  - **40 ER transactions per run means 40 signatures. Phantom will not auto-approve them.** The design says 'every door click is an ER tx' and never mentions session keys. You need a delegated ephemeral keypair held in browser memory with a scoped authority (can advance a run, cannot open runs, cannot withdraw), plus rotation and a revoke path. That is 1-2 weeks and it is the highest-severity security surface in the build — a leaked session key that can BANK on the player's behalf is a griefing vector, and one that can OPEN runs drains the purse.
  - **Sharded vaults and a single LP share price are mutually exclusive.** Share price = total_assets / total_shares. If assets live across 16 shard PDAs, either every settle writes one global accounting account (the hot writable account you sharded to avoid, 12M CU/block cap) or share price is stale and a depositor can time entry against an unswept shard. At 300 settles/day the CU cap is irrelevant anyway — 12M / ~50k CU per settle = 240 settles per 400ms block = 600/sec. Ship one vault, one accounting account, and delete the sharding line from the pitch; it is solving a problem you will not have at 100x target volume.
  - **The stated per-run drain bound is wrong by 8x.** 'bankroll/600 bounds the drain rate to ~0.17% of bankroll per run' computes the STAKE as a fraction of bankroll. Maximum payout is 8.0x, so maximum single-run bankroll loss is 8 × bankroll/600 = 1.33% of bankroll, not 0.17%.
  - **The epoch modifiers are being validated with the wrong tool.** 'Monte-Carlo'd to ≥96.5% optimal RTP' — Monte Carlo simulates a POLICY. If the simulated policy is heuristic, you are measuring heuristic RTP and systematically under-estimating optimal RTP, which is the exact direction that hides a +EV line. The state space is (floor 0-12) × (HP 0-10) × (Light 0-25) × (multiplier discretised at 0.05 to 8.0 = 160) ≈ 595k states with 4 actions. Exact backward-induction DP solves it in under a second. C(24,3) = 2,024 modifier triples × 595k states ≈ 1.2B state evaluations = one afternoon on one machine. Solve it exactly. Never Monte Carlo a table you are underwriting.
- **Economic holes:**
  - **3% is not competitive and the market you are recruiting from knows the number.** Stake Mines runs 99% RTP (1% edge). Bustabit runs 99%. Stake Dragon Tower runs 98%. Moonbet is already live on Solana at 99%+ with community house pools. DELVE asks for 3x to 4x the incumbent edge and the entire pitch is 'we publish the edge so you can check it.' Publishing a worse number does not sell it. The 3.2M Solana wallets you are fishing in are the same wallets already on those sites.
  - **The revenue model requires players to play badly while the marketing teaches them to play well.** Projected dev revenue is 25% of a 3.0% edge; the 7.6% population edge is the number that actually funds anything interesting, and closing that gap is explicitly the advertised mastery ladder. You are paying for your own revenue's destruction. A community solver browser extension will exist by day 3 (the DP is 595k states) and population RTP converges to ~97%. House gross falls from 7.6% to 3.0% — a 61% revenue cut — and the dev's cut at TARGET volume of 300 runs/day × 0.30 SOL is 0.675 SOL/day = **$54/day**. At the honest week-one figure of 8 players × 20 runs × 0.083 SOL cap that is 13.3 SOL/day wagered, 0.40 SOL/day gross, **$8/day to the dev**.
  - **LP variance swamps LP signal for the better part of a year, and the design does not disclose the ratio.** Per-run house EV = 0.03s; per-run payout σ ≈ 2.0s for a push-your-luck game with an 8x ceiling. At 300 runs/day, 0.30 SOL average: daily EV = 2.70 SOL, daily σ = √300 × 0.6 = 10.4 SOL — variance is 3.9x the edge. Weekly σ = 27.5 SOL = **6.9% of a 400 SOL bank**; a 2σ bad week is −36 SOL = **−9% net**. The pitch says LPs will 'visibly draw down 5-8% on a bad week' — that is not a bad week, that is a 1σ week, and it happens roughly one week in three. Runs needed for edge to clear 3σ of noise: n > 9σ²/μ² = 9(0.6)²/(0.009)² = **40,000 runs = 133 days at target volume, 250 days at 8 players.** LPs cannot distinguish your edge from luck for eight months.
  - **'0.5%/day' is 517% simple APR and that number selects for exactly the wrong capital.** Conservative capital reads 517% APR as a scam; mercenary capital deposits and exits on the first −9% week, which arrives within ~2-3 weeks with high probability. The 24h queued withdrawal at then-current price is the correct anti-front-run design but it makes the exit worse, not better, which accelerates the flight the moment it is tested. Bustabit's own operator logged an 800 BTC two-day swing at a 1% edge with far more volume than you will ever have — that is the honest comparable.
  - **The max-stake rule caps per-run exposure but not throughput, so it does not bound the drain rate at all.** ER cost is 0.0003 SOL/session + 0.0001/commit ≈ 0.0004 SOL per run. A bot is limited only by settlement, not by cost or attention. 'An exploit takes thousands of runs to matter' — thousands of runs is a few hours. See botExtraction.
- **Cold start:** Player-side genuinely works at N=1 — this is the design's real and underrated strength, and the review should not pretend otherwise. The cold start that kills it is the one the design correctly identifies and then under-costs: **the LP side**. A 50 SOL self-seeded bank at the bankroll/600 rule caps stakes at 0.083 SOL, so week one is 8 players betting 8 cents a run and the dev clearing $8/day. To raise the cap to a stake anyone finds interesting (0.5 SOL) you need a 300 SOL bank. You are asking strangers for 300 SOL, locked behind a 24h queue, in a non-transferable position PDA with no secondary market, on a solo anonymous dev's unaudited program, against an advertised return whose 1σ weekly swing is 7% and whose statistical significance arrives in eight months. That is a harder raise than the design's 'it is a Crypto Twitter pitch rather than a game-design problem' line implies, and the max-stake flywheel runs backwards in a drawdown: a bad week shrinks the bank, which lowers the stake cap, which lowers volume, which lowers the edge collected, which extends the drawdown. The flywheel is symmetric and nobody models the downside spoke.
- **Bot extraction:** **≈ 0 SOL, and that is a genuine, rare design win — but the bot still costs you 60% of your revenue without extracting a lamport.** A solver bot playing perfectly loses 3% of everything it wagers; there are no signup bonuses, no per-wallet drops, no rate-limited rewards, so Sybil buys literally nothing. Correct. The damage is indirect: the solver's existence drags population RTP from 92.4% toward 97%, cutting house gross from 7.6% to 3.0% and dev take-home at target volume from ~$137/day to $54/day. **Tail risk is where the real number lives.** If a table error or a modifier interaction yields a +10% line: stake cap on a 50 SOL bank is 0.083 SOL; per-run cost is ~0.0004 SOL; a bot can settle ~5,000 runs/day trivially (settlement, not ER latency, is the binding constraint). 5,000 × 0.083 = 415 SOL wagered → **41 SOL/day extracted = 82% of the bank in 24 hours**, detected by a dashboard a human checks in the morning. The bankroll/600 rule caps per-run exposure at 1.33% and does nothing about rate. Fix: a rolling-hour wagered-volume cap of ~2% of bankroll, enforced on-chain, plus an automatic circuit-breaker that halts opens when trailing-1000-run house P/L is below −4σ. Also note the 3-of-24 modifier interaction surface is where a +EV line will actually come from, and interactions are precisely what per-modifier testing cannot find.
- **Prior art:** This product exists, ships today, and prices better. **Bustabit (2014)** is the canonical implementation of everything in the moneyFlow section: a 1% house edge game with a publicly-funded bankroll that anyone can invest in pro-rata, share-price accounting, and published all-time-high watermarks — its operator publicly logged an 800 BTC two-day swing, which is the honest preview of your LP experience. **Just-Dice (2013)** did investor bankrolls first. **Stake Mines (99% RTP) and Dragon Tower (98% RTP)** are the identical push-your-luck / cash-out-or-continue mechanic with published RTP tables and provably-fair seed commitment; Dragon Tower even has the difficulty tiers your epoch modifiers imitate. On Solana specifically, **Moonbet** already ships originals at 99%+ RTP with fully on-chain house-edge constants and community house pools you can LP into, and **Zeebit**'s entire pitch is 'you can check the RNG.' **Hyperliquid HLP** is the DeFi-native version of the LP vault and demonstrates that this capital tolerates drawdown only when the edge is legible in weeks, not months. **Loot Survivor (Starknet)** is the closest on-chain roguelike-with-money precedent — Play2Die, fully on-chain state, ticket revenue routed to buybacks — and it monetised through tickets rather than RTP precisely because RTP-based extraction from a roguelike does not sustain; it did not retain. Nothing in DELVE's mechanic is new. What is new is only 'the vault is a Solana program instead of a Bustabit database,' and Moonbet already did that.
- **Suggested fix:** Three changes, in priority order. **(1) Delete the Private ER and the ephemeral rollup entirely.** Replace hidden information with the standard provably-fair construction: operator commits H(server_seed) at run open, player contributes client_seed, doors = HMAC(server_seed, client_seed || floor), server_seed revealed at settle, full transcript recomputable. Same trust guarantee, zero TEE dependency, no session keys, and it removes the dependency the design names as its own fatal risk. Run the 30-40 decisions client-side against the committed seed and settle in ONE L1 transaction: stake escrow + transcript hash + payout, ~5,000 lamports of base fee, one signature, done. Build drops from 16 weeks to ~8, and the ER becomes an optional latency nicety rather than a load-bearing piece. **(2) Cut the edge to 1.5% and make the vault the product.** You cannot win on price against Stake at 1% with a 3% game, and you cannot win on trust against Moonbet, which already publishes constants on Solana. What is genuinely underbuilt is the LP side priced honestly: publish the Sharpe, not the APR — 'edge 1.5%, weekly 1σ = ±7%, expected time-to-significance 8 months, here is the live equity curve' — and market it to the DeFi-vault audience, not to gamblers. That audience exists, it is bigger than your player audience, and nobody is serving it with an honest variance disclosure. **(3) Ship the solver yourself, on day one, as a first-class feature.** You lose the 4.6-point ignorance premium regardless; capture the goodwill instead. 'Optimal-play assistant, on by default, toggle off for hard mode' converts your biggest revenue leak into your differentiator versus every casino that hides its optimal line, and it makes the per-wallet RTP leaderboard a genuine execution-and-nerve contest rather than a tax on people who did not install an extension. Then re-underwrite: at 1.5% edge and 97% realised population RTP, target volume must be ~4x higher to clear the same revenue, which is the honest number you should be planning against before you write a line of Rust.

## NINE FRONTS
*Lens: house-and-oracle*

**Pitch.** Split 100 troops across nine battlefields against a house that plays whatever counters what everyone else played yesterday — and everyone else's plays are public.

**Core loop.** A campaign is five battles, about three minutes total. Battle 1: you allocate your 100 troops as nine integers summing to 100 (one tx, trivially inside the 1,232-byte limit) and commit hash(allocation, nonce) on-chain. Switchboard On-Demand VRF then draws the house's allocation, 1–2 slots later. You reveal. Each front is won by strictly more troops; ties go to the house, which is one of two edge sources. Troops on won fronts return at 1.10x; troops on lost fronts are gone. Your surviving army carries into battle 2, and you re-allocate whatever you have left across the same nine fronts. After five battles your final army T (capped at 400) pays stake × (T/100) × the epoch's calibration factor. Start 100, and a good campaign ends around 180–260. Stakes 0.02–1.0 SOL, hard-capped at bankroll/150. Not revealing forfeits the stake, and since revealing never costs more than the stake, revealing weakly dominates — no TEE needed, commit-reveal is sufficient and, as the brief notes, strong for two parties.

**Depth curve.** The house is not random and it is not fixed. Each epoch (24h) the program stores a public 9×101 histogram of every allocation players actually made, and the house's mixed strategy for the next epoch is: with probability λ=0.60 sample from a best-response distribution against that histogram, with probability 0.40 sample uniformly from the equilibrium support. So the house is a mirror of the crowd, aimed at the crowd. On day 1 you play something reasonable. On day 100 you are reading yesterday's heatmap, reasoning about who else read it, and deciding whether to play the counter, the counter-counter, or the thing nobody has touched in six days — a Keynesian beauty contest with a published scoreboard. It plays differently on day 100 because the opponent is literally different on day 100, and it is different *because of what players did*, which no amount of solving removes. The second depth axis is that this is Blotto wrapped around a multiplicative growth process: winning fronts compounds, so battle 3 is a Kelly problem — do you concentrate your shrunken army on four fronts for a near-certain 4/9 (a loss) or spread for a shot at 5/9? Army management across five correlated rounds is a genuinely separate skill from allocation itself.

**Skill vs chance.** Per campaign roughly 40/60 skill to variance; the house's draw is stochastic by construction, so a level-3 read still loses campaigns routinely. Over 200 campaigns skill dominates hard: I expect the top decile to run 100–108% RTP and the bottom decile 78–85%, against a population calibrated to 94%. That spread is wider than DELVE's because you are not fighting a fixed table, you are fighting other players' habits through a house that transmits them. This ratio is right because the game must be losable by good players in the short run — if reading the meta produced reliable per-campaign wins, the meta would be arbitraged flat in a week and the crowd would stop supplying the misallocations that pay the skilled.

**Why on-chain.** The histogram is the game, and the histogram has to be credibly unmanipulated — the entire strategic premise collapses if I can quietly reweight what the house saw. On-chain, the aggregation is a program that anyone can replay from tx history, and the house's draw is a VRF proof, so 'the house countered you' is verifiable rather than a claim. Also, self-calibrating payouts (below) require players to trust that I recomputed the ladder honestly from last epoch's real results; on-chain, that recomputation is a permissionless crank anyone can call. Otherwise: a web server could run Blotto fine. The instant global settlement of a 0.05 SOL stake with no account is real but secondary.

**Solana primitives.**
  - Commit-reveal on L1 — hash(allocation, nonce) then reveal; cheap, strong for 2-party, no TEE dependency, and forfeit-on-no-reveal is incentive-safe here because revealing weakly dominates
  - Switchboard On-Demand VRF for the house draw, one callback per battle, 1–2 slots of latency which is invisible inside a 30-second turn
  - No rollup at all — this game lives entirely on L1 at 400ms/350ms slots, because a turn is 30 seconds and a campaign is 5 txs plus 5 reveals
  - Per-campaign PDA (writable only by one player, so local fee markets keep priority fees near zero), closed at settle for rent refund
  - Epoch histogram stored as a single 9×101 u32 account = 3,636 bytes, well under the 10 MiB account cap, updated by a permissionless crank — and deliberately written once per epoch, not per campaign, so it never approaches the 12M CU/writable-account/block ceiling
  - Solana Attestation Service for a portable Colonel rating that survives outside the game
  - Jito bundles for the reveal+settle pair so a settle can never land without its reveal

**Money flow.** Stakes in, payouts out, no token, no LPs — the payout ceiling is 3.76x so house exposure per campaign is at most 2.76 × stake, which a modest dev-funded bankroll covers without outside capital. Target: 500 campaigns/day at 0.15 SOL = 75 SOL/day wagered, 6% edge = 4.5 SOL/day (~$360/day at SOL $80). I keep it; there is nothing else to pay. Bankroll math: with edge e=0.06 and per-campaign payout σ≈1.1, full-Kelly house bet is bankroll/20; I cap stakes at bankroll/150 (≈0.13 Kelly), giving risk of ruin exp(−2·0.06·150/1.21) ≈ 3×10⁻⁷. A 100 SOL bankroll therefore supports a 0.67 SOL max stake and cannot realistically be drained by one lucky player: their single best possible campaign takes 1.8% of it.

**House edge / rake.** 6% expected, but the mechanism is unusual and is the honest core of the pitch: the payout ladder is not a fixed table, it is recomputed by a permissionless crank at each epoch boundary so that *last epoch's realized aggregate RTP was 94%*. Concretely, the calibration factor multiplying (T/100) is set to 0.94 × (total staked last epoch) / (total un-calibrated payout owed last epoch). If the population gets better, the ladder tightens; if a wave of newcomers plays badly, the ladder loosens and skilled players get paid more. Players tolerate it because it is 6% and they can watch it being computed, and because the design says out loud what it is: skilled players are being paid out of unskilled players' losses, and the house takes 6% for running the market and guaranteeing settlement. That is poker's social contract with the rake made legible.

**Bots.** Bots are structurally fine here and I would not fight them. A bot that computes a best response to the public histogram becomes part of next epoch's histogram, which the house then counters — the arms race is self-correcting rather than extractive, and this is the one property that makes a public-information game bot-tolerant. Sybil buys nothing: there are no bonuses, no per-wallet limits worth splitting, and payout is linear in stake so splitting a 1 SOL stake into ten 0.1 SOL campaigns has identical EV. The real exposure is one very good modeler farming the weakest tenth of the population, exactly as in poker. Mitigations are social and structural, not technical: per-wallet lifetime RTP is public on-chain so newcomers can see they are in a shark tank, and the self-calibrating ladder mechanically returns a slice of shark profit to the population every time it loosens. If I ever need it, a per-epoch per-wallet stake cap of 25 SOL bounds any one account's extraction.

**Cold start plan.** Pure PvE — one player is a working game, which is the entire reason this design exists for a solo dev. The bootstrap problem is the histogram, not the players: epoch 1 has no crowd to mirror. I seed it with a synthetic histogram generated from a simulated population of level-0 (uniform-ish), level-1 (concentrate-on-five), and level-2 players, publish the generator script, and label the on-chain account `synthetic=true` so nobody thinks they're reading real people. With 8 real players in week one the histogram is thin and noisy, which is itself interesting — the meta is legibly readable when N=8, and 'I can see what all seven other people did yesterday' is a fun small-world state, not a broken one. I blend synthetic and real with a weight that decays to zero once an epoch clears 200 campaigns.

**First session.** Minute 0: link from CT to a web page. Minute 0–1: connect wallet; onramp widget if empty. Minute 1: they land on yesterday's heatmap first, not a stake prompt — nine columns, a public histogram of what everyone played, and the house's stated λ=0.60. That page is the hook and it is free to look at. Minute 2: a free campaign with play chips against the same house policy, five battles, three minutes. Minute 5: results screen shows front-by-front and 'the house put 22 on front 4 because 61% of you put 8 or less there.' Minute 6: stake 0.05 SOL, one tx commits, VRF resolves, they reveal — five commit/reveal pairs across three minutes, each landing in under a second on L1 through a Helius Sender / staked-connection path so nothing visibly drops. Minute 9: settle pays stake × (T/100) × calibration directly to their wallet and closes the campaign PDA, refunding rent. Total onchain cost to them: ~10 signatures, ~50,000 lamports.

**Viral mechanic.** The daily heatmap is the shareable object and it renders as pure CSS — a 9×101 public record of what an entire population of strangers believed yesterday. People will argue about it in quote-tweets, which is free distribution that requires no art and no influencer. Second: the 'the house countered you' result card, which reads as a small personal drama ('61% of you underweighted front 4') and is intrinsically screenshot-shaped. Third: a weekly published 'meta report' generated automatically from on-chain data — a solo dev can ship a daily content stream with zero content work because the players generate it.

**Four-week MVP.** Ship: 9 fronts, 100 troops, 5-battle campaign, 1.10x return on won fronts, ties to the house, T capped at 400, commit-reveal, Switchboard VRF, epoch histogram + permissionless calibration crank, synthetic epoch-1 histogram with published generator, free-play mode against the same policy (separate histogram so it cannot pollute the real one), CSS heatmap page, and a landing layer using staked connections because dropped reveals are the one failure mode that breaks trust. Cut: Solana Attestation Service ratings, USDC, mobile, per-wallet stake caps, any campaign modifiers, and the weekly meta report (generate it by hand for the first month).

**Biggest risk.** Meta convergence. If the population's allocations settle onto the Blotto equilibrium support and stop moving, the house's best-response term stops having anything to bite, every player runs the same ~94% RTP, and the game collapses into a coinflip with a 6% rake — a casino with a theme, and I would owe players that admission. I have one lever (tuning λ, which is public and would itself become a meta event) and one honest hedge (five-battle army compounding gives skill somewhere to live even at allocation equilibrium, because Kelly-style concentration decisions under a shrinking army are not solved by the histogram). Whether that is enough is the thing I would be watching in month two.

### Hostile review

- **Verdict:** needs-redesign
- **Solo-dev estimate:** 10 weeks
- **Degenerate strategy:** **Put all 100 troops on one front and ignore the other eight numbers.** Because a front is won by strictly more troops and won troops return 1.10x while lost troops vanish, your objective is to maximise the fraction of your army sitting on fronts you win — and the only way to hold w = 1.0 is to place everything where the house cannot beat you. Putting 100 on a single front means the house must also place its full 100 on that exact front to take it (ties go to the house). Splitting 50/50 across two fronts is strictly worse: the house need only clear 50, and if the house also plays 50/50 the probability it overlaps at least one of your fronts is 1 − C(7,2)/C(9,2) = 41.7%, giving an expected army multiplier of 0.583(1.1) + 0.417(0.55) = 0.87 versus 0.978-1.05 for the all-in. **The entire nine-dimensional Blotto simplex collapses to a 9-way matching-pennies guess.** The 9×101 histogram you built collapses to a 9-vector. Now price it: against a house that best-responds to the crowd 60% of the time and plays uniform-support 40%, a contrarian all-in on the least-popular front survives with probability ≈ 1 − 0.4/9 = 0.956, so per battle 0.956 × 1.10 = 1.051, and over five battles **1.051^5 = 1.284 = 128% raw RTP.** Meanwhile spread-players return ~5%. If the population is half all-inners and half spreaders, raw population RTP is ~0.67, so the calibration crank sets the factor to 0.94/0.67 = 1.40 — and the all-inner's realised RTP becomes **1.284 × 1.40 = 179%.** The calibration mechanism does not merely fail to prevent the degenerate line, it *amplifies* it by taxing spreaders to subsidise all-inners. The terminal state is that everyone all-ins, calibration falls to 0.94/1.284 = 0.73, and NINE FRONTS is a nine-sided coin flip with a 6% rake and a heatmap. That is the biggestRisk the design lists ('meta convergence'), arriving in week one rather than month two, and by a route the design did not anticipate.
- **Technical blockers:**
  - **Five VRF draws per campaign destroys the unit economics.** Switchboard randomness costs a callback transaction; even at the optimistic end (~0.0005 SOL all-in with priority, versus the ~0.002 SOL historically quoted for VRF requests) 5 draws/campaign at the target 500 campaigns/day = 2,500 randomness resolutions/day = **1.25 SOL/day at 0.0005, 5.0 SOL/day at 0.002**, against a stated gross of 4.5 SOL/day. At the high end oracle cost exceeds 100% of revenue; at the low end it is 28%. The player's own signature cost is correctly negligible (10 sigs × 5,000 lamports = 0.00005 SOL). Fix: ONE VRF per campaign, expand to five house allocations via a hash chain — house_alloc_k = H(vrf_output || k) mapped into the epoch's mixed strategy. Or drop per-campaign VRF entirely: derive the house draw from the epoch VRF XOR'd with the player's revealed nonce, which is unpredictable at commit time and costs zero oracle calls. That is a 10x cost reduction for one afternoon of work and it should be in the MVP, not discovered in month two.
  - **The VRF callback latency claim is optimistic under load.** '1-2 slots, invisible inside a 30-second turn' is true for one request. At 2,500 requests/day clustered into evening hours you are queueing behind the same oracle crank, and a 5-battle campaign has five serialised oracle round-trips — one stalled callback strands a player mid-campaign with escrowed funds and no timeout path described. You need an explicit expiry-and-refund instruction per battle or the first oracle hiccup produces stuck stakes, which is the exact failure that kills trust in a settlement-first pitch.
  - **The 9×101 u32 histogram is 3,636 bytes and cannot be reallocated in one instruction — fine — but the calibration crank is a single hot writable account touched at every epoch boundary, and it is also the account an attacker most wants to write.** 'Permissionless crank' plus 'calibration derived from last epoch's aggregates' means the crank must read every campaign settled in the epoch. Either you accumulate into the histogram account incrementally at each settle (making it a hot writable account written by every settle — at 500 settles/day trivial, but it re-couples every player's transaction to one lock and one local fee market) or the crank iterates historical accounts, which cannot be done in one 1.4M CU transaction past a few hundred campaigns. Neither path is specified. Pick incremental accumulation with per-epoch double-buffering and say so.
  - **No landing-layer budget is stated for the reveal path, which is the one tx that must never drop.** Five commit/reveal pairs in three minutes with forfeit-on-no-reveal means a dropped reveal costs the player their entire stake through no fault of their own. Staked connections / Helius Sender are named but the deadline design is not: you need a reveal window measured in minutes with an on-chain grace period and a client that retries with escalating priority, and you need forfeiture to require an explicit expiry crank rather than firing on the first missed slot.
- **Economic holes:**
  - **The payout arithmetic is internally impossible and the bankroll math is built on it.** Army evolves as T_{k+1} = 1.10 × (troops on won fronts) ≤ 1.10 × T_k. Starting at 100 over five battles the mathematical maximum is 100 × 1.1^5 = **161.05 troops**. The spec states 'T capped at 400', 'a good campaign ends around 180-260', and 'the payout ceiling is 3.76x so house exposure per campaign is at most 2.76 × stake.' All three are unreachable. Actual payout ceiling is 1.61x × calibration; actual max house exposure is 0.61 × stake before calibration. Everything downstream is therefore wrong: the Kelly calculation uses σ ≈ 1.1 for a distribution bounded in [0, 1.61] whose true σ is under 0.5, and the quoted risk of ruin of 3×10⁻⁷ is derived from an edge the game does not have (see degenerateStrategy).
  - **Break-even requires winning 90.9% of your troops every single battle, which makes spread allocation catastrophically losing.** You need 1.10 × w = 1, i.e. w > 0.909. An even 11-per-front spread against a comparable house wins roughly half your troops: w ≈ 0.5 → army × 0.55 per battle → **0.55^5 = 0.05, a 95% loss**. The 'nine integers summing to 100' strategy space the pitch is built on is not a strategy space at all — 99% of it returns single-digit percentages of stake. Only the extreme corners of the simplex are viable.
  - **The self-calibrating ladder is backward-looking and therefore trivially poisonable, and at low volume one wallet controls it.** calibration = 0.94 × (total staked last epoch) / (total uncalibrated payout owed last epoch). At 8 players and ~24 campaigns/day, one whale is >90% of both terms. Deliberately staking X and losing it in epoch N inflates epoch N+1's calibration by roughly (S+X)/S; stake X again at the inflated factor and recover more than X whenever the calibration lift exceeds your loss rate. The mechanism has no dampening, no cap, no multi-epoch smoothing, and no minimum-volume floor before recalibration fires. It is a one-account oracle.
  - **'Skilled players are paid out of unskilled players' losses' with a 6% rake is poker's contract, but poker has a rake CAP and a recreational-player-protection culture, and this has neither.** A stated top-decile RTP of 100-108% versus bottom-decile 78-85% means the bottom decile funds the top decile at a 20-30 point transfer rate on top of the 6% rake. Poker rooms that let that spread persist die; the ones that survive cap the rake and throttle the sharks. Your only stated lever is a 25 SOL per-epoch per-wallet cap you have explicitly cut from the MVP.
  - **Nothing in the design generates the misallocations that pay skilled players except newcomers, and newcomers can read the on-chain per-wallet RTP ledger you are proudly publishing.** The transparency that makes the game trustworthy is the same transparency that tells every fish it is a fish. In poker the fish does not have a public graph of its own losses. Here it does, by design, on the landing page.
- **Cold start:** PvE at N=1 works, and the synthetic-histogram bootstrap with a published generator and an on-chain `synthetic=true` flag is genuinely good practice — credit where due. But at 8 players the histogram is not merely 'thin and noisy,' it is **writable by any single participant**. Twenty-four campaigns a day means one wallet submitting 20 campaigns owns >80% of the distribution the house best-responds to. The attack is two-step and costs nothing: epoch N, all-in front 3 across 20 cheap campaigns to make front 3 the mode (these campaigns are themselves +EV, so the poisoning is free); epoch N+1, the house's 60% best-response mass sits on front 3, so all-in front 7 and collect at ~1.05 per battle with near-certainty. You have built an opponent that publicly announces its strategy one epoch in advance and lets its opponent write its inputs. Worse, the synthetic seed makes epoch 1 the single most exploitable epoch in the game's life: a simulated level-0/1/2 population playing spreads has raw RTP well under 1, so epoch 1's calibration factor is inflated from the first block, and the first all-in bot collects that premium before a single real datapoint exists. The 'small-world state where I can see what all seven other people did yesterday' is charming right up to the moment one of those seven is a script.
- **Bot extraction:** **The entire bankroll, inside 48 hours, and the operator's own risk model will report a 3×10⁻⁷ chance of that happening.** Concretely: 100 SOL bankroll, stake cap bankroll/150 = 0.67 SOL, campaigns are ~3 minutes and fully scriptable, and payout is linear in stake so Sybil is genuinely neutral (the design is right about that) — a single wallet suffices. Epoch 1 runs on the synthetic histogram, so calibration is inflated from block zero; a contrarian all-in bot collects ~1.284 raw × ~1.4-1.9 calibration = **180-240% RTP, i.e. +80 to +140% EV per campaign.** At 0.67 SOL and 200 campaigns/day (single-threaded, three-minute campaigns, 10 hours) that is 134 SOL staked → **+107 to +187 SOL/day against a 100 SOL bankroll.** The bank is gone before the first calibration crank fires, and it is gone to one wallet running a strategy that takes five minutes to derive from the published rules. Even under the most charitable assumption — calibration at exactly 1.0, no histogram poisoning, house best-responding perfectly — the all-in line still returns 128% and extracts +36 SOL/day. There is no bot-resistance story here; there is an arithmetic error being described as a bot-resistance story. Note also that the design's own defence ('bots become part of next epoch's histogram, so the arms race is self-correcting') fails precisely because the bot's move is *contrarian by construction*: it always plays where the histogram is thinnest, so feeding it into the histogram makes that front popular and the bot simply moves to the next-thinnest front. The feedback loop chases it, one epoch behind, forever.
- **Prior art:** Colonel Blotto is Borel 1921 and has a century of equilibrium literature (Roberson's continuous solution, the multiplayer and heterogeneous-battlefield extensions, discrete two-battlefield results). Searching turns up essentially **no significant on-chain wagering implementation of Blotto** — that gap is real and it is this concept's genuine asset; the reason to persist with NINE FRONTS is that the underlying mechanic is the least-copied of the three. But the *crowd-mirroring house* is very well-trodden and its failure modes are documented: it is Keynes's beauty contest wrapped in Challet-Zhang's minority game, and its money-game incarnation is the **lowest-unique-bid auction**, whose academic and commercial record is exactly the two failure modes here — bot dominance in thin populations, and equilibria that collapse to mixed strategies over a tiny support once players stop being naive. The nearest live consumer analogue is the **NFL survivor/contrarian pick pool**, where the entire skill content is 'pick the team the crowd is under-picking' and where public pick percentages made the meta converge within a couple of seasons. The self-calibrating payout ladder's honest ancestor is **parimutuel betting**, which achieves the same 'skilled paid from unskilled, house takes a fixed cut, house never carries inventory risk' property — but does it *within* a pool rather than across epochs, which is why parimutuel operators cannot be poisoned by last week's results and you can.
- **Suggested fix:** **Change the payout rule so that spreading is viable, then the rest of the design becomes what it claims to be.** The single arithmetic decision that kills this game is 'won troops return 1.10x, lost troops vanish,' which sets the break-even at holding 90.9% of your army and forces the corner solution. Replace it with a **front-value payout**: each of the nine fronts carries a published value v_i (heterogeneous, redrawn per epoch by the epoch VRF — this is the well-studied heterogeneous Blotto), and your payout is stake × Σ(v_i over fronts won) × calibration, with troops NOT compounding. Now allocation genuinely matters, winning six cheap fronts can beat winning one expensive one, and the 9×101 histogram is actually a 9×101 histogram rather than a 9-vector. Keep the five battles as five independent rounds against a re-drawn house rather than a multiplicative army process — multiplicative compounding at 1.1x per round is what forced the corner in the first place, and 'this is secretly a Kelly problem' is not worth the price of destroying the allocation space. **Second: recompute the bankroll model from the real payout distribution.** With a bounded payout in [0, ~2x] and a true σ near 0.5, the honest Kelly-fraction stake cap is roughly bankroll/60, and you should state risk of ruin against the *realised* edge, not the intended one — instrument the program to halt new campaigns when trailing-200-campaign house P/L breaches −4σ, because your first live week will be spent discovering that your edge is not 6%. **Third: replace the backward-looking calibration crank with a forward parimutuel.** Pool all stakes in an epoch, take 6% off the top, and distribute the remainder proportionally to each player's realised front-value score. The house then has literally zero inventory risk, the calibration is automatic and un-poisonable (you cannot inflate a pool you must also fund), skilled-paid-from-unskilled is enforced by construction rather than by a crank you must be trusted not to have mis-specified, and the entire risk-of-ruin section of the pitch disappears. **Fourth: one VRF per campaign, hash-chained**, or zero VRF using epoch-randomness XOR player nonce. **Fifth: do not launch the calibration or best-response house until an epoch clears 200 real campaigns** — below that, run a fixed published house strategy, because a crowd-mirror with a crowd of eight is a mirror one person is holding.

## SEAM
*Lens: house-and-oracle*

**Pitch.** A shared 12×12 map of mining tiles that visibly runs dry as people dig it — you pick where to put your money four times a day, and the crowded tiles pay worse than the ones everybody forgot about.

**Core loop.** An epoch is 6 hours; four per day. During the 5-hour commit window you submit hash(tile, amount, nonce) — sealed, so nobody sees where the money is going. During the 1-hour reveal window you reveal. Then one Switchboard VRF draw resolves the entire map. For each tile: base return = f(R_T) × dilution(S_T), where R_T is that tile's public richness (0.20–1.00) and S_T is the total revealed stake on it. A fresh tile at R=1.00 with light traffic pays ~1.12x; a mined-out tile at R=0.20 pays ~0.60x; a fresh tile that forty people piled onto pays ~0.88x because dilution is real. Your individual payout is your pro-rata share × a published jitter distribution (mean 1.0, range 0.30–3.00) so outcomes still feel like digging rather than like a yield curve. Then depletion applies: R_T −= k·min(S_T, cap), and every tile regenerates +0.04 per epoch toward 1.00. A check-in is 2–4 minutes. There is no latency race, no frontrunning, and no need for anyone else to be online at the same time as you.

**Depth curve.** Day 1 you dig the reddest tile on the map and get diluted by everyone else who did the same. Day 100 you are running a regeneration calendar — tile (7,3) was hammered on Tuesday, regens 0.04/epoch, crosses back into positive EV Thursday at 14:00 UTC, and the four people who also know that will be there, so you take (7,4) instead. This is a minority game over a resource landscape with public state and sealed moves, and its depth is entirely social-predictive: the optimum is always 'be where the crowd is not, but only where the crowd being absent is a mistake.' The second axis arrives with Claims: dig a tile 20 times and you mint a compressed NFT claim on it that pays you 2% of everyone else's yield on that tile for 30 days, then expires. Now there is a landlord layer — you want traffic on tiles you hold and you want to be early on tiles nobody has claimed — and it is deliberately expiring so it never becomes a permanent creditor class extracting from future players. Day 100 also has a physically different map than day 1, because the map is a record of everything everyone extracted.

**Skill vs chance.** Per dig, mostly variance — the jitter band is 0.30x to 3.00x and that is intentional, because a pure pro-rata yield calculation is a spreadsheet, not a game. Per week, mostly skill: congestion prediction and regen timing produce a durable RTP spread, and I would expect strong players around 103–110% against a population calibrated to 95%, with naive red-tile-chasers at 82–88%. The high per-dig variance is the right choice specifically because it is the only thing preventing the game from being solved into a boring, correct, unchanging allocation — with jitter, even a perfect congestion read loses individual epochs, so players keep experimenting, and experimentation is what keeps the crowd's distribution noisy enough to be readable.

**Why on-chain.** Honestly: a common-pool resource sim runs fine on a web server, and I should say so rather than pretend. What the chain adds that matters: the richness ledger is a public, replayable history, so 'the house is not quietly nerfing tiles to protect its bankroll' is checkable rather than promised — and in a game whose entire tension is depletion, a silently adjustable depletion constant would be the obvious cheat. Beyond that: compressed NFT claims at fractions of a cent make a 2,304-tile ownership layer economically possible where per-item mint costs would kill it, Token-2022 transfer hooks can enforce the claim's revenue share unrevocably when claims trade, and 4-times-daily sub-cent global payouts to a few hundred wallets is not a thing a small operator does with banks.

**Solana primitives.**
  - One PDA per tile (144 at launch, 2,304 at full map) — this is state sharding as a design principle, keeping every writable account far under the 12M CU/writable-account/block ceiling that a single global map account would hit immediately
  - Commit-reveal for sealed tile selection; forfeit-on-no-reveal is incentive-safe because the worst reveal outcome (0.60x) strictly beats forfeiting
  - Switchboard On-Demand VRF — exactly one draw per 6-hour epoch resolves the whole map, so VRF cost is ~4 callbacks/day total regardless of player count
  - State compression / Bubblegum v2 compressed NFTs for expiring 30-day tile Claims at collection scale
  - Token-2022 transfer hooks for enforced, unrevocable claim revenue-share if Claims become tradable
  - Local fee markets — each tile PDA is its own fee market, so even a stampede onto one tile taxes only that tile's commit txs, not the game
  - Per-epoch resolution crank is permissionless and idempotent per tile, so anyone can push settlement and the game does not depend on my server being up

**Money flow.** Stakes in, payouts out, in SOL (USDC later via 165-byte token accounts, ~0.00204 SOL rent). Target: 4 epochs/day × 30 SOL per epoch = 120 SOL/day staked. 5% take = 6 SOL/day, split on-chain as 2% to me (2.4 SOL/day ≈ $190/day at SOL $80), 2% to the Seam Fund, 1% to the Deep Strike jackpot PDA. The Seam Fund is the persistent-world budget: it pays for map expansion (new regions unlock when trailing-7-day volume crosses published thresholds) and for the regeneration subsidy that keeps early tiles positive-EV during growth. The jackpot PDA can never pay more than it holds — a 1-in-2,000 VRF event drains it to one digger — so it is structurally incapable of hurting the bankroll. Bankroll exposure is bounded by construction rather than by Kelly: worst-case epoch payout is 1.40 × total revealed stake (whole map fresh, no dilution), so the program caps epoch volume at bankroll/8, making the worst single-epoch loss 0.40 × bankroll/8 = 5% of bankroll. One lucky player cannot drain it because the jitter cap is 3.00x on a pro-rata share, so a single wallet's maximum epoch payout is 3.00 × its own stake × its dilution share, and stakes are capped at 0.5 SOL.

**House edge / rake.** 5% flat on staked volume, taken at resolution before payouts, split 2/2/1 as above and asserted in the program so the split is verifiable. Players tolerate it because 3 of the 5 points visibly come back to them — 2% funds map expansion and regeneration that raises everyone's EV, 1% accumulates into a jackpot that only players can win — so the actual net extraction is 2%. That is the lens's 'house edge funds a persistent world' made into arithmetic instead of a slogan, and it is the honest version: the world does not grow because I am generous, it grows because a program routes 2% of every dig into growing it and I cannot redirect that.

**Bots.** Bots will play and they will be better than humans at tracking 144 regeneration curves. Two structural facts make that survivable rather than fatal. First, this is a minority game: bots that share a congestion model crowd the same tiles and dilute each other into mediocrity, so the bot population is self-limiting in a way a fixed-payout farm is not. Second, Sybil is exactly neutral — payout is linear in stake within a tile, so splitting 1 SOL across ten wallets on the same tile pays identically to 1 SOL on one wallet, and splitting across tiles is diversification any single wallet can already do. The genuine risk is not extraction but flavor: if the top of the board is visibly all bots, humans leave. Mitigation is that the sealed commit window means bots cannot react to human moves within an epoch, only to last epoch's public history, which levels the reaction-speed advantage to zero — the entire latency edge that makes bots dominant elsewhere simply does not exist in a 5-hour sealed window.

**Cold start plan.** Congestion is the EV, so few players means high EV — the game is at its most generous when it is at its emptiest, which is the exact inverse of the usual solo-dev multiplayer death spiral. But I cannot let 8 players farm 2,304 fresh tiles at 1.12x on my bankroll, so map size is a published function of trailing volume: launch at 12×12 = 144 tiles, and a region unlocks only when 7-day volume crosses a threshold. With 8 players doing 0.1 SOL × 4 epochs/day, that is 3.2 SOL/day across 144 tiles — enough traffic that tiles visibly deplete and the strategic layer is legible from day one. It is also fully asynchronous: nobody needs to be online with anybody, ever, which removes the single most common solo-dev multiplayer failure. Week one is 8 people quietly mining a small map that is obviously running out, and that is a complete game.

**First session.** Minute 0: CT link opens the map — 144 hexes colored by richness, a live epoch countdown, and last epoch's payouts per tile. No wallet needed to look, and looking is the pitch. Minute 1: connect wallet, onramp if empty. Minute 2: the tutorial is one sentence on the page — 'rich tiles pay more, crowded tiles pay less, and nobody can see where you dug until reveal.' Minute 3: they pick tile (5,8) because it is the reddest, stake 0.05 SOL, one tx commits the hash. Minute 4: they close the tab; the epoch resolves in four hours whether they are there or not, and the reveal is a single tx (with an optional pre-signed auto-reveal so a forgotten reveal never costs them). Hours later: a browser/wallet notification — 'tile (5,8) drew 41 diggers, you got 0.043 SOL back at 0.86x — the tile you almost picked, (9,2), paid 1.31x.' That near-miss is the hook, and the next epoch starts in ten minutes.

**Viral mechanic.** The map is the content. Every epoch the resolution crank emits enough state to render a deterministic SVG of the world — depletion scars, regenerating frontier, newly unlocked regions — and posting that image four times a day is a zero-effort, zero-art-budget content engine that reads as a living world rather than as a game ad. Watching a shared thing get used up is intrinsically compelling in a way a leaderboard is not. Secondary: Claim holders posting their passive 2% takes, and the Deep Strike jackpot hit, which is a genuine one-in-two-thousand event with a VRF proof attached and is the single most screenshot-able thing in any of these three designs.

**Four-week MVP.** Ship: 12×12 map with per-tile PDAs, 6-hour epochs, commit-reveal, one VRF draw per epoch resolving all tiles, the richness/dilution/depletion/regen math with all constants public, the permissionless resolution crank, the SVG map renderer, and auto-reveal. Cut explicitly: Claims and the entire compressed-NFT layer (week 6+), the Deep Strike jackpot, the Seam Fund's automated map expansion (I expand manually and publish why), Token-2022 transfer hooks, USDC, and the notification system — week one gets an epoch countdown and nothing else. The commit-reveal + single-VRF-per-epoch architecture is the reason this fits in four weeks: there is no rollup, no TEE, and roughly one on-chain interaction per player per six hours.

**Biggest risk.** That it reads as a yield farm rather than a game, and attracts the exact population the record says kills these things — speculators who compute the EV, extract during the generous cold-start phase, and leave the moment the map is picked over. The design's honest defense is that there is no token to pump, no asset to sell ahead of launch, and the payouts come from other players' consumption rather than from new deposits, so the failure mode is 'gets boring' rather than 'ponzi decays.' The tuning risk is concrete and second: if map-size-versus-volume is mis-calibrated, either the bankroll subsidizes a subsidy nobody needed or the whole map goes flat-EV and the strategic layer disappears, and I only find out which after two weeks of live data.

- **Verdict:** needs-redesign
- **Solo-dev estimate:** 12 weeks
- **Degenerate strategy:** **Dig the highest-richness tile that nobody dug last epoch, at maximum stake, every epoch, forever — and because the map cannot deplete below the population threshold, that tile always exists.** The minority-game depth the pitch promises requires the crowd to be large enough that congestion is the binding constraint. With 144 tiles regenerating at +0.04/epoch and fewer than ~40 daily players, the set of fresh, uncrowded, +EV tiles never empties, so the optimal policy contains no prediction, no regeneration calendar, and no read of other players. It is a two-line script: sort tiles by R descending, filter to those with zero reveals in the last N epochs, stake the cap. Realised EV ≈ 0.95 × 1.12 = **+6.4% per epoch, risk-adjusted only by the jitter band**, which is symmetric noise. Above the density threshold the game does become a genuine minority game — but then a second degeneracy appears: because dilution is the only downward force and it is a published function, the equilibrium is players spreading to equalise f(R)/dilution across all tiles, at which point every tile pays identically, the map goes flat-EV, and the only remaining variance source is the jitter. The design names this ('the whole map goes flat-EV and the strategic layer disappears') and treats it as a tuning risk. It is not a tuning risk; it is the equilibrium of any published-formula congestion game with a large enough population. You get the arbitrage below the threshold and the flat map above it, and the window in between is narrow and moves as the population changes.
- **Technical blockers:**
  - **Pre-signed auto-reveal is not possible as described, and the entire first-session story depends on it.** A Solana transaction's recent blockhash expires after 151 slots ≈ 60 seconds at 400ms (≈53s at 350ms). You cannot pre-sign a reveal four hours ahead. The only fix is a durable nonce account (80 bytes, ~0.0015 SOL rent, and it serialises — one in-flight transaction per nonce account, so a player with multiple pending commits needs multiple nonce accounts). But the deeper problem is unfixable by nonce: **a pre-signed reveal transaction contains the plaintext tile and amount.** Whoever holds it to broadcast it later knows your commit before the window closes, which destroys the sealed-bid property that is the game's central mechanic. Auto-reveal is therefore either (a) self-custodied in a browser tab that must stay open — contradicting 'Minute 4: they close the tab' — or (b) custodial and leaky, handing a relayer the exact information the seal exists to hide. There is no third option with plain commit-reveal. This is a load-bearing feature listed in the four-week MVP that cannot be built as specified.
  - **The commit hashes the amount, so the program cannot size the escrow at commit time, and the spec never resolves this.** hash(tile, amount, nonce) means at commit the program knows nothing about how much is at stake. Two options and both break something. Escrow a declared maximum at commit: leaks an upper bound on every position and forces capital lockup. Escrow at reveal: **non-reveal becomes free**, which detonates the game (see botExtraction). The claim 'forfeit-on-no-reveal is incentive-safe because the worst reveal outcome (0.60x) strictly beats forfeiting' is only true if the stake was actually escrowed at commit. As written it is not.
  - **Late-reveal information asymmetry is real and unaddressed.** Reveals are public as they land during the 1-hour window. A player revealing in minute 59 sees most of S_T for every tile. They cannot change their tile — but they can decide whether to reveal at all (free if escrow is at reveal), and they can decide how much to reveal if amount is a revealed field rather than a hash-bound one. The design needs reveals to be either simultaneous (they cannot be) or economically irrelevant (they are not).
  - **The resolution crank cannot be atomic and the fee-routing accounts become hot.** Max 64 account locks per transaction and 1.4M CU per transaction. Resolving 144 tile PDAs plus per-digger position accounts requires pagination with per-tile idempotency — the design says 'idempotent per tile', good, but every payout also credits the rake PDA, the Seam Fund PDA, and the jackpot PDA. Those are three global writable accounts touched once per payout. At ~15k CU per credit, the 12M CU/writable-account/block ceiling caps you at ~800 payouts per block on the rake account, which is fine at launch but means fee routing must be accumulated per-tile and swept, not credited per-digger, before you are anywhere near the volume the economics require. Design it that way now; retrofitting a sweep after the fund PDA is live is a migration.
  - **Deterministic SVG rendering 'from state the crank emits' means an indexer, a renderer, and a poster — three pieces of infra the four-week MVP does not budget for.** It is the stated content engine and it is not in the ship list.
- **Economic holes:**
  - **The house is short a menu of formula-priced options and players only ever buy the cheap ones. This is not a tuning risk; it is an arbitrage, and it is structurally worse the fewer players you have.** Payouts are computed from a formula f(R_T) × dilution(S_T) against the house bankroll, not redistributed from a pool. A fresh, lightly-trafficked tile pays ~1.12x. Rake is 5%. So the net expected return on a fresh uncrowded tile is 0.95 × 1.12 = **1.064 — a 6.4% edge to the PLAYER.** A mined-out tile at 0.60x nets 0.57 and nobody will ever dig it. Regeneration at +0.04/epoch means the +EV menu is permanently restocked at 0.16 richness/day. Rational players take the +EV side every epoch, forever, and the house eats the difference. The design's 'worst-case epoch payout is 1.40 × total revealed stake' correctly identifies the tail but misses that the *expected* case is already negative whenever congestion falls short of the level the constants assume — and the house has no control over congestion whatsoever.
  - **Depletion — the entire visual and strategic hook — does not switch on until roughly 50x the player count the cold-start plan assumes.** Regen is +0.04 per epoch per tile. For a tile to stay visibly depleted, per-epoch depletion must exceed 0.04, which requires it to be dug. At 8 players there are ~8 digs per epoch across 144 tiles: **94% of the map is untouched and regenerating every epoch**, and any tile that is dug refills within a day. To make even 30% of the map visibly deplete you need ~43 digs/epoch = 172 digs/day ≈ **40-60 daily active players.** Below that, SEAM is not a common-pool resource game; it is a slot machine with a map-shaped skin that never changes. 'Week one is 8 people quietly mining a small map that is obviously running out' is arithmetically false — the map cannot run out at that volume, and 'congestion is the EV so few players means high EV' is a restatement of 'the strategic layer is off and the house is paying for it.'
  - **The revenue split is quoted on volume the design cannot generate and the net take is 2%.** 4 epochs × 30 SOL = 120 SOL/day staked at 5% = 6 SOL/day, of which the dev keeps 2% = 2.4 SOL/day ≈ $190/day. At the honest week-one figure — 8 players × 0.1 SOL × 4 epochs = 3.2 SOL/day — the dev's 2% is **0.064 SOL/day ≈ $5/day**, and the house is simultaneously paying out 6.4% of 3.2 SOL = −0.20 SOL/day. **Net week-one economics are negative for the operator: you lose about $11/day to run the game.** That is not a slow start, that is a bankroll with a half-life.
  - **Jitter variance dwarfs the rake at low player counts, so the house cannot even detect that it is losing.** Jitter is mean 1.0 over [0.30, 3.00]; call σ ≈ 0.6. With 8 diggers per epoch, epoch payout σ from jitter alone is 0.6/√8 = **21% of staked volume**, against a 5% rake. Epochs needed for the rake to clear 3σ: 9(0.21/0.05)² ≈ **160 epochs = 40 days** — and the underlying edge is negative anyway, so all 40 days of data will be telling you a true story you will read as noise.
  - **The 2/2/1 split is presented as 'net extraction is only 2%' but the Seam Fund and jackpot are liabilities, not rebates.** The Seam Fund pays for 'regeneration subsidy that keeps early tiles positive-EV during growth' — i.e. 2% of volume is explicitly earmarked to make the arbitrage above *larger*. You are funding the leak from the rake and describing it as a player benefit.
- **Cold start:** Fully asynchronous, no synchronous concurrency requirement, one VRF draw per epoch regardless of player count — architecturally this is the best cold-start posture of the three, and the 6-hour epoch means the whole thing survives on ~4 on-chain interactions per player per day. That part is right. But the *economic* cold start is inverted from what the design claims. 'The game is at its most generous when it is at its emptiest' is presented as an advantage; it is a description of the operator subsidising every early player at 6.4% of volume with no upside path, because the mechanism that is supposed to switch on and reclaim that edge — congestion — requires ~40-60 DAU that the plan explicitly does not expect. Meanwhile the experiential hook is also off: at 8 players nobody sees a map running dry, they see a static red grid and a payout that looks like a dice roll, so the SVG-of-a-dying-world content engine has nothing to render for weeks. And the epoch volume cap of bankroll/8 makes the empty state actively hostile: a 100 SOL bank caps the epoch at 12.5 SOL, which one motivated participant fills in the first minutes of a 5-hour commit window, locking out all eight humans. **At 8 concurrent players SEAM is a negative-margin lottery whose central mechanic is invisible and whose capacity is trivially monopolised.**
- **Bot extraction:** **Two lines, and the second one is the bankroll in a week.** *Line one, the honest arbitrage:* the per-stake cap is 0.5 SOL and the per-epoch volume cap is bankroll/8 = 12.5 SOL on a 100 SOL bank. Twenty-five wallets fill the entire epoch cap. Sybil is **not** neutral here — the design claims it is, but the binding constraint is the per-stake cap, not linearity, so multi-accounting is exactly what converts a 0.5 SOL position into a 12.5 SOL one. Extraction: 12.5 × (0.95 × 1.12 − 1) = 0.80 SOL/epoch × 4 epochs = **3.2 SOL/day = 22.4 SOL in week one, 22% of the bankroll**, while simultaneously crowding out all eight human players from every epoch. Note this also falsifies 'there is no latency race' — filling the epoch volume cap is a first-come race at the commit window open, and it is the only race that matters. *Line two, if escrow happens at reveal:* commits cost nothing, so commit 20 tiles from one wallet, watch 55 minutes of public reveals, and reveal only the tiles where S_T came in light. You convert the average outcome (~0.95 net) into the best-case outcome (~1.064 net) on every position you keep: roughly **+18% per epoch, bounded only by the volume cap = 2.25 SOL/epoch = 9 SOL/day = 63 SOL/week from a 100 SOL bank.** The design does not specify which escrow model it uses, which means it currently permits the second. *Additionally:* the Deep Strike jackpot at 1-in-2,000 with 25 wallets running 4 epochs/day gives the bot 100 draws/day, so it wins the jackpot roughly every 20 days and the jackpot is majority-funded by everyone else. Structurally incapable of hurting the bankroll, yes — structurally captured by the highest-volume participant, also yes.
- **Prior art:** **ORE on Solana (April 2024)** is the closest and most instructive precedent: pro-rata-diluted mining rewards on a shared resource, won the Solana Renaissance Hackathon, and then generated so much transaction spam that over 70% of non-vote transactions were being rejected network-wide; mining was halted on 16 April, fourteen days after launch, with the creator conceding the v1 algorithm was 'too gameable' and that miners had no reason to do anything but claim and dump. Every structural feature ORE got wrong — dilution as the only competitive lever, a population of scripts rather than players, an emission that only paid while new entrants arrived — is present in SEAM's skeleton, with the improvement that SEAM has no token to dump. **Sunflower Land (Polygon)** is the shared-depleting-resource-economy precedent: it accidentally congested Polygon in January 2022 and has since permanently banned 15,000+ farm NFTs, over 10% of supply, for botting — which is the honest base rate for 'bots will play and they will be better than humans at tracking 144 regeneration curves.' **Dark Forest** is the prior art for sealed moves on a shared map, and is worth studying specifically for how it handled the reveal-timing asymmetry SEAM has not addressed. And the correct formal ancestor is **parimutuel betting**, a 150-year-old mechanism that solves precisely the exposure hole SEAM reintroduces: the pool pays the pool, congestion sets the odds automatically, and the operator's take is a fixed cut with zero inventory risk. SEAM has rebuilt parimutuel with a formula in place of the pool, which is the one change that turns a risk-free operator into a short-options desk.
- **Suggested fix:** **Make it strictly parimutuel. This one change fixes the bankroll exposure, the cold start, the flat-EV endgame, and the low-density degeneracy simultaneously, and it is a smaller program than the one specified.** Concretely: total payout for an epoch = 0.95 × total revealed stake, no more and no less. Each digger's share = (their stake × f(R_T) × jitter) / Σ(all diggers' stake × f(R) × jitter). Richness becomes a *weight*, not a multiplier, so a fresh tile pays better only relative to what everyone else did — which is exactly the congestion tension the pitch wants, except now it is enforced by conservation rather than by hoping the constants were calibrated to a population you do not have. The house is never a counterparty, the epoch volume cap and the bankroll/8 rule can both be deleted, the operator's margin is exactly 5% at any player count from 1 to 100,000, and the low-density arbitrage is gone because with two diggers on the map the two of them are splitting each other's money. **Second: kill the commit-reveal.** With parimutuel weights the reason for sealing is that people would pile onto the best tile at the last second — but parimutuel racing has lived with exactly that for a century and it is the interesting part, not the bug: open positions, a visible running pool, odds that move as money arrives, and a hard close. Dropping the seal removes the auto-reveal blocker, the durable-nonce work, the escrow-timing ambiguity, the late-reveal asymmetry, and one full transaction per player per epoch. It also makes the map *live* — a pool you can watch fill is far better content than a grid that only updates four times a day. **Third: fix depletion to the population, not to a constant.** Make regen a function of the map's utilisation — e.g. regen per tile = 0.04 × (144 / active_tiles_last_24h) — so the world visibly depletes at 8 players and at 800. If you will not do that, shrink the launch map to 16 tiles, not 144: with 8 players and 32 digs/day, a 16-tile map genuinely runs dry and the hook is real on day one. **Fourth: escrow at commit or delete the amount from the hash.** Pick one before writing the program, because 'free non-reveal' is a bankroll-ending bug and it is currently underspecified rather than decided. **Fifth: hold the compressed-NFT Claims layer permanently, not until week six.** A tradable instrument paying 2% of other players' yield on a tile is a creditor class with a price, and the record on creditor classes formed before a game has retention is unambiguous — the 30-day expiry is a thoughtful mitigation but the right time to test it is after you know whether anyone plays this in month two.

## ASSAY
*Lens: market-native-sim*

**Pitch.** A market where the events being bet on are generated by a hidden formula instead of by the news — you profit by figuring out the formula faster than everyone else, and your bot is allowed to play.

**Core loop.** Six markets open every day at 00:00 UTC and resolve 24h later. Each market is a synthetic risk event with 7 public integer features — e.g. VOYAGE 4412: route_length 6, season 3, hull_grade B, cargo_density 71, crew_tenure 14, escort 0, port_congestion 22. A hidden generating model maps those features to a true loss probability p (typically 0.15-0.65), and Switchboard On-Demand VRF draws the outcome against p at resolution. Your 30-second loop: open the day's six markets, run your estimate (in your head, in a spreadsheet, or in your bot), compare it to the LMSR price — say the market says 0.41 and you think 0.29 — and buy the side you think is mispriced. Trade sizes 0.50-25 USDC. The LMSR with b=5 USDC means your own 10 USDC buy moves the price from 0.41 to roughly 0.33, so you cannot extract unbounded value from a single conviction; you are always trading against your own impact. Resolution posts the outcome and, crucially, appends the full feature vector and outcome to a permanent public dataset. You come back because every resolved market is a new training row for everyone, including your opponents.

**Depth curve.** Day 1 you are guessing from feature names and the crowd's price. By day 10 you have 60 resolved events and a bad logistic regression that beats nobody. By day 100 you have 1,800 rows and the interesting problems are the ones a fresh player cannot even see: (1) the generating model has interaction terms — route_length matters three times more when escort=0 — and finding interactions from 1,800 noisy binary outcomes is genuinely hard statistics; (2) the model DRIFTS: every 28-day season the coefficients are resampled from a published prior and the drift is partial, so 60% of your old model transfers and 40% is now wrong, and knowing which 40% to discard is the real skill; (3) the crowd itself is the second signal — the LMSR price aggregates every other modeller, so on markets where you and the crowd agree there is no money, and your P&L comes entirely from the markets where you are confidently opposed to consensus, which forces genuine calibration rather than accuracy; (4) inventory and bankroll: with b=5 the optimal bet size is a Kelly problem against a model whose own error you must estimate. This is the retention answer and it is strong — it is the same reason people play Metaculus and Kaggle for free for years. What is being mastered is empirical model-building under drift and adversarial pricing, and that has no ceiling.

**Skill vs chance.** About 85% skill / 15% variance over 500 markets, and a pure coin flip on any single one. That ratio is correct and I would not change it: the irreducible aleatoric noise is what makes the game solvable-but-not-solved. If true p were ever 0 or 1, a perfect model would extract the AMM's entire subsidy risklessly and the game would be a puzzle with a payday rather than a market. Because true p lives in 0.15-0.65, even a player with the exact generating model in hand is only right about 65% of the time and must still size bets, still lose weeks, and still be beaten by a lucky novice over 20 markets. Expected edge of a strong player: 3-6% of traded notional. Someone trading 120 USDC/day of notional at a 4.5% edge makes about 5.40 USDC/day gross, 4.20 after fees — small in absolute terms, which is why the design encourages larger bankrolls and why the leaderboard ranks by return-on-notional, not by dollars.

**Why on-chain.** This one earns it, on exactly one axis: verifiable resolution. The entire product is 'trust that the outcome was drawn fairly against a probability you were never shown'. On a web server that is an unfalsifiable promise from a house that profits when you lose. On-chain: the generating model's parameters are committed as a hash at season start and revealed at season end (so you can retroactively verify every market you traded was priced against the model the operator claimed), the outcome draw is a Switchboard On-Demand VRF proof anyone can check, and the LMSR's b parameter and fee are in program bytecode. That is Zeebit's pitch applied to a strategy game rather than a slot machine, and it is the one claim about blockchain gaming that is straightforwardly true. Secondary and weaker: sub-cent global settlement means a modeller in any country can trade 0.50 USDC positions, which a payment processor will not support.

**Solana primitives.**
  - One PDA per market — 6 new writable accounts/day, no shared hot state, so the 12M CU/writable-account/block ceiling is never approached and local fee markets keep priority fees at effectively zero
  - Switchboard On-Demand VRF for outcome resolution — 6 callbacks/day, ~1-2 slots latency, with the proof retained on-chain as the audit trail
  - Commit-hash of season model parameters posted on-chain at season open, revealed at season close — cheap (32 bytes) and makes the operator's honesty checkable after the fact rather than trusted in advance
  - LMSR implemented in fixed-point Rust inside a single instruction, well under the 200,000 CU default
  - Solana Attestation Service for optional verified modeller identity, so a bot author can build a portable public track record across seasons
  - Market PDAs closed 7 days after resolution, refunding rent (~6,960 lamports/byte) to the market creator
  - Blinks for one-click 'take this side at this price' shares on X (wallet-extension rendered, not native — a sharing primitive, not an acquisition channel)

**Money flow.** Value enters as USDC deposits into a per-player balance PDA. Value moves player-to-player through the LMSR and player-to-house through fees. The dev posts LMSR liquidity subsidy: with b=5 USDC the provable maximum loss per market is b*ln(2) = 3.47 USDC, so 6 markets/day caps worst-case subsidy at 20.80 USDC/day, and realised subsidy loss is typically 30-50% of the worst case because prices rarely go to the extremes. Fee: 2% of traded notional, charged on entry only. Arithmetic at 8 active players trading 90 USDC notional each per day = 720 USDC/day volume, fees 14.40 USDC/day, subsidy cost ~7 USDC/day, net ~+7 USDC/day. It roughly breaks even at 8 players — which is the point, the subsidy is a bounded, known customer-acquisition cost rather than an open-ended one. At 80 active players: 7,200 USDC/day volume, 144 USDC/day fees against the same fixed ~7 USDC/day subsidy (subsidy does not scale with players, only with market count), so ~$137/day or ~$4,100/month. This scales cleanly with players in a way Saltline does not, because the AMM's cost is capped by b and not by pool size. Raise b to 15 once daily volume exceeds 3,000 USDC to deepen the book.

**House edge / rake.** 2% of notional on entry, no exit fee, no spread beyond the LMSR's own curvature. On a market you hold to resolution that is a 2% drag against an edge of 3-6%, so a strong player nets positive and a coin-flipper bleeds 2% per trade — which is the correct incentive gradient. Players tolerate it because the operator is a market maker with published, provable parameters rather than a counterparty with a hidden book: they can compute the LMSR's implied spread themselves, verify the VRF, and check after the season that the model hash matches. The subsidy is also visible to them — the house is demonstrably taking the other side of thin markets at its own bounded risk, which is a fee people accept in a way they do not accept an opaque vig.

**Bots.** Bots are the intended player and this is the design's biggest differentiator: ASSAY is Battlecode or a Kaggle competition with continuous settlement. Day one ships a documented read API (feature vectors, full resolved history as CSV, current prices) and a reference bot in Python with a logistic-regression baseline. A bot's edge is model quality, not latency — markets are open 24 hours and resolve on a fixed schedule, so there is nothing to snipe. What stops the degenerate case: the LMSR with b=5 caps how much a perfect model can extract per market at a few dollars before price converges to its estimate, so even a solved model is a modest annuity, not a drain; and the seasonal parameter drift means a model that stops being retrained decays within a month. Multi-accounting gains nothing — there are no per-account bonuses, no airdrop, and running the same model across five wallets just splits your bankroll across five copies of the same impact curve. The only genuine attack is a group sharing a model and coordinating to move price before resolution, and since the LMSR is path-independent for a given final quantity, coordinating buys does not beat one large buy. The bot ecosystem is the marketing: a public model leaderboard scored by Brier and by return-on-notional is content CT actually shares.

**Cold start plan.** ASSAY works with one player because the LMSR is always the counterparty — there is no matchmaking, no lobby, no opponent required. That is the whole cold-start answer and it is why the AMM was chosen over an order book despite the CLOB being more capital-efficient at scale. With 8 players the markets are thin and prices are bad, which is GOOD for the first cohort: mispricing is where the money is, so early players earn more per unit of skill and have a genuine reason to arrive early that does not involve a token. Supports: a permanent free paper league on the identical markets scored by Brier score, so the modelling game is playable with no wallet — and if people will not play the forecasting game for free, there is no game and I want to learn that in week one. Plus a seeded historical dataset: before public launch the dev runs 400 markets against a scripted crowd and publishes the resolved CSV, so a day-one player is not starting from zero rows.

**First session.** Minute 0: land on a plain table — six rows, seven feature columns, a price, a BUY YES / BUY NO. No connect wall, no art, no animation. The visual identity is a Bloomberg terminal rendered in CSS, which costs nothing and reads as serious to the trading population that is actually the audience. Minute 1: a one-paragraph explainer and a link marked 'download 400 resolved markets (CSV, 38KB)'. Minute 2-5: you play the free paper league — same six markets, paper stake, Brier scored — and you place three forecasts. Minute 6: 'to trade for money, deposit USDC.' Wallet-standard connect or Solana Pay QR. Minimum deposit 5 USDC. On first deposit the program opens your balance PDA and covers the 165-byte token account rent (0.00204 SOL) plus 0.003 SOL of signature float from a faucet PDA, both recovered when you close your account. Minute 8: your first real position, 2 USDC on the NO side of VOYAGE 4412 at 0.41. Minute 9: you close the tab. Resolution is tomorrow at 00:00 UTC and the result arrives as a link you can bookmark. The daily rhythm is one visit, six decisions, five minutes — the lowest-friction retention shape of the three concepts.

**Viral mechanic.** The public model leaderboard, ranked by Brier score and by return-on-notional, with every player's full trade history permanently readable on-chain. That produces two shareable artifacts: a calibration curve (your predicted probability vs realised frequency, bucketed) which is a beautiful and self-flattering image that costs nothing to render in SVG, and a bot-vs-bot narrative — 'strain_theory has beaten the crowd on 61% of markets for 40 days' is exactly the content CT amplifies. Secondary: a Blink that posts a single market with its feature vector and current price to X, letting a reader take a side in one click if they have a wallet extension. Tertiary and probably the strongest: publishing the resolved dataset weekly makes ASSAY a free public benchmark, and machine-learning people write blog posts about benchmarks.

**Four-week MVP.** Ship: Anchor program with market creation, LMSR buy/sell in fixed-point, Switchboard On-Demand VRF resolution, a per-player USDC balance PDA, and a 2% fee. Six markets/day, 5 features (not 7), a FIXED generating model for season 1 with its hash committed on-chain (no drift yet — drift is a season-2 feature and shipping it early with a buggy prior would be worse than not shipping it). Client is one HTML page with a table and two buttons. Read API and reference bot on day one, non-negotiable, because the bot players are the launch audience. Pre-generate and publish 400 historical markets before launch. Explicitly cut: order book, drift, multi-outcome markets, portfolio margin, MagicBlock ER (nothing here is latency-sensitive — six markets a day does not need 10ms slots), Private ERs, mobile, charts, and any UI that is not a table.

**Biggest risk.** The events are fake. Polymarket and Kalshi let people trade on things they actually care about at $44.8B/month, and ASSAY asks them to model a synthetic voyage instead. The bet is that a durable minority — the Kaggle/Metaculus/quant-curious population, which overlaps heavily with the 3.2M daily Solana wallets — prefers a clean, stationary-ish, fully-observable modelling problem with a published dataset over real-world events that are dominated by news access and insider information. That is a real preference and it is why prediction-market bots exist at all, but it is a smaller audience and I cannot prove it converts. The second risk is subtler: if the generating model is too simple, a strong player solves it in three weeks, extracts the capped subsidy, and the crowd's prices become correct enough that nobody can earn. The defence is drift plus interaction terms plus the p in 0.15-0.65 band, but calibrating 'hard enough to be interesting, learnable enough to be fair' is a tuning problem I would expect to get wrong twice before getting it right.

### Hostile review

- **Verdict:** needs-redesign
- **Solo-dev estimate:** 7 weeks
- **Degenerate strategy:** FIT THE PUBLISHED CSV, THEN QUOTE THE TRUE PRICE AND STOP. Because the season-1 DGP is fixed and the operator hands out 400 labelled rows, the optimal line is: fit offline, then each day take each of the six markets from 0.50 to your model's p in one transaction, collect the bounded LMSR profit (2.35 USDC to move 0.50 to 0.80, ~1.0-1.5 in expectation), and do nothing else. Two consequences, both terminal. First, the prices are now correct from minute one of every market, so every other player's edge is exactly zero and there is no reason for a second participant to exist — the game becomes one bot farming a $6/day annuity from the operator's subsidy account. Second, the human product dies: the daily loop the design sells is 'compare your estimate to the market price,' and a solved market price is always right, so the honest answer to 'is this mispriced?' is permanently no. The design's defences — drift, interaction terms, p bounded in [0.15, 0.65] — are all CUT from the MVP. Drift is deferred to season 2, and drift is the only one of the three that actually matters. Shipping a fixed DGP plus a training set is shipping a puzzle with the answer key in the readme.
- **Technical blockers:**
  - Genuinely the cleanest of the three on infrastructure and I want to say so plainly: one PDA per market means 6 new writable accounts/day and zero shared hot state, so the 12M CU/writable-account/block ceiling is never within two orders of magnitude. A 200-byte market PDA is (200+128) x 6,960 = 0.00228 SOL = $0.18, and 6/day held 7 days is a rolling $7.67 float. An LMSR buy at ~30k CU means you could fit 400 trades per block against one market. Nothing here is latency-sensitive. The doc's decision to cut MagicBlock ER is correct.
  - FIXED-POINT ln/exp IS THE ONLY REAL ENGINEERING RISK AND IT IS UNDERPRICED IN THE TIMELINE. The LMSR cost function C(q) = b*ln(sum exp(q_i/b)) must be monotone, path-independent, and never allow a round-trip buy-then-sell to net the trader money through rounding. Getting exp/ln right in u64/i128 fixed point with guaranteed-conservative rounding at every boundary, plus an invariant test suite that fuzzes round-trips for rounding-arbitrage, is a full week on its own and it is the one place a subtle bug silently drains the subsidy account.
  - VRF COST IS COMPARABLE TO TOTAL REVENUE, WHICH IS AN ARITHMETIC PROBLEM DISGUISED AS AN INFRA PROBLEM. Six Switchboard On-Demand resolutions/day at ~0.0005 SOL all-in is ~0.003 SOL/day = $0.24/day at SOL $80. Against realistic fee revenue of $0.72/day (see economicHoles), the oracle alone eats a third of gross. This is fine in absolute terms and fatal in relative terms.
  - Read API + reference bot on day one is correctly called non-negotiable, but note it is a second deliverable with its own hosting, its own CSV pipeline, and its own docs — it is not free, and it is on the critical path because the bot authors ARE the launch audience.
- **Economic holes:**
  - b=5 CAPS THE ENTIRE BUSINESS AT UNDER $1/DAY, FOREVER, AT ANY PLAYER COUNT. This is the headline and it invalidates the moneyFlow section outright. With b=5, pushing one market from 0.50 to 0.85 costs 6.02 USDC. Across 6 markets that is 36.12 USDC of TOTAL daily volume capacity — for the entire player base combined, not per player. A 2% fee on that is $0.72/day. The doc claims '8 active players trading 90 USDC notional each = 720 USDC/day' and '80 players = 7,200 USDC/day': both are arithmetically impossible. Volume in an LMSR is bounded by b, not by the number of players. Even driving a market to 0.99 — a maximally aggressive trade — only absorbs 19.56 USDC. Revenue scales with b x markets and with nothing else, so the claimed jump from break-even at 8 players to $4,100/month at 80 players cannot happen.
  - THE OPERATOR IS STRUCTURALLY THE NET LOSER TO ITS OWN TARGET AUDIENCE, AND RAISING b DOES NOT FIX IT. Fee revenue scales linearly in b (2% of ~k*b of flow). Informed-trader profit — which IS the operator's LVR — also scales linearly in b, bounded by b*ln2 = 3.47 USDC per market at b=5. Break-even requires informed traders to capture less than ~9% of the AMM's maximum loss bound. That holds in a market of noise traders. ASSAY is explicitly recruiting Kaggle-grade modellers whose entire pitch is 'find the mispricing.' The design needs mispricing to be attractive and needs no mispricing to be solvent. That is not a parameter to tune, it is a contradiction in the mechanism.
  - THE PLAYER'S PRIZE IS TOO SMALL TO RETAIN THE EXACT PERSON THE DESIGN NEEDS. A trader with a PERFECT model extracts at most b*ln2 = 3.47 USDC per market, realistically 1.0-1.5, so 6-9 USDC/day across all six markets — and that is the total pot shared by every skilled player. The doc's own '5.40 USDC/day gross' for a strong player is therefore roughly the ENTIRE daily subsidy going to one person. You are asking someone capable of recovering a 15-parameter DGP from noisy binary outcomes to do it for six dollars a day. Numerai pays out over $1M/month to 30,000+ participants with 1,200+ staked models and STILL fights for modeller attention. Kaggle and Metaculus get that population for free — which cuts both ways, but it means the money is not the draw and $6/day is worse than no money because it anchors the value of the work.
  - THE 400-ROW BOOTSTRAP DATASET SOLVES SEASON 1 BEFORE THE FIRST MARKET OPENS. The MVP ships 5 features and a FIXED generating model with no drift. That is roughly 15 parameters (5 linear + 10 pairwise interactions). Binary-outcome estimation needs ~15-20 events per parameter for stable coefficients, i.e. 225-300 events. The design publishes 400 resolved markets pre-launch as a cold-start support. A competent modeller downloads the CSV on day zero, fits a gradient-boosted model, recovers p to within ~0.03, and season 1 is solved before anyone has traded. From that point every market prices at true p within a few dollars of flow, extraction goes to zero, and the fee revenue drops toward $0. The doc's biggestRisk anticipates a three-week solve; the real number is day one, and the cause is its own onboarding asset.
  - 'THE CROWD IS THE SECOND SIGNAL' — THE STATED SOURCE OF ALL P&L — DOES NOT EXIST AT LAUNCH. depthCurve item (3) says your P&L comes entirely from markets where you are confidently opposed to consensus. With b=5, one 5 USDC trade sets the price for everyone. At 8 players there is no consensus to be opposed to; there is one bot's opinion plus the AMM's 0.50 prior. The deepest retention layer is unavailable until you have dozens of independent modellers, which is the same cold-start problem the design claims to have solved.
- **Cold start:** Mechanically the best of the three: the LMSR is always the counterparty, so one player is enough, no matchmaking, no lobby, no synchronous concurrency. That is a real and undervalued advantage and it is the correct reason to choose an AMM over a CLOB at this scale. But it converts the cold-start problem into a subsidy problem rather than solving it. At 8 players you have $0.72/day of gross fees against ~$0.24/day of VRF and a $7/day expected subsidy bleed — you are paying roughly $6.50/day for the privilege of eight users, which is a fine CAC in isolation but it does not decrease and it does not convert into anything, because the same subsidy at 80 players is still $7/day and the revenue is still capped at $0.72/day by b. And the qualitative failure is worse: with b=5 the first bot to arrive sets every price permanently, so early players do NOT get the 'mispricing is where the money is' experience the doc promises them — they get a book that is already correct and a two-dollar maximum position size.
- **Bot extraction:** This is the one place where the answer is reassuring and damning at the same time. A well-capitalised operator cannot deploy capital here: the binding constraint is b, not bankroll. Absolute theoretical maximum extraction is b*ln2 x 6 markets = 20.8 USDC/day; realistic extraction with a perfect model is 6-9 USDC/day. So week one, a bot operator with a solved model and unlimited capital extracts $45-$145 total, and that is simultaneously the entire subsidy budget, the entire prize pool, and the entire reason for anyone else to be there. Nobody can drain you — and nobody can make a living, or a hobby income, or frankly a cup of coffee. The multi-accounting analysis in the doc is correct (no bonuses, no airdrop, splitting a bankroll across wallets just splits it across the same impact curve) and the coordination analysis is correct (LMSR is path-independent, so coordinated buys cannot beat one large buy). Those are both right and both irrelevant next to the fact that the maximum prize is smaller than the transaction costs of caring.
- **Prior art:** Numerai is the direct and dominant precedent: obfuscated features, a hidden target, stake-weighted payouts, over $43M paid cumulatively, 30,000+ participants and 1,200+ staked models in the meta-model weekly. It works because payouts are funded by a real hedge fund's alpha on real markets — the participants' models have exogenous economic value, so the prize pool is not a closed loop. ASSAY strips out the exogenous funding source and keeps the tournament, which is why the prize collapses to $6/day. The synthetic-event-betting side has an even more instructive precedent: RNG-driven virtual sports are 78.6% of the virtual sports betting market and a genuinely large consumer product — but they are universally house-banked fixed-margin products, deliberately run at a LOWER margin than slots because, as the operator literature puts it, 'players will immediately feel the high house edge, their bankrolls will evaporate, and they will churn.' Nobody has made synthetic events work as a peer-to-peer skill market, and the reason is that synthetic events have no informational asymmetry to arbitrage other than the one the operator manufactures. On Solana, Hedgehog Markets ($3.5M seed, $20M+ TVL, 2,500+ markets) and Monaco Protocol / BetDEX both went to real-world events precisely because that is where the $44.8B/month lives. On the AMM design itself, Othman & Sandholm's liquidity-sensitive market maker (CMU) is the standard answer to exactly the solvency problem below.
- **Suggested fix:** The shell is excellent — verifiable resolution is the one genuinely true claim in blockchain gaming, the committed-then-revealed model hash is the right pattern, cold start is genuinely solved, and the Bloomberg-terminal-in-CSS aesthetic is correctly matched to the audience. Keep all of it. Replace the economic engine. (1) STOP BEING THE COUNTERPARTY. Move to a parimutuel pool with a 3-4% rake, using a small LMSR of at most 2 USDC per market purely as a bootstrap quote that retires once two-sided flow exists; or, if you want to keep a cost-function MM, use a fee-augmented / liquidity-sensitive cost function (Othman-Sandholm) whose operator P&L is provably non-negative. Either way the operator earns the spread instead of paying LVR to the exact modellers it is recruiting. (2) RAISE CAPACITY 20-40x. b=150 or a parimutuel with no cap. A skilled player must be able to earn $50-100/day, not $6, or the target population will not open the tab twice. (3) SHIP DRIFT IN V1, NOT SEASON 2, and resample coefficients per-market from a committed prior rather than per-season. Drift is not a nice-to-have depth feature, it is the ONLY thing standing between you and a solved market, and every other defence was cut. (4) DO NOT PUBLISH 400 LABELLED ROWS. Publish 400 feature vectors with outcomes withheld, or 400 rows from a retired season's DGP. You are currently handing out the training set for the live season. (5) Seriously consider mixing in real exogenous data as features — the moment part of the DGP depends on something outside your program, the problem stops being solvable in a weekend, and the 'the events are fake' objection in your own biggestRisk softens at the same time.

## MYCELIA
*Lens: market-native-sim*

**Pitch.** You breed microbial strains whose genetics are secret even from you, sell breeding rights to strangers, and collect a cut of every descendant forever — enforced by the token itself, not by a promise.

**Core loop.** You hold 1-12 strains. Each strain has a 24-locus genotype stored inside a Private Ephemeral Rollup and visible to nobody, including you. What you see is its assay history. The 30-second loop: pick a strain, pick one of 4 substrates (each substrate weights the loci differently — Substrate C rewards loci 9-16 and punishes homozygosity), pay a 0.20 USDC assay fee, and Switchboard VRF resolves a yield in 0-100 with the strain's true expected yield under that substrate plus sigma=9 noise. The number posts publicly, permanently, attached to the strain. That is one data point about a genotype you own but cannot read. The second half of the loop is the market: you list a CROSS — the right for someone else to breed their strain against yours — priced by you, or you buy one. A cross consumes one fertility charge from each parent, runs a VRF meiosis (each offspring locus drawn from one parent, with a 3% mutation rate), and mints the offspring as a Token-2022 NFT whose transfer hook permanently routes 5% of every future sale and 5% of every future cross fee back up two generations of ancestry. You return because every assay narrows the posterior on your genotype and every assay by someone else narrows theirs — publicly.

**Depth curve.** Day 1 you buy a strain, assay it four times, and it averages 47. You have no idea whether that is good. Day 100 you are running a breeding program, and the specific things you have mastered do not exist on day 1: (1) SELECTION UNDER NOISE — with sigma=9 per assay, distinguishing a 54-strain from a 58-strain takes ~16 assays costing 3.20 USDC, so every breeding decision is an explicit value-of-information problem: is another assay worth more than the cross it would fund; (2) EPISTASIS DISCOVERY — yield is not additive, there are 32 hidden pairwise interaction terms per season, so two strains that each assay at 55 can produce offspring averaging 34 or 71 depending on whether their good loci interact, and the players who map interaction pairs from public assay history across thousands of strains have an edge nobody can copy without doing the same work; (3) INBREEDING MANAGEMENT — homozygosity carries an explicit yield penalty (-0.9 per homozygous locus beyond 12), so the naive strategy of breeding your best line into itself collapses in four generations, and the counter is maintaining two divergent lines and crossing them, which costs capital and patience; (4) THE INFORMATION MARKET — this is the deepest layer. Your genotype is secret. A rival who wants your genetics must buy a cross and infer your alleles from the offspring's assays, which takes them 6-10 crosses and hundreds of assays. So you are constantly deciding whether to keep your line dark (higher long-run edge, lower stud demand) or publicly reveal your genotype via an on-chain attestation (stud fees triple overnight, but every competitor can now target-breed against you). That decision has no correct answer and it changes as the season ages. Day 100 is unrecognisable from day 1.

**Skill vs chance.** Per cross: about 70% chance. Meiosis is a VRF draw and a bad cross is a bad cross. Over a 100-cross breeding program: about 75% skill. The high per-event variance is the correct design and is load-bearing rather than tolerated — it is the entire reason a market exists. If a cross's outcome were deterministic, every strain's value would be a computable function of its parents and there would be nothing to price, nothing to speculate on, and no reason to trade. Because outcomes are genuinely uncertain, breeding rights have a bid-ask, unproven young strains trade at a discount to proven old ones, and the skill being sold is estimating expected value under uncertainty — which is exactly the skill this design wants to reward. Expected edge of a strong player: a top-decile breeder should produce strains with mean yield roughly 1.4 standard deviations above the population by season end, translating to something like 60-75% of the Harvest pool concentrating in the top 10% of breeders, plus a stud-fee annuity that compounds because winning strains attract crosses that mint royalty-paying descendants.

**Why on-chain.** This is the strongest on-chain case of the three and it does not rest on 'true ownership'. Two things genuinely break off-chain. First, the lineage royalty: the whole economic proposition is 'sell breeding rights cheaply now, collect 5% of everything your line ever produces, forever'. On a web server that is a promise from a company that will change its terms or shut down, and no rational player prices a perpetual claim against a startup's goodwill. With a Token-2022 transfer hook the royalty is enforced at the token program level, unrevocably, by anyone's wallet, including on marketplaces the operator has never heard of — the operator cannot turn it off even if they want to. That converts a promise into a bearer instrument, which changes what people will pay for a cross. Second, credible supply commitment: the fear in every breeding economy is that the operator secretly mints an elite genotype for themselves or a partner. Here the season's epistasis matrix and every genotype-generating VRF proof are on-chain, so total strain supply and the absence of privileged genotypes are checkable. Third and weaker: composability — strains being Token-2022 NFTs means Magic Eden and Backpack index them for free, which is distribution a solo dev cannot otherwise buy.

**Solana primitives.**
  - Token-2022 transfer hooks — the load-bearing primitive; 5% lineage royalty on every strain sale and cross fee, enforced by the token program, unrevocable, split 3%/2% across two generations of ancestry
  - Token-2022 permanent delegate on strain mints, used solely to burn strains that hit 0 assay charges (the senescence sink) without requiring the holder to sign
  - Private Ephemeral Rollups (TEE-backed) — genotypes live as delegated accounts inside a PER so the 24-locus vector is genuinely hidden from the operator and from other players, with only assay outcomes committed to L1
  - Switchboard On-Demand VRF — one call per assay batch and one per cross; batched to 1 VRF callback per 5-minute window to keep callback cost bounded
  - State compression / concurrent merkle trees (Bubblegum v2) for assay records and lineage edges — a season generates hundreds of thousands of assay rows at fractions of a cent; SHARDED ACROSS 8 TREES because a single merkle tree is one writable account and would hit the 12M CU/writable-account/block ceiling under load
  - Solana Attestation Service for voluntary public genotype reveal — a signed, permanent attestation that this strain's genotype is X, which is what makes the reveal-vs-stay-dark decision binding rather than cheap talk
  - MagicBlock ER commit at 0.0001 SOL per commit for batching assay results to L1

**Money flow.** Value enters as USDC spent on assays (0.20 each) and cross fees (set by the strain owner, typically 0.50-8 USDC). Value exits through the weekly HARVEST: every Sunday, all accumulated fees minus the house cut are paid out to the top 40 strains in an open trial where each entrant pays 0.50 USDC to enter and is assayed 5 times on a VRF-selected substrate. This is the critical structural point — the payout pool is funded entirely by CONSUMPTION (assay fees, cross fees, trial entries), never by new deposits, so there is no ponzi decay curve. If activity halves, the Harvest halves; nothing breaks, nothing unwinds, nobody is left holding a claim on new entrants. The supply sink is equally hard: every strain has exactly 40 assay charges and 8 fertility charges and is burned when either hits zero, so the elite-strain population is continuously destroyed and must be continuously rebred. Arithmetic at 8 players: 8 x 25 assays/day x 0.20 = 40 USDC/day of assay flow plus maybe 15 USDC/day of cross fees. House takes 6% of assays and 4% of crosses = ~3 USDC/day. Weekly Harvest pool ~340 USDC across 40 strains. That is a thin but non-embarrassing early economy. At 300 players: ~1,700 USDC/day flow, ~$100/day house, weekly Harvest ~11,000 USDC. Plus the house's 2.5% of secondary sales, collected automatically by the same transfer hook that pays lineage royalties.

**House edge / rake.** 6% of every assay fee, 4% of every cross fee, 2.5% of every secondary strain sale (enforced by the transfer hook, uncollectible-around), and 5% of the Harvest pool. Blended take is roughly 5.5% of all value flowing through the system. Players tolerate it because 94% of what they spend comes back to players — the Harvest is visibly funded by the fees on the same screen where the fees are charged, and the number is public and updates live. The comparison a player actually makes is not 'is 5.5% cheap' but 'is the pool I am competing for larger than what I put in', and for a top-decile breeder it emphatically is. The lineage royalty is NOT house revenue and this must be loud: 5% goes to other players' ancestors, which is the mechanic that makes early strains valuable and gives the first cohort a reason to exist that is not a token.

**Bots.** Bots run breeding programs well and this is mostly fine, because the binding constraint is capital, not compute — every assay costs 0.20 USDC and every cross costs a fertility charge, so a bot cannot brute-force the search space, it can only allocate a budget more rationally than a human. That makes bots a strong-player archetype rather than an exploit, and it is why assay charges are capped at 40 per strain: a bot cannot grind information out of a single strain, it must buy or breed more strains, which costs real money and feeds the pool. Two genuine attack surfaces. First, statistical inference bots that mine the public assay corpus to reverse the epistasis matrix — I consider this legitimate expert play and would ship the corpus as a downloadable dataset rather than fight it, since the matrix resets every 28-day season and the map is only worth a season. Second, multi-accounting to farm Harvest placements: the Harvest pays the top 40 STRAINS, not the top 40 players, so one player fielding 12 elite strains already legitimately takes 12 slots and splitting them across wallets gains nothing — this is a deliberate structural choice, sybil resistance by making identity irrelevant to the payout rule. The remaining exploit is wash-trading crosses between your own wallets to build a fake stud reputation; the counter is that stud pricing is informed by public assay history, which wash trades cannot fake, only by paying real assay fees that go to the pool anyway.

**Cold start plan.** MYCELIA is playable by one person on day one. Assaying, breeding, and the Harvest all function with a single player — you are competing against the strain population and against the epistasis matrix, not against a matched opponent. That solves cold start completely and is the single biggest reason to prefer this concept over Saltline for a solo dev with 8 players. The market layer (crosses, strain sales, stud fees) is a bonus that scales in, not a prerequisite. Supports: (1) Genesis strains — the program mints 200 founder strains with VRF-drawn genotypes and sells them at a flat 3 USDC each, no auction, no allocation, first come; and critically the dev keeps none, which is checkable on-chain and is the anti-'creditor class' commitment. (2) A free sandbox season with a different epistasis matrix and no money, permanently open, so the breeding puzzle is playable with zero wallet — the honest test of whether this is a game or a slot machine with lineage. (3) The Harvest runs weekly regardless of participant count; with 4 players the pool is 60 USDC and 40 strains place, which means almost everyone gets paid something in week one. That is deliberate: the first Harvest should feel generous and nearly-universal, because the retention risk at low N is a payout screen that says 'you placed 41st of 43'.

**First session.** Minute 0: land on a page showing a grid of strain cards — each is four lines of monospace text and a procedurally generated colour blob rendered from the strain's public ID hash in CSS gradients. Zero art budget, and the aesthetic is legible: it looks like a lab inventory. Minute 1-4: free sandbox, no wallet. You are given 3 sandbox strains, run 8 assays, and watch a scatter of yields appear. The tutorial makes exactly one point: 'These two strains both average 51. One of them is actually a 58 with bad luck. Assays cost money. How sure do you need to be?' Minute 5: you run your first sandbox cross and see offspring that beat both parents, or don't. Minute 7: deposit prompt. Wallet-standard connect or Solana Pay QR; minimum useful buy-in is one Genesis strain at 3 USDC plus ~2 USDC of assay budget, so 5 USDC total. The program opens your strain's Token-2022 account (~0.00204 SOL rent) and airdrops 0.004 SOL of signature float from a faucet PDA; both are refunded when you close out. Minute 9: you own strain #0147, know nothing about it, and have 40 assay charges to spend finding out. Minute 10: you list a cross at 1 USDC — a pure lottery ticket for both sides at this stage — and close the tab. First Harvest is Sunday.

**Viral mechanic.** The royalty stream, and it is the most CT-native mechanic of the three. Every strain's page shows a live, permanent, on-chain figure: 'DESCENDANTS: 214. LIFETIME ROYALTIES: 41.80 USDC.' A player who bred a good founder strain in week one collects money from strangers indefinitely while doing nothing, the transfer hook guarantees it cannot be switched off, and 'my strain earned 41 USDC while I slept' is precisely the flex that the pump.fun-adjacent population shares without being asked. Second mechanic: the lineage tree, an auto-generated SVG showing a strain's ancestry with each node coloured by yield, which is a genuinely handsome image produced from data you already have. Third: the weekly Harvest results post, a single table of 40 strains with breeder handles and payouts, published every Sunday, which is a recurring content beat that costs nothing to produce.

**Four-week MVP.** Ship: Anchor program with 16 loci (not 24) and 16 epistasis pairs (not 32), 4 substrates, strains as Token-2022 NFTs with the transfer hook implemented and enforcing the 5% lineage royalty — the hook is IN v1 because it is the entire differentiator and shipping without it makes this a generic breeding game. Genotypes stored as encrypted account data with a commit-reveal fallback rather than a full Private ER integration, since PER integration is the highest-uncertainty item and the honest v1 hidden-information story is 'the operator could theoretically read it, here is the roadmap to PER'. VRF via Switchboard for assays and meiosis. Plain PDAs for assay records, capped at 5,000 strains. Weekly Harvest as a crank the dev runs Sundays. Client is a card grid plus a scatter plot, all CSS and inline SVG. Explicitly cut: state compression (5,000 strains do not need it), Private ERs, MagicBlock ER, secondary marketplace UI (list via a two-instruction escrow and let Magic Eden index the mints for free), Solana Attestation Service reveals, inbreeding depression if the epistasis math runs long, mobile, and any strain visual beyond a hash-derived gradient.

**Biggest risk.** Two, and they compound. The build risk: this is the most mechanically ambitious of the three concepts — Token-2022 transfer hooks are finicky, hidden genotypes want a PER the dev has never integrated, and the genetics simulation needs tuning that only shows up under real play. Four weeks is genuinely tight and a half-finished version of this is worse than a finished version of ASSAY. The economic risk: the Harvest pool is consumption-funded, which is the correct and honest structure, but it means week-one payouts are tiny — a 60 USDC pool split 40 ways is 1.50 USDC and that reads as a dead game to anyone who arrived expecting yield. The design deliberately refuses the obvious fix (seeding the pool with dev capital or a token, which is how every dead crypto game started) so the only remaining levers are patience and making the breeding puzzle fun enough that the Harvest is a scoreboard rather than a paycheque. If it is not fun at 1.50 USDC a week, it is not fun, and no amount of pool-seeding would have fixed it — it would only have delayed finding out.

### Hostile review

- **Verdict:** buildable-with-caveats
- **Solo-dev estimate:** 15 weeks
- **Degenerate strategy:** HARVEST SLOT-FARMING WITH DISPOSABLE STRAINS. Because trial SE (4.02) is comparable to the entire skill spread and the Harvest pays 40 slots by rank, the dominant line is to maximise ENTRIES rather than QUALITY: breed or buy the cheapest strains available, skip evaluation entirely (evaluation is the expensive part — 40 assays at 0.20 is 8 USDC and it consumes the strain's whole life), enter everything, and let the best-of-k order statistic do the work. 40 junk entries costs 20 USDC of entry fees and produces an expected top score of 58.7, beating a genuinely bred 58-yield strain, while also capturing a large share of the 40 available slots outright. This is exactly the failure mode the ancestral games hit: CryptoKitties collapsed on kitty oversupply within months (resale probability fell below 50% within a month of launch), and Zed Run died the same way. The design's supply sinks (40 assay charges, 8 fertility charges, burn-on-exhaustion) are the correct and unusually disciplined answer to oversupply — they are genuinely the best sink design of the three concepts — but the Harvest payout rule reintroduces the incentive to spam the exact supply the sinks are trying to destroy. One line: pay on excess-over-population-mean, not on rank.
- **Technical blockers:**
  - THE FLAGSHIP CLAIM IS FALSE: A TRANSFER HOOK CANNOT TAKE A PERCENTAGE OF A SALE PRICE. This is the load-bearing differentiator and it does not work as specified. A Token-2022 transfer hook is CPI'd during the transfer of THAT mint and receives only the transfer amount — for an NFT, the number 1. It has no visibility into the payment leg, which is a separate USDC transfer in a separate instruction routed by a marketplace the hook has never heard of. The hook cannot read the sale price, therefore it cannot compute 5% of it, therefore 'enforced at the token program level, unrevocably, on marketplaces the operator has never heard of' is not achievable. What a hook CAN do is FAIL a transfer unless it is routed through an allowlisted program — which is the OCP / MIP-1 allowlist model, and that model requires per-marketplace integration and has already lost on Solana: Magic Eden shipped Open Creator Protocol in Dec 2022 and moved to optional royalties; Tensor enforces only on MIP-1 collections and makes royalties optional otherwise. You would be re-fighting a war the ecosystem settled three years ago, as a solo dev, with a custom hook that no indexer has seen. The corollary also kills the free-distribution claim: Magic Eden and Tensor do not reliably index arbitrary Token-2022 mints with custom transfer hooks, so 'let Magic Eden index the mints for free' is not a plan you can rely on.
  - THE CROSS-FEE ROYALTY, HOWEVER, WORKS PERFECTLY AND NEEDS NO HOOK AT ALL. A cross is executed by YOUR program, so your program splits the USDC fee 3%/2% to the two ancestor generations natively. That is the half of the royalty story that is real, and it is the half that actually drives behaviour. Ship that and drop the hook. Note also that the 2-generation cap is technically necessary — an unbounded ancestry walk is impossible inside a transfer hook, whose ExtraAccountMetaList must be statically resolvable, and would blow CPI depth 4 and the 64-lock limit regardless. But 2 generations directly contradicts the pitch's 'collect a cut of every descendant forever': by generation 3 a founder earns nothing, which makes the 'DESCENDANTS: 214, LIFETIME ROYALTIES' hero screenshot unreachable.
  - PLAIN PDAs FOR ASSAY RECORDS COST $23,000 OF RENT. The MVP cuts state compression saying '5,000 strains do not need it.' It is not strains, it is ASSAYS. 5,000 strains x 40 charges = 200,000 records. At 80 bytes of data plus the 128-byte account overhead, each is (80+128) x 6,960 = 1,447,680 lamports = 0.00145 SOL = $0.116. Times 200,000 = 289 SOL = $23,163. Per player it is worse in the way that matters: 25 assays/day x 28 days = 700 records = $81 of rent against 140 USDC of assay fees paid — a 58% surcharge on the core game action, paid by whoever signs. Fix costs one afternoon: keep running (n, sum, sum_sq) per (strain, substrate) on the strain account — 4 substrates x 12 bytes = 48 bytes — and emit each individual assay as a CPI event for an off-chain indexer. Rent drops to ~zero and nothing is lost, because the public assay corpus you want to publish is an indexer artifact anyway.
  - VRF BATCHED EVERY 5 MINUTES COSTS 2-8x TOTAL HOUSE REVENUE AT LAUNCH. 288 batches/day at ~0.0005 SOL all-in is 0.144 SOL/day = $11.52/day at SOL $80, against a stated ~$3/day of house take at 8 players. Even the optimistic 0.0003 SOL gives $6.91/day. Coarsen to one VRF seed per day and derive each assay's draw from H(seed, strain, substrate, nonce) — the outcome only needs to be unpredictable at the moment the player commits the assay, not freshly oracle-sourced per assay. That drops oracle cost to ~$0.04/day. Hourly batching is the conservative middle at $0.96/day.
  - PER IS REAL BUT THE V1 FALLBACK IS NOT HIDDEN INFORMATION. MagicBlock's Private Ephemeral Rollups (Intel TDX) went live on mainnet in March 2026 with validators in Asia, EU, US and a TEE region, so the v2 story is credible. But the MVP substitutes 'encrypted account data with a commit-reveal fallback,' and the doc is honest that the operator could read it. That means the deepest layer of the depthCurve — the dark-vs-reveal information market — is not merely unavailable at 8 players, it is not cryptographically real in v1, in a game whose entire premise is that your own genotype is secret.
- **Economic holes:**
  - THE GENESIS SUPPLY IS SNIPEABLE FOR 600 USDC AND THE DESIGN'S OWN FEATURE MAKES IT PERMANENT. 200 founder strains at a flat 3 USDC, first come, no per-wallet cap, no auction. One bot buys 100% of generation zero for 600 USDC in the first minute. Because lineage royalties route 5% of every cross fee up two generations, that 600 USDC purchases a permanent tax on essentially all early breeding activity: at the doc's own 300-player projection (~1,700 USDC/day of flow, of which crosses and secondaries are ~680), 5% is 34 USDC/day = $12,410/year in perpetuity on a $600 one-time buy. This recreates exactly the 'selling assets before shipping — creates a creditor class paid in appreciation forever' failure mode, except via mechanism rather than presale, and the transfer hook is specifically engineered so it cannot be undone. The dev keeping none of the founders is a genuinely good commitment that becomes worthless the moment one sniper takes all 200.
  - THE ASSAY STATISTICS DO NOT SUPPORT THE STATED LOOP. The doc says distinguishing a 54 from a 58 takes ~16 assays for 3.20 USDC. With sigma=9 and delta=4, an 80%-power two-sample comparison needs n = 16*(9/4)^2 = 81 assays PER STRAIN. At n=16 the SE of the difference is 3.18 against a 4-point gap — a coin flip dressed as measurement. Even at the strain's FULL lifetime allowance of 40 charges, SE of the difference is 2.01 and you get ~84% power, meaning you only learn what a strain is at the exact moment you have consumed it and it burns. The information is destroyed by its own acquisition. Either sigma must come down to ~4, or assay charges must rise to ~120, or the meaningful yield spread must widen to ~10 points.
  - THE HARVEST PAYS FOR VOLUME, NOT QUALITY. Trials are 5 assays, so trial SE = 9/sqrt(5) = 4.02, which is roughly 40% of a plausible population sd. Slots pay by RANK. Entering k strains gives you a best-of-k order statistic: k=12 junk strains at true yield 50 produce a best trial score of 56.6 for 6 USDC of entry fees; k=24 gives 57.8 for 12 USDC; k=40 gives 58.7 for 20 USDC. A genuinely elite strain at true 58 scores 58.0. So 24 warm bodies at 12 USDC ties a strain that cost ~8 USDC of assays plus a monopoly stud fee plus weeks of breeding. The doc notices the mechanism ('one player fielding 12 elite strains legitimately takes 12 slots') and blesses it as sybil resistance without noticing it rewards quantity over quality. Fix: pay proportional to max(0, trial_mean − population_mean) rather than by rank, so noise-mining with 50s pays literally zero.
  - THE FERTILITY CAP MAKES THE ELITE GENOME UNPROPAGATABLE, WHICH IS EITHER THE BEST OR WORST THING IN THE DESIGN. 8 fertility charges means the frontier line goes extinct after 8 crosses. Real thoroughbred breeding — and Photo Finish LIVE, which allows 35 covers per stud per season — resolves this into extreme super-sire concentration. With 8 charges, the elite genome is a perishable monopoly asset whose stud fee gets bid to its full option value, meaning the breeder captures ~100% of the surplus and the cross buyer's EV is approximately zero. That is a defensible design if it is intentional, but it means the 'sell breeding rights cheaply now, collect forever' pitch is inverted: rights will not be cheap, they will be priced at exactly what they are worth.
  - WEEK-ONE HARVEST MATH IS A REBATE, NOT A COMPETITION. Eight players starting with 1-2 genesis strains each have 8-16 strains in existence; the Harvest pays the top 40. Every strain in the game places. Placement carries no information, the ranking is pure trial noise, and the payout is mathematically a refund of your own assay fees minus the ~5.5% blended take. The doc's stated week-one experience — 'the first Harvest should feel generous and nearly-universal' — is accurate and is exactly the problem: a leaderboard where everyone wins is not a scoreboard.
- **Cold start:** Genuinely solved on the core loop, and this is the strongest single reason to prefer MYCELIA for a solo dev with 8 players — assaying, breeding and the Harvest all function with one participant, no matchmaking, no synchronous concurrency, and the market layer scales in as a bonus rather than being a prerequisite. Credit that properly; it is a rarer property than it sounds. But be honest about what remains at N=8: crosses are rare, so the stud market, the stay-dark-vs-reveal decision, and the public-corpus epistasis mining — the three layers the depthCurve calls the deepest — do not exist. What is left is pay 0.20 USDC, see a noisy number, repeat, with a weekly Harvest that pays everyone. That is a spreadsheet-flavoured slot machine, and the free sandbox season is the right instrument to find out whether the breeding puzzle survives without the market on top. If the sandbox does not retain, no amount of market layer will save it, and the doc says as much.
- **Bot extraction:** Two distinct week-one attacks with very different magnitudes. The trivial one is the genesis snipe: 600 USDC takes 100% of generation zero and buys a perpetual 5% claim on the game's cross-fee flow — at 300 players that is $34/day forever, and the transfer hook is specifically designed so nobody can revoke it. That is the highest-ROI action available in any of the three concepts and it takes about ninety seconds. The second is Harvest farming: an operator running 40 disposable strains pays 20 USDC of entry fees and, on the current rank-based payout, plausibly takes 20-40% of the slots in a 340 USDC weekly pool, i.e. $70-140/week for $20 of entries plus cheap strain acquisition, with no modelling at all. Notably, the design is RIGHT that bots are otherwise fine here: the binding constraint is capital (0.20 USDC per assay, a fertility charge per cross), not compute, so a bot cannot brute-force the search space — it can only allocate a budget more rationally, which is legitimate expert play. Shipping the assay corpus as a public dataset rather than fighting the epistasis-mining bots is the correct call. The problem is the two rules that let capital convert directly into payout without passing through skill.
- **Prior art:** Zed Run is the near-exact precedent and it is not encouraging: hidden horse attributes revealed only through race results, player-set stud fees with bloodline-weighted minimums (0.075 ETH floor), hard breed-count caps per class to protect scarcity, six breeds and a bloodline hierarchy. It ran the same mechanism and died of breeding oversupply and thin racing demand. CryptoKitties ran it first — public sire auctions where the sire's owner collects the fee, breeding fee starting at 0.002 ETH — and by mid-2018 it was over; the transaction-network analysis literature dates the turn to within a month of launch, when resale probability fell below 50%. Photo Finish LIVE is the live Solana counterexample and it is genuinely encouraging for the stud-market half: players set their own fees, studs cover 35 times per season, one horse (Shockwave) reportedly cleared $35,000 in a single week of stud fees, and the game has paid $9.7M in track earnings. It also demonstrates the concentration outcome — one horse taking $35k/week is the super-sire dynamic, matching real thoroughbred breeding where the top 2026 stud fees cluster at $200-250k and stallion influence has concentrated sharply. Nobody has shipped this with an enforced perpetual lineage royalty, because as above, that primitive does not work the way the pitch needs it to.
- **Suggested fix:** Keep the economic engine — it is the only genuinely non-ponzi money flow of the three concepts and deserves saying so: payouts funded entirely by consumption (assays, cross fees, trial entries), hard supply sinks that burn strains on exhaustion, no token, no emissions, no claim on new entrants. If activity halves, the Harvest halves and nothing unwinds. That is correct and rare. Five changes. (1) DROP THE SALE-PRICE ROYALTY CLAIM ENTIRELY. Charge the 5% lineage split on CROSS FEES in your own program, where it works natively and where it actually drives behaviour. Use the transfer hook only as an optional allowlist if at all, and rewrite the pitch: 'collect a cut of every cross for two generations' is true, defensible, and still novel. 'Forever, enforced by the token' is not. (2) CAP GENESIS AT 2 PER WALLET over a 48-hour window with the remainder minted on a slow drip. A 600 USDC snipe of all of generation zero is a game-ending event that your own unrevocable-royalty feature makes permanent. (3) MOVE ASSAY RECORDS OFF-CHAIN: running (n, sum, sum_sq) per strain-substrate on the strain account, individual assays as CPI events. Saves $23,163 of rent and 58% of per-player cost. (4) COARSEN VRF TO ONE DAILY SEED with per-assay derivation; saves $11/day against $3/day of revenue. (5) CHANGE THE HARVEST TO PAY max(0, trial_mean − population_mean) rather than rank, and raise trial length from 5 assays to 12 (SE drops 4.02 to 2.60), so the payout tracks genetics rather than order statistics. Finally, on scope: 15 weeks is my honest estimate against a 4-8 week budget, and the largest single savings available is cutting the transfer hook — which fix (1) already does. Without the hook, and with assay records as events, this is a ~9-week build and the differentiator survives intact.

## SALTLINE
*Lens: market-native-sim*

**Pitch.** A closed trading economy where you run caravans between twelve towns, and the money you make depends on guessing what the other traders are hauling — because you can't see them coming.

**Core loop.** Ticks are 4 hours (6/day). In a 30-second visit you: (1) read the 3 towns you have eyes on — a price grid of 4 commodities, e.g. Vell buying Salt at 2.41 USDC/unit, down from 2.88 two ticks ago; (2) decide a single committed action per caravan — BUY n units, SELL n units, or MOVE along one edge (1-3 ticks of travel); (3) submit it as a hashed commitment (32 bytes) so nobody, including the crank operator, can see it before the tick resolves. At tick close the program reveals all orders, sums delivered quantity Q per town per commodity, and clears price with P = P_base * (1 - 0.0022*Q) floored at 0.35*P_base. Dumping 100 salt into one town moves its bid ~18% against you; two players dumping 100 each move it ~36% and both eat it. You come back next tick to see whether your read was right, and to see the public 'collision report' — total volume that hit each town, but never who did it. Caravan capacity 120 units, capital per player 10-500 USDC, 1-3 caravans. Season = 168 ticks (28 days).

**Depth curve.** Day 1 you are doing spatial arbitrage: buy at 1.90, sell at 2.60, three towns away. That is solvable by hand and it works — for about a week, until enough traders find the same lane and the lane's spread compresses to zero through their own delivery volume. From week two the actual game is second-order: not 'where is the spread' but 'where will the crowd NOT be'. This is an El Farol / Cournot problem with private information and a 1-3 tick delivery lag, so your order is committed before you can observe consequences. The mastered skills are (a) inferring rival positions from public aggregate volume — you see that 340 units of iron hit Doss last tick, you know roughly how many caravans were in range, so you can estimate how many are now empty and where they must reload; (b) managing your own market impact by splitting deliveries across ticks and towns, which costs travel time and therefore opportunity; (c) reading the closed money supply — town treasuries are finite and public, so a town that has been farmed for 40 ticks has a drained treasury and bids low regardless of its posted demand, and the good players rotate ahead of that drain. Day 100 you are not price-hunting, you are modelling a population of opponents. That said: the honest risk is that with a stable player pool the metagame converges. The counter is seasonal reset of the map's base-price parameters and shock schedule every 28 days, and mixed-size seasons (6-player and 24-player pools clear very differently).

**Skill vs chance.** Roughly 30% variance / 70% skill over a full 168-tick season, and near 50/50 on any single delivery. VRF shocks (5% per town per tick: famine +60% base price, blockade -45%, road washout +1 tick travel) mean a well-reasoned route can be destroyed by one draw. That ratio is right because the shocks are what keep the map from being a solved graph problem — a deterministic map with hidden orders would converge to a mixed-strategy equilibrium a bot could compute; a map that keeps moving forces continuous re-estimation from noisy signals, which is the actual skill being sold. Expected edge of a strong player: about +25 to +40% on capital per season against a field averaging 0% gross, -3% net of rake. Bottom-quartile players lose 30-50%. This is explicitly a negative-sum game for the average participant and the UI says so on the deposit screen.

**Why on-chain.** Partly honest, partly not. The simulation itself does not need a chain — a Postgres server could run these twelve towns fine. What needs the chain: (1) real money escrowed and settled instantly and globally with no bank and no withdrawal desk, which is the whole premise; (2) commit-reveal that is verifiable — in a Web2 version the operator can see your sealed order and trade against it, and there is no way for you to prove they didn't, which is fatal in a game whose entire mechanic is hidden simultaneous commitment; (3) the closed money supply is auditable — anyone can sum every town treasury plus every player balance and confirm the operator is not printing. That third one matters because the failure mode of every trading sim is 'the house quietly adds liquidity'. I would not claim more than that.

**Solana primitives.**
  - MagicBlock Ephemeral Rollups (sdk 0.16.2) — town PDAs and caravan PDAs delegated to one ER per season; ~10ms slots make tick resolution a single cheap batch; 0.0003 SOL per session + 0.0001 SOL per commit, so a 168-tick season commits ~6x/day for well under 0.1 SOL total
  - Private Ephemeral Rollups (TEE-backed) in v2 for continuous hidden caravan positions rather than per-tick commit-reveal
  - On-chain commit-reveal (32-byte hash then reveal) for v1 sealed orders — cheap, strong, and needs no VRF or TEE
  - Switchboard On-Demand VRF — ONE call per tick that seeds all 12 towns' shock draws, not one call per event, to keep callback cost at ~6 txs/day
  - Per-season sharded PDAs: each season has its own town accounts, so two concurrent seasons never contend for the 12M CU/writable-account/block ceiling and local fee markets keep priority fees near zero
  - Session PDAs closed at season end, refunding ~6,960 lamports/byte of rent to the player

**Money flow.** Value enters only as player deposits in USDC (min 10, max 500). At season genesis the program splits the pooled deposits: each player's stake stays in their caravan account, and the 12 town treasuries are seeded from a 15% protocol float that the dev posts once and recovers at season end. Buying goods moves your USDC into a town treasury; selling moves treasury USDC to you. Total USDC in the world is therefore constant — no faucet, no emissions, no token. Value exits as withdrawals at season end. The dev earns: 1.5% of every deposit at entry, plus 5% of net profit at withdrawal (losers pay nothing on the way out). Arithmetic at 24 players x 120 USDC average = 2,880 USDC pool. Entry rake 43 USDC. If the top 8 players end +45% on average, profit pool ~1,000 USDC, withdrawal rake 50 USDC. Total ~93 USDC per 28-day season per 24-player pool. Four concurrent pools = ~$370/month. This is a $400-1,500/month business at realistic solo-dev scale and I am not going to pretend otherwise; it becomes interesting only at 300+ concurrent traders or with the v2 spectator layer (a parimutuel on which trader finishes top-3, rake 4%, which historically does 5-10x the notional of the underlying game).

**House edge / rake.** 1.5% on deposit + 5% on net profit at withdrawal. Effective take is ~3% of pool per season. Players tolerate it because it is charged against other players' money, not against a house that is also the counterparty — the operator never takes a position, never sets a price, and cannot see an order before it clears. The forward-contract auction in v2 adds 1% of contract notional. No fee on individual trades: a per-trade fee would tax exactly the behaviour (splitting deliveries to manage impact) that the design wants to encourage.

**Bots.** Bots show up in week two and they are fine here. The tick structure is the defence: one committed order per caravan per 4-hour tick means latency is worth literally zero — there is no mempool race, no front-run, no sniping, because all orders are hashed and revealed simultaneously. A bot's only edge is better inference about rival positions from public aggregate volume, which is the same edge a good human has, so a bot is a legitimate strong player rather than an exploit. Multi-accounting is weakly negative: capital is capped at 500 USDC per caravan account but market impact is superlinear in your own volume, so splitting 500 across five accounts genuinely does help you hide — and the counter is that it also splits your information (you only see towns where you have a caravan) and multiplies your rent and travel-time inefficiency. I would ship a public read API and a reference bot on day one and let the bot players be the loud early adopters. What does get banned mechanically, not by policy: collusion rings coordinating deliveries to avoid mutual impact. The counter is that the collision report publishes aggregate volume per town per tick, so a ring's suspiciously smooth impact profile is visible to everyone, and any non-colluder can profitably attack a lane they know is being kept thin.

**Cold start plan.** Saltline works at six players. Twelve towns, four commodities, and six traders with 120 USDC each is already a market where your impact is felt — in fact impact is MORE brutal at small N, which makes the core tension sharper, not weaker, at launch. Three supports: (1) The Guild — 4 NPC caravans running a fully published, deterministic rule set (buy the lowest-priced commodity within 2 edges, sell where the bid exceeds cost by 22%). Their capital comes out of the town treasuries, not from the dev, so they move goods without printing money, and because their algorithm is public they are predictable liquidity rather than a hidden house edge. Beating the Guild is the day-one skill ladder. (2) A permanently running free paper season with the identical map and identical Guild, so the game is playable with zero wallet and zero dollars — if it isn't fun there, it is a casino with a map and I would rather find that out in week one. (3) Small pools by default: seasons cap at 24 and a new season opens whenever the current one fills or every 28 days, whichever first. A 6-player season is a real season, not a lobby waiting to fill.

**First session.** Minute 0: click a link on CT, land on a text-and-CSS price grid — 12 town names, 4 commodities, a colour-coded price table. No connect wall. A burner keypair is generated in localStorage and you are dropped into the free paper season immediately with 100 paper USDC and one caravan. Minute 1-3: a two-panel tutorial ('you are in Vell. Salt is 1.88 here and 2.61 in Doss, two ticks away. But 3 other caravans are within 2 edges of Doss and you cannot see them.') You submit a buy. Minute 4: the paper season runs at accelerated 60-second ticks for your first 6 ticks, so you see a full buy-move-sell cycle plus one collision inside four minutes. Minute 8: you have won or lost paper money and seen the collision report. Now the deposit prompt: 'Real seasons start Mondays. Minimum 10 USDC.' Wallet connect via wallet-standard (Phantom/Backpack/Solflare) or Solana Pay QR from a phone wallet. You transfer USDC; the program opens your caravan PDA and airdrops 0.004 SOL from a faucet PDA to cover your signature fees for the season (~$0.32 at SOL $80, recovered when the session PDA closes and refunds rent). No swaps, no bridge, no gas token to think about. Minute 10: you queue your first real order and close the tab — the next tick is 4 hours away. That gap is the design, not a flaw: Saltline is an asynchronous strategy game you visit six times a day for 30 seconds, which is the only session shape a solo dev can support with 8 players spread across timezones.

**Viral mechanic.** The collision report. After every tick the program emits, per town, total delivered volume and the resulting price move, and the client renders it as a shareable text card: 'VELL, tick 88 — 340 salt delivered by 4 caravans. Bid fell 2.88 → 1.71. Combined realised loss: 214 USDC. None of them knew about the others.' Schadenfreude is the most reliably shared content on CT and this generates it automatically, six times a day, with real dollar figures attached and no named victims. Secondary: end-of-season lane maps, an auto-generated 12-node graph coloured by where each surviving trader made their money, which reads as a personality test.

**Four-week MVP.** Ship: Anchor program with 8 towns (not 12), 2 commodities (Salt, Iron), 1 caravan per player, commit-reveal orders, deterministic price impact, VRF shocks via one Switchboard On-Demand call per tick, USDC escrow, a crank the dev runs on a 4-hour cron, and 4 Guild NPCs. Client is a single HTML page: a table, a form, and a log. Deploy on L1 first — 6 ticks/day across 8 town accounts is nowhere near the 12M CU/writable-account ceiling and does not need a rollup. Run the free paper season for two weeks, then open one 24-cap money season. Explicitly cut: MagicBlock ER (only needed when ticks go sub-minute), Private ERs, forward contracts, the spectator parimutuel, multiple caravans, the 3rd and 4th commodity, any map beyond the one hand-authored graph, mobile layout, and every leaderboard except a single sortable table.

**Biggest risk.** The economy is closed and negative-sum after rake, so a small number of sharp players will farm the field, and unlike poker there is no rake-free social layer to keep losers around. If the bottom half of a 24-player season loses 40% of stake in 28 days, they do not come back, and by season four you have six sharks and no fish. The mitigations are real but unproven: capped stakes (500 USDC max) compress the shark's absolute winnings and therefore their motivation to grind; the free paper season stays permanently populated so the skill gap is learnable without paying tuition; and seasonal parameter resets partially reset the incumbency advantage. But the honest failure mode is that Saltline has a churn problem structurally identical to a poker room's, and poker rooms solve it with marketing budgets a solo dev does not have.

### Hostile review

- **Verdict:** needs-redesign
- **Solo-dev estimate:** 11 weeks
- **Degenerate strategy:** SHOCK-CAMPING, AND IT ENDS IN A SILENT MAP. There is no carry cost, no spoilage, no interest, no caravan upkeep — idling is free. The design itself concedes that normal spatial spreads compress to zero by week two through delivery volume. That leaves famine shocks (+60% base price, 5%/town/tick) as the only reliably positive-EV trade. So the dominant line is: hold one full caravan of the cheapest commodity parked at a high-degree node, refuse every ordinary trade, and wait for a famine within one edge. This is a pure El Farol / minority game, and the econophysics literature is unambiguous that learning agents converge to the mixed equilibrium fast — well inside a 168-tick season. The equilibrium is degenerate: every famine attracts ~5 camped caravans, 5 x 50 units = 250 units gives 0.0022 x 250 = 55% impact, which more than erases the +60% shock. No positive-EV trade exists anywhere on the map, so every caravan parks and the game goes quiet with 24 players staring at a price grid nobody is willing to touch. The fix is one line of code: a per-tick carry cost of ~0.25% of inventory value, which makes idling strictly -EV and forces circulation.
- **Technical blockers:**
  - SELECTIVE NON-REVEAL IS AN UNBONDED FREE OPTION. Commit-reveal on a public chain means reveals land as visible transactions during the reveal window. A bot watches rivals' reveals land and simply declines to reveal its own when it sees a collision forming. Modelling per-delivery P&L as N(+2, 20) USDC on a 120 USDC stake: P(bad)=0.46, E[X|X<0]=-15.3, so the abort option is worth 7.0 USDC per delivery. Over ~10 deliveries in week one that is +70 USDC on a 120 USDC stake (+58%) with zero market skill; over a 42-delivery season it is +295 USDC, or 2.5x the entire bankroll. There is no forfeiture bond anywhere in the spec. This is not a nitpick, it is the single largest edge in the game and it is available to anyone who can read a WebSocket. Fixes: (a) bond the commitment at >= max downside, forfeited to the town on non-reveal with a default HOLD executed; (b) move orders into the MagicBlock PER in v1 — the doc lists PER as a v2 nicety when it is actually the v1 requirement.
  - ACCOUNT LOCKS FORCE A MULTI-TX CRANK AND ALTs ARE MANDATORY, NOT OPTIONAL. 24 players x 3 caravans + 12 town PDAs = 84 account locks against the 64-lock ceiling — 2 txs minimum. Raw keys alone are 84 x 32 = 2,688 bytes against a 1,232-byte tx (SIMD-0296's 4,096 is still PENDING, do not plan on it). Address Lookup Tables are required and the ALT itself must be created/extended and warmed one slot before use. The crank must therefore be two-phase and order-independent: phase 1 accumulates Q into town accounts, phase 2 settles caravans against the cleared price. That is correct and achievable, but it is a distributed-systems problem with partial-failure semantics (what happens if phase 2 tx 3 of 5 fails to land?), not the cron job the fourWeekMvp describes. Budget the landing layer: 6 ticks/day x 28 days x 5 txs = 840 crank txs per season that MUST all land, and a single dropped settlement tx corrupts a paying season.
  - CU and per-writable-account throughput are genuinely fine and I will not pretend otherwise: 12 town accounts each taking ~300k CU per tick sits at 2.5% of the 12M CU/writable-account/block ceiling, and per-player PDAs see near-zero local priority fees. The SOL float math is also right — 2 txs/tick x 168 ticks x 5,000 lamports = 0.00168 SOL = $0.13 against a 0.004 SOL airdrop. Credit where due.
  - VRF SHOCK RESOLUTION IS ON THE CRITICAL PATH OF A PAID TICK. Switchboard On-Demand costs 1-2 slots of callback latency and a callback tx. If the shock draw for tick N must land before clearing, a failed or delayed callback stalls a tick in which real USDC is at stake. Six times a day for 28 days is 168 chances to fail. Pre-commit the whole season's shock schedule as a hash at genesis and reveal per tick, or derive tick shocks from a single season VRF seed — you need unpredictability at commit time, not fresh entropy per tick.
- **Economic holes:**
  - THE TOWN TREASURIES ARE UNDERCAPITALISED BY ~8x AND THE ECONOMY IS INSOLVENT ON TICK ONE. 24 players x 120 USDC = 2,880 pool; the 15% dev float is 432 USDC across 12 towns = 36 USDC per town. A single full caravan (120 units at 2.41) is 289 USDC of goods. The town literally cannot pay for one delivery. Ratio 8:1 at MVP scale, and 48:1 against the full 3-caravan capacity of 20,000+ USDC of goods in flight.
  - THE OPERATOR IS THE COUNTERPARTY FOR EVERY COLLISION, WHICH DIRECTLY FALSIFIES THE PITCH. Aggregate player P&L is exactly the negative of the change in total town treasury; there is no direct player-to-player transfer channel. When two caravans collide and price impact destroys 214 USDC of value, that 214 USDC accrues to the town treasuries — i.e. to the dev's float. The 'collision report' viral card is a receipt for the house's winnings. Symmetrically, the dev's claim to 'recover the float at season end' is impossible if players collectively profit: total player winnings are capped at 432 USDC (15% of pool, or 12.5% on a 120 USDC stake), which cannot fund the claimed '+25 to +40% for a strong player' unless the dev eats the difference. Either the dev is the bank (and loses money), or the field cannot win, and the doc asserts both.
  - THE GUILD DRAINS THE FLOAT 9x OVER IN ONE SEASON. Four NPC caravans running a fully published deterministic rule set, funded from town treasuries, is a free 4-caravan-wide front-run. 4 caravans x 42 round trips x a conservative 20% predictable loss on 120 USDC of goods = 4,032 USDC of dev-funded bleed against a 432 USDC float. At 8 players the Guild is a third of all caravans, so the optimal human strategy is entirely 'trade against the published algorithm', which is not the opponent-modelling game the design is selling.
  - THE IMPACT COEFFICIENT IS 3-5x TOO STEEP FOR THE STATED CAPACITY. P = P_base(1 - 0.0022Q) floors at 0.35 when Q = 295 units. One 120-unit caravan alone moves price 26.4%. 24 players x 1 caravan x 120 units = 2,880 units, enough to floor 9.8 town-commodity pairs out of the 16 that exist in the MVP. At full 3-caravan capacity the entire map sits at the price floor permanently. For a full caravan to move price a healthy 8%, the coefficient should be 0.00067, not 0.0022.
  - FORCED BET SIZING IS 2x KELLY, SO EVEN PLAYERS WITH A REAL EDGE GO BUST. A delivery risks 100% of one caravan's capital. With a 2% edge and 20% per-delivery sd, Kelly says risk 50%. Over 40 deliveries, sd of log wealth = sqrt(40) x 0.20 = 1.26, so a one-sd bad season leaves a SKILLED player at 28% of stake. The 'bottom quartile loses 30-50%' estimate is materially too optimistic; the median outcome is worse than stated.
  - THE HOURLY RATE IS BELOW THE ATTENTION FLOOR OF THE TARGET POPULATION. 168 mandated check-ins over 28 days for a top-player outcome of +40% on 120 USDC = 48 USDC, which is $0.286 per session. You are asking a population that trades pump.fun to show up six times a day for a month for 29 cents a visit. And on a 4-tick round trip (buy, move, move, sell), ~50% of those check-ins have literally zero decision because the caravan is in transit.
- **Cold start:** Worse than claimed, and the failure is specifically in the mechanic the whole game is named for. The core experience is COLLISION, and collisions are quadratic in concurrent deliveries. On a 4-tick round trip only ~25% of caravans are delivering in any given tick. At 8 players across the MVP's 16 town-commodity venues that is ~2 deliveries/tick and 0.06 collision-pairs/tick — roughly 0.4 shareable collision cards PER DAY, on ~8 USDC of realised loss, not the 214 USDC in the hero mock. You need ~32 players to see 10 cards/day and ~60 to hit the density the pitch describes. The doc's claim that 'impact is MORE brutal at small N' is backwards: impact per collision is worse at small N, but the RATE of collision falls with the square, and rate is what the player experiences. At 8 players Saltline is a single-player spreadsheet against 4 published NPCs and a 36-USDC-per-town treasury that goes insolvent on the first full delivery. Separately, the free paper season with 60-second accelerated ticks is a second, differently-parameterised execution path — it is not a config flag, it is a second backend, and it is a meaningful slice of the 11 weeks.
- **Bot extraction:** A well-capitalised operator does not need capital here — the game is stake-capped at 500 USDC — so 'well-capitalised' converts into MULTI-ACCOUNTING, which the doc explicitly concedes is net-positive for hiding impact. Week one, ten wallets at 120 USDC each = 1,200 USDC deployed. The extraction is not from market skill: it is (1) the selective non-reveal option at 7.0 USDC per delivery per caravan — 10 caravans x 10 deliveries = ~700 USDC in week one, i.e. 58% of deployed capital, with no model at all; plus (2) systematic exploitation of the 4 published-algorithm Guild caravans, whose season-long predictable bleed is 4,032 USDC against a 432 USDC treasury, so the bot simply front-runs the Guild's next lane every tick until the treasuries are empty. Realistic week-one take against a 24-player, 2,880 USDC season: the bot extracts the ENTIRE 432 USDC town float plus ~700 USDC of abort-option value, then the towns are insolvent and the season is over on roughly day 6. Latency is worth zero, which the doc correctly identifies — but it identified the wrong axis. The exploitable surface is the reveal window and the published NPC, not the mempool.
- **Prior art:** Prosperous Universe (simulogics, live since 2019, on Steam since 2022) is the closest working precedent: an asynchronous player-driven commodity economy explicitly designed so 'most players only log in one or two times a day.' It survives on a SUBSCRIPTION, not a rake on player P&L, has never been financialised, and its playerbase is measured in the low thousands after seven years — which is the realistic ceiling for this genre's appeal, and it is a ceiling reached with no wallet friction at all. Trade Wars 2002 and Merchant Prince II are the ancestral turn-based versions. On the hidden-commitment side, Dark Forest (zkSNARK fog of war) and conquest.eth (commit-reveal) are the direct on-chain precedents, and conquest.eth's own documented friction is exactly the one Saltline inherits: 'this requires players to check back later to perform the reveal step' — a step which, as above, players will rationally skip when it is losing. The El Farol / minority game literature (Arthur 1994; the econophysics line through Challet-Zhang) is the formal statement of Saltline's metagame, and its central result is that adaptive agents self-coordinate to below-random fluctuation levels within a few hundred rounds. A 168-tick season sits inside that convergence window, so the doc's own worry that 'the metagame converges' is not a risk, it is a theorem.
- **Suggested fix:** Three changes, in order of severity. (1) MAKE IT ACTUALLY ZERO-SUM AND GET THE DEV OFF THE OTHER SIDE OF THE TRADE. Delete town treasuries as money holders. Towns become pure price functions; player deposits go into a single season prize pool that is redistributed by final RANK at settlement. Now aggregate P&L is exactly zero before rake, collision losses transfer to the players who avoided the collision rather than to the operator's float, the 'closed money supply is auditable' claim becomes true instead of merely technically true, and the dev is a rake-taker rather than a bank funding a 432 USDC prize pool with a 4,032 USDC Guild liability. (2) KILL THE REVEAL OPTION. Ship on MagicBlock PER in v1 — the TEE means nobody, including you and the crank operator, learns anything until the tick clears, commit-reveal disappears entirely, and the ER's 64 KB tx and 0-SOL gameplay fees make the tick crank trivial. If PER integration is too much risk for the timeline, bond every commitment at >= max downside with a default HOLD on non-reveal. (3) RETUNE: impact coefficient to 0.00067, add a 0.25%/tick inventory carry cost so shock-camping is -EV, cut the tick to 8 hours (3/day) so the mandated check-in count drops from 168 to 84 and the transit dead-time ratio improves, and replace the deterministic published Guild with a Guild whose rule set is published but whose parameters are drawn per-season from a committed hash. Do NOT ship a paid season before the free paper season has held 30 concurrent players for two consecutive weeks — the collision math says the product does not exist below ~32 players, and everything else is a rounding error next to that.

## ASSAY
*Lens: persistent-stakes-world*

**Pitch.** A hidden ore field nobody has seen — not even me — where you pay a dollar a hole to learn what's underground, and the only way to cash in your knowledge is to dig it up in public where everyone can watch.

**Core loop.** The world is a 256×256 subsurface grid — 65,536 cells — containing roughly 240 spatially correlated, log-normally sized ore deposits, generated at genesis from a seed committed inside a MagicBlock Private Ephemeral Rollup (TEE-backed). Land is sold as Claims: 8×8 blocks, 1,024 of them, 5 USDC flat from the registry if unclaimed, or whatever you can negotiate from a player. Your loop, every few hours: spend 1 USDC to drill one cell in a claim you own and receive the assay PRIVATELY from the TEE — nobody else, including me, sees the number. Correlation means one good hole tells you a lot about its neighbours, so drilling is Bayesian search, not a slot pull. When you think you know, you build a Rig (20 USDC, 3-day build, one per player) and issue an Extraction Order, which writes the tonnage and the claim ID PUBLICLY on-chain. That is the tension the entire game hangs on: you cannot convert knowledge into money without broadcasting where the ore is, and since deposits are spatially correlated, your extraction just repriced every claim around you. At season end (60 days) the Season Pool — 100% of all drill fees plus 100% of all registry claim sales, minus 8% — is paid out pro-rata by extracted tonnage. Claims carry superlinear maintenance of 0.1×n USDC per week for your nth claim, so hoarding is self-taxing, and a lapsed claim can be jumped by anyone for the flat 5 USDC.

**Depth curve.** Day 1 you are drilling semi-randomly and learning that holes cluster. Day 100 you are running three things at once. First, a geological model: 60 days of public extraction records is a partial map of the correlation structure, and reading it better than the field is a real, durable skill. Second, the lemons market: drill counts per claim are PUBLIC, assay results are not, so a claim with 14 drills and no extraction offered for sale is screaming "lemon" — the seller's silence is the signal, and the counter-play (bluff-drilling a claim you already know is barren, at 14 USDC of real cost, to sell it as a mystery) is a genuine, executable deception that costs money. Third, timing: extracting early gets the pool share locked in but hands your correlation data to everyone; extracting on the final day keeps it secret but risks a claim-jump or a rival draining an adjacent body first. Anti-snowball: a rich player converts money into better TARGETING, not more throughput, because one Rig per player caps extraction at one claim per 2 days for everyone regardless of wealth, and superlinear maintenance makes a 24-claim portfolio cost 30 USDC/week. Meanwhile every dollar the whale spends drilling goes straight into the pool everyone splits. The week-six newcomer joins a world that is still ~70% unsurveyed (200 players × 100 drills is only 30% of 65,536 cells), buys three frontier claims for 15 USDC, and has genuinely live information others don't — because the public extraction record tells them exactly which regions the field already picked over and which it ignored.

**Skill vs chance.** ~50% skill, 50% variance in any single season, and high variance is the CORRECT answer here rather than a compromise. This is a prospecting game; the emotional payload of the genre is the hole that comes back rich, and stripping the variance out would leave an inference exercise nobody feels anything about. More concretely, a pari-mutuel pool paying pro-rata by tonnage means a lucky newcomer with 25 USDC can take a real slice, which is precisely what keeps a field of 150 people playing against 10 people who are better. The skill shows up over seasons, not within one: across four 60-day seasons a strong prospector should run roughly +30 to +60% on spend while the median runs -35%, but in any single season the top-ten leaderboard will contain two people who simply got a good first hole, and I want it to.

**Why on-chain.** This is the one design in the set that genuinely requires a primitive that doesn't exist off-chain. The hidden geology must be hidden from the OPERATOR, not just from other players — if I hold the seed on a server, I can front-run every claim on the map and no rational player should ever believe I didn't. A TEE-backed Private ER holds the seed and serves per-cell assays with an attestation that the seed was committed before the first claim was sold and never read outside the enclave. "You can verify nobody, including the developer, saw the map" is the exact auditable-randomness value proposition that makes money-on-the-line hidden-information games possible at all. Everything else — claims, the market, the pool — is ordinary settlement, but that one thing is load-bearing and unavailable anywhere else.

**Solana primitives.**
  - MagicBlock Private Ephemeral Rollup (TEE) holding the genesis geology seed and serving private per-cell assays — 0 SOL base fee per ER tx makes 1-USDC drills economically sane, 0.0003 SOL per ER session and 0.0001 SOL per L1 commit are the only protocol costs
  - Switchboard On-Demand for the genesis seed, committed into the enclave, with the hash published before the first claim sale so the commitment is auditable
  - State compression / Bubblegum v2 for the 1,024 Claims — collection-scale ownership at fractions of a cent, making claim trading cheap enough to be a real market
  - Claim PDAs with lazy superlinear maintenance accrual against Clock::unix_timestamp; permissionless claim-jumping on lapse, no keeper
  - Pro-rata pool settlement at season end from a single Pool PDA — the only globally writable account in the design, and it is written once per season, so the 12M CU/writable-account/block ceiling never binds
  - Account close and rent refund on every Claim and Rig at season end
  - Jito bundles for extract+publish+pool-accrue so a tonnage can never be recorded without its pool credit

**Money flow.** There is no emission whatsoever and the pool size is publicly visible at every moment. Money enters as USDC: 1 USDC per drill, 5 USDC per registry claim, 20 USDC per Rig, plus maintenance. 92% of drill fees and registry sales flow into the Season Pool; 8% is the house. At season end the pool pays out pro-rata by extracted tonnage. That is a closed, fully-funded, skill-weighted pari-mutuel — it cannot ponzi-decay because the prize is never promised in advance, it is simply whatever players put in, displayed live. Arithmetic at 150 players averaging 55 USDC of spend per season: 8,250 USDC in, 660 to the house, 7,590 in the pool. A top prospector extracting 7% of the season's tonnage takes 531 USDC on maybe 180 spent. The median player spends 55 and gets back ~35. Dev revenue: 660 USDC per 60-day season at 150 players, plus 2% on claim-market trades — thin, and it only becomes a living at 1,000+ prospectors. I would rather state that than pretend.

**House edge / rake.** 8% of everything flowing into the Season Pool, plus 2% on secondary claim trades. Players tolerate it because it is the structure of every pari-mutuel they already understand (horse racing takes 15-20%), the pool is displayed live so the take is visible rather than inferred, and — the real reason — the 92% is being competed for on inference rather than surrendered to a house edge on a random draw. Nobody is playing against me; they are playing against the other 149 people, and I am the venue.

**Bots.** A bot that runs proper Bayesian inference over the public extraction record and its own private assay log is simply a good player, and it is welcome — this is a game ABOUT inference, so building the best inference engine is the intended mastery, and I would publish the data schema to encourage it. What bots cannot do is get more drilling budget or more Rigs; throughput is hard-capped at one Rig per player, so a bot's edge is bounded by capital and by the quality of its priors, which is exactly where I want the competition. Sybils are neutralised by construction: a pro-rata pool means ten wallets splitting one budget extract the same total tonnage as one wallet with the full budget, and each of the ten pays its own superlinear maintenance from n=1, which is strictly worse. The one real exploit is wash-trading claims between your own wallets to manufacture a price signal for a lemon — mitigated by the 2% trade rake and by the fact that public drill counts already tell the true story of how much someone knows.

**Cold start plan.** This has the strongest cold start of the three because the antagonist is the map, not the other players. At 8 players the loop is fully intact: drill, infer, extract, watch the pool. Week one with 8 players and ~60 USDC of spend each puts about 440 USDC in the pool, which is a real prize for eight people. The claim market is thin but claims are cheap from the registry so nobody is blocked. Every extraction is still a public event that moves everyone's model, and with 8 players you know exactly who found what — arguably the social layer is TIGHTER at 8 than at 200. The design works at 8, is good at 40, and is best at 100-400; past 400 the field surveys too fast and I open a second 256×256 field with a fresh seed rather than enlarging the first, because the joy is in the unknown fraction.

**First session.** Link from Crypto Twitter, web page, no install. Wallet or passkey in 20 seconds, funding via an on-page swap, minimum 20 USDC. Minute 1: the map is a CSS grid of 1,024 claim tiles — grey for unsurveyed, coloured by public extraction history, with drill counts printed on each. It reads instantly as a treasure map with holes in it. Minute 2: three free tutorial drills on a sandbox claim show the correlation structure — hole, hole, hole, and the third one lights up because it was next to the second. That is the entire game taught in 40 seconds with no text. Minute 3: buy a real claim for 5 USDC, one transaction. Minute 4: first real drill — 1 USDC, ER transaction, result back in about a second because the ER runs ~10ms slots and the fee is zero. Minute 5: the client shows your private assay overlay next to the public extraction record and asks the only question that matters: drill again here, or move? Minute 6: I show the live pool number and your current zero share of it.

**Viral mechanic.** Extraction is inherently public and inherently dramatic: every Extraction Order fires a live ticker event — "claim H-14 pulled 340 tonnes" — and a share card showing the claim, the tonnage, the number of holes it took to find it, and the total spent to get there. A 12-USDC find that pulls a top-ten body is the best possible advertisement and it produces itself. Secondly, and more interestingly, the extraction ticker is a public information good, so people will build and share their own inference maps on Twitter — third-party analysts are free content, free credibility, and free distribution, and I would seed that deliberately by publishing the schema on day one. The season-end payout, being a single on-chain pari-mutuel settlement anyone can verify, is its own event.

**Four-week MVP.** Week 1: Claim PDA via Bubblegum v2, registry sale, superlinear maintenance with lazy accrual, permissionless claim-jumping on lapse. Week 2: the hidden-geology service. Honest sequencing risk — the PER/TEE path is the newest and least-documented tech in this whole brief, so MVP v0 ships a dev-run assay oracle with the genesis seed HASH published on-chain before the first claim sells and the seed revealed at season end for full verification. Trust-minimised, not trustless, and I say so on the landing page. v1 moves the seed into the PER. Week 3: Rig build, Extraction Order with public tonnage write, Season Pool accrual, pro-rata settlement, deposit/withdraw, account closure. Week 4: client — claim grid, private assay overlay in local storage plus encrypted backup, public extraction ticker, share cards. EXPLICITLY CUT: the secondary claim market with listings and bids (v0 uses direct wallet-to-wallet claim transfer with a manual price, which is uglier and honestly funnier), multiple Rigs, ore types, refining, alliances, mobile, and any second season mechanic.

**Biggest risk.** Two, and they compound. First, the TEE dependency: if the Private ER path slips or its attestation story is weaker than advertised, I ship a game whose core selling point is "nobody saw the map" while being the person who saw the map, and the first person who notices writes the thread that kills it. Second, and more likely: the game may be finished. Once the field is 80% surveyed, the season's remaining tension collapses and everyone is just waiting for settlement — so the pacing of the reveal has to be tuned so that survey coverage crosses 60% around day 50 of 60, and I do not yet know the drill-rate elasticity that produces that. Get it wrong in either direction and the season either resolves in week two or never gets interesting.

### Hostile review

- **Verdict:** buildable-with-caveats
- **Solo-dev estimate:** 12 weeks
- **Degenerate strategy:** Never drill, never extract early. Buy three cheap frontier claims to be eligible, then spend the season doing nothing but reading the extraction ticker and public drill counts. When any honest prospector reveals a body, buy the adjacent claims at 5 USDC from the registry and extract on his correlation. Order your own extraction on the final possible day so you leak nothing and can see the tonnage denominator before committing. Total spend ~35 USDC against the median player's 55, with materially higher expected tonnage. Once two or three people run this line publicly, drilling stops, the pool stops growing (it is 92% drill fees), and the season resolves into a race to snipe adjacency around whichever naive player still believes in prospecting. The tell that this is the real line: it costs less than the intended strategy, requires none of the skill you are selling, and is unbeatable by the counter-play you built — bluff-drilling at 14 USDC is EV-negative against someone who simply never buys drilled claims.
- **Technical blockers:**
  - TEE SEED PERSISTENCE ACROSS A 60-DAY SEASON IS THE UNSOLVED PROBLEM AND YOU HAVE NOT COSTED IT. PERs are real — Intel TDX, mainnet since March 2026, TEE-backed validator region live. But an Ephemeral Rollup is ephemeral: accounts are delegated, JIT-cloned, and the session is charged at undelegation. You need a secret surviving 60 days and an unbounded number of ER sessions, which means sealing the seed to the enclave and reloading it — enclave key management, not a documented path for a game dev. Budget 4–6 weeks for this alone and assume you are the first person doing it.
  - A PUBLISHED SEED HASH DOES NOT PREVENT DEVELOPER FRONT-RUNNING, SO YOUR v0 PITCH IS FALSE. hash(seed) proves you did not CHANGE the map; it proves nothing about who READ it. In v0 you know the seed, claims are pseudonymous, and a dev sybil buying the best 40 claims for 200 USDC and extracting the top bodies takes 30–40% of the pool undetectably. Every sophisticated player will assume exactly this, because it is what they would do. This is a launch-ORDER problem, not a feature-order problem: you cannot ship season 1 on trust and add the TEE in season 2, because season 1's results permanently taint the record and the thread writes itself.
  - BUBBLEGUM v2 IS THE WRONG TOOL FOR 1,024 ITEMS. State compression is for millions. cNFT ownership cannot be read in-program without a merkle proof — depth-10 tree = 320 bytes of proof inside a 1,232-byte tx on EVERY drill, unless you buy a full canopy (~32 KB account, ~0.22 SOL, which you should). 1,024 plain PDAs at ~80 bytes cost 0.00056 SOL each, 0.57 SOL total, fully refundable, and are simpler in every dimension. Cutting cNFTs saves a week and removes a bug class.
  - THE 65,536-CELL DRILLED BITMAP MUST BE SHARDED. 8 KB fits in one account, but that account is written by every drill in the world. Split into 1,024 per-claim 64-bit bitmaps colocated with the claim PDA. Same for the Pool PDA — you describe it as 'written once per season' but it accrues on every 1-USDC drill, ~1,200 writes/day at 150 players. CU is not the constraint (12M / 15k = 800/block); the hot-account fee market is.
  - Per-drill economics are fine and this part you got right: 0 SOL ER base fee, 0.0003 SOL/session, 0.0001 SOL per L1 commit (~$0.008 at SOL $80 — 0.8% of a $1 drill, and batchable). This is the only one of the three designs whose cost model actually supports its action frequency.
- **Economic holes:**
  - DRILLING FUNDS THE POOL AND NOT-DRILLING WINS IT. Payout share = your tonnage / total tonnage; the pool is other people's drill fees; deposits are spatially correlated and extraction is published by claim. So the optimal player never drills: he watches the ticker, and when someone extracts a fat body at H-14 he buys the adjacent claims for 5 USDC and extracts on correlation the other player paid 30 USDC of holes to discover. Finding costs 20–40 USDC; free-riding costs 5. You describe this as the design's central tension — 'your extraction just repriced every claim around you' — without noticing it makes honest prospecting strictly dominated. The people funding the prize pool are precisely the people publishing the information that beats them.
  - THERE IS NO REASON TO EXTRACT BEFORE THE LAST DAY, SO NOTHING OBSERVABLE HAPPENS ALL SEASON. You claim early extraction 'locks in the pool share.' It does not — the pool is pro-rata at settlement, so extracting early locks in nothing and costs you your entire information edge. Every rational player therefore orders extraction on day 59. Consequence: the ticker never fires, the lemons market has no signal, the correlation record never accumulates, third-party analysts have no data, your share cards never generate, and your viral mechanic — genuinely the best one in this set — is silent for 59 of 60 days. Your stated biggest risk is the field being surveyed too fast; the real risk is 59 days of nothing followed by one day of settlement. Structural, not tunable.
  - PARI-MUTUEL ON LOG-NORMAL TONNAGE IS WINNER-TAKE-MOST AND YOU START AT SMALL N. Log-normal deposit sizes mean the top body may be 10x the median. At 150 players that is the healthy variance you correctly argue for. At 8 players it means one person takes 60%+ of the pool and seven people lose ~100% in week one and never return. Your variance argument is right at scale and inverts exactly where you must start.
  - THE SEASON IS A TOURNAMENT, NOT A PERSISTENT-STAKES WORLD. Claims dissolve, accounts close, a new field opens — fine as a product, but it is not the category brief, and it means re-solving cold start every 60 days, which is the specific thing that killed Dark Forest's round model.
  - DEV REVENUE IS 660 USDC PER 60 DAYS AT 150 PLAYERS AND YOU SAY SO, WHICH I RESPECT. That is $330/month. One dev breaks even around 2,500–3,000 concurrent prospectors — ~0.09% of all daily active Solana wallets, every one of whom must be persuaded to do Bayesian search instead of trading. The economics are honest and thin; the distribution ask is the number to stare at.
- **Cold start:** The best of the three, and you are right about why — the antagonist is the map, so the loop is fully intact at N=8. 8 x 60 USDC = 480 USDC pool, 442 after rake, a real prize for eight people. 8 players x 10 holes/day x 60 days = 4,800 holes = 7.3% of 65,536 cells, so the field stays 93% unknown, which is correct — the joy is the unknown fraction. Two caveats. First, the lemons market and the claim market need counterparties and have none at 8, so the deception layer — your most interesting claim — does not exist until roughly 40 players. Second and more serious: pari-mutuel with 8 entrants and log-normal bodies is a lottery. One winner takes most, seven are down 100%, and week two has three players. Fix that before you fix anything else about small N.
- **Bot extraction:** 1,500–2,500 USDC out of an 8,250 USDC pool in week one — real, but the most bounded of the three, and your throughput caps (one Rig, one claim per 2 days) are doing genuine work. The extraction does not come from better geology; it comes from two places you did not defend. (1) Lapse sniping: an indexer over maintenance_paid_through across 1,024 claims plus a Helius Sender landing layer takes 100% of expired claims at 5 USDC each, and public drill counts let the bot see which lapsed claims someone already paid to survey — it buys other people's information for 5 USDC. Call it 40 lapses in week one x ~25 USDC expected value = 1,000 USDC for 200 USDC of cost. (2) The lemons market is trivially bot-solved: 'never buy a claim with more than 6 drills and no extraction' beats every human seller and defeats your bluff-drilling counter-play at zero cost. Defences that work: a 48-hour on-chain grace warning before a claim becomes jumpable, and coarsening public drill counts into buckets (0 / 1–5 / 6–15 / 15+) so the lemons signal is noisy enough that bluffing has EV again.
- **Prior art:** Dark Forest (2020, Gubsheep, ETH/xDAI) is the direct ancestor — procedurally generated hidden map, zkSNARK fog of war, a universe nobody has seen. It proved the mechanic is enthralling and proved the failure shape: rounds ended, organised guilds dominated (OrdenGG won the final three v0.6 rounds by wide margins; dfdao), and the game is no longer playable. Your PER/TEE approach is genuinely better than zkSNARKs here — cheaper per query, no proving time, and it hides the map from the operator, which zk-DF also achieved structurally. Prospectors (2019, EOS) is the closest economic relative: an MMO real-time economic strategy game about gold mining with real money extraction and spatially distributed resources, still nominally running, never at scale — the lesson being that mining-for-real-money attracts optimisers, not prospectors. The grid-treasure genre has been patented into the ground on the lottery side (US 9,177,434 map-based lottery games; US 8,771,059 prize discovery games — GeoSweep-style hidden treasure on a grid where strategy affects winnings), which is a useful signal that the hook is real and the framing well-trodden. Academic mineral-exploration simulations (MDPI 2025, ten scenarios of ore-body spatial distribution) are the closest thing to your actual inference loop and confirm the Bayesian search is a genuine, teachable skill. Nobody has combined hidden correlated geology with an operator-blind commitment and a pari-mutuel — that combination is the most defensible original idea in this whole set.
- **Suggested fix:** One change fixes your two worst problems at once: multiply extracted tonnage by a time factor, e.g. tonnage x (1 + 0.5 x fraction_of_season_remaining). Now early extraction is paid for the information it leaks, the ticker fires all season, the correlation record accumulates, the lemons market gets signal, your share cards actually generate, and free-riding on someone else's reveal is partly priced because the free-rider extracts later at a lower multiplier. Do this before anything else. Second: cap any single player's pool share at 25% with the excess redistributed, or pay top-N places tournament-style — that is the structure your audience already understands from poker and it converts small-N winner-take-all into something seven losing players will re-enter. Third: shorten the geological correlation length to 4 cells or fewer so a deposit never straddles an 8x8 claim boundary; you lose some 'my extraction repriced the map' drama but you kill outright adjacency free-riding, and you can restore the drama by publishing extractions at region granularity. Fourth, on sequencing: do not launch on a dev-held seed. Either get the PER working or have a named third party hold it with a public attestation — your one genuinely unavailable-elsewhere primitive is also the only reason this is on-chain, and shipping season 1 without it burns the exact credibility the whole design exists to buy.

## MARCHLAND
*Lens: persistent-stakes-world*

**Pitch.** A persistent map of holdings where every player's wallet balance sits inside a fort that other players can crack open — and the only way to get your money out is to send it across the map in a caravan anyone can ambush.

**Core loop.** Every 8 hours (3 world ticks/day, fixed UTC, no crank — each Hold PDA stores last_tick_index and any instruction touching it replays elapsed production deterministically) you open the map and make one decision, one transaction. Your Hold produces 12 Levy/tick per held tile and pays superlinear upkeep of 2n Levy/tick for your nth tile. You spend Levy on exactly one of: claim a neutral Freehold (deterministic NPC garrison = hash(tile_pda, tick_index) % range, no money at stake, this is the tutorial), raid a neighbouring player, reinforce your three wall segments, or launch/escort a Caravan. A raid is Colonel Blotto: attacker commits sha256(alloc_seg0, alloc_seg1, alloc_seg2, feint, nonce) in one tx, defender's total garrison is public but its split across the three segments was committed at set-time, both reveal inside one tick or forfeit 100% of committed Levy. Win a segment when attack > defense × 1.15; take 2 of 3 and you breach. Spoils = 20% of the defender's Granary, capped at 2× your own Granary (you can never win more than you have exposed), 5% to the house. The 30-second unit is: read the map, read who got rich this tick, decide who is worth cracking. You come back because the tick is a clock everyone else is also on, and because your Granary is a number on a public map with your name on it.

**Depth curve.** Day 1 you are solving "can I beat this garrison." Day 100 you are solving "what is this specific human's Blotto distribution, and what does his caravan schedule tell me about when he is going to quit." Blotto has no pure-strategy equilibrium — the mastery object is reading a specific opponent's mixing bias over 40+ observed engagements, plus target selection under the rule that spoils scale with the DEFENDER's wealth. That rule is also the entire anti-snowball system: farming poor neighbours is worthless (a Hold below 0.5× map-median Granary yields 5% spoils instead of 20% and still costs full Levy), so the strong are mechanically forced to attack each other. Wealth is aggro. Territorial snowball is capped separately by superlinear upkeep: 12 tiles produce 144 Levy/tick and cost 156, so empires plateau near 11 tiles and further growth must go through Vassalage — a vassal pays 10% of production to a liege, and if the liege fails to answer a third-party raid on the vassal within 2 ticks the oath breaks for free. That turns alliances into contracts with enforceable failure conditions instead of Discord promises. Newcomer in week six: the map grows a fresh 60-tile ring whenever occupancy passes 70%, the ring is at the periphery of everyone's supply range, a founding Hold is worth ~0.25 SOL so raiding it returns 5% of nearly nothing, and Freeholds give a money-free ladder to competence. Seasons are 90 days (270 ticks); at season end all Granaries unlock fee-free for 7 days, the map regenesizes, and the top ten Holds get a permanent Solana Attestation Service credential — reputation persists, power does not.

**Skill vs chance.** ~70% skill, 30% variance, and the variance is almost entirely opponent-modelling error rather than dice — the only RNG in the whole game is a single Switchboard On-Demand draw at world genesis to assign tile quality, and NPC Freehold garrisons derived from hash(tile_pda, tick_index) which are unmanipulable but not secret. That ratio is right because Blotto's mixed equilibrium means even a perfect player loses individual engagements roughly a third of the time, which keeps a weaker player's hope alive across the 20+ raids in a season while the aggregate still resolves to skill. Zero dice on the money means nobody can claim the house rolled against them: every loss is traceable to a revealed allocation the loser can inspect on-chain. Expected edge: a strong player nets +35 to +45% on Granary per 90-day season against an average field; the median player is down 10-15%; the house takes 5% of every spoils transfer and 2% of contested exits.

**Why on-chain.** Two things break on a normal web server. First, the spoils are real money moving between strangers with no counterparty relationship, three times a day, in sub-cent-fee increments — that is exactly the permissionless-wagering PMF and nothing else does it. Second, the Blotto commit-reveal has to be verifiably tamper-proof or the loser will always suspect the server peeked at the commitment before resolving; a program that can only read the hash makes the accusation impossible. The persistent Hold state itself could absolutely live in Postgres — I am not going to pretend otherwise. The chain is here for settlement and for the auditability of the reveal, not for the simulation.

**Solana primitives.**
  - PDAs sharded per Hold, per Tile and per Raid so no global writable account is ever touched during gameplay — the 12M CU/writable-account/block ceiling is never approached and local fee markets keep priority fees near zero on per-player PDAs
  - Commit-reveal via sha256 for the Blotto allocation (brief-endorsed as cheap and strong for 2-party), avoiding VRF cost and latency entirely
  - Switchboard On-Demand called exactly once, at world genesis, to seed tile quality
  - Jito bundles (5 tx, atomic, same slot) for the reveal+resolve+spoils-transfer path so a raid can never partially apply
  - Account close + rent refund on Raid PDAs and on dissolved Holds at season end (~6,960 lamports/byte recovered)
  - Solana Attestation Service for cross-season reputation credentials, replacing a token as the persistence layer
  - Helius Sender / staked SWQoS connection for the landing layer — with 8h ticks a dropped tx is a lost turn, which is the single worst UX failure this design has

**Money flow.** There is no faucet. Every USDC in Marchland was deposited by a player; tiles produce Levy and Grain, never money. That is deliberate and it is the whole economic thesis: the world is zero-sum plus rake, like poker, so it cannot ponzi-decay when deposits slow — it just gets smaller. Founding a Hold requires 0.25 SOL minimum and Granary is hard-capped at 5 SOL, so you cannot buy power, only expose more of it; power comes from tiles and attention. Money moves player-to-player through raid spoils and caravan interception. Dev revenue: 5% of every spoils transfer, 2% on contested (>0.2 SOL) exits, 4% on small courier exits. With 150 active Holds averaging 1.2 SOL Granary and a raid frequency of 0.4 successful breaches per Hold per week, weekly spoils volume is roughly 150 × 0.4 × 0.24 SOL ≈ 14.4 SOL, so ~0.72 SOL/week to the house from raiding plus exit fees — call it $80-120/week at SOL $80 with 150 players, scaling linearly. That is not a business at 150 players; it is a business at 2,000. Say that out loud before building.

**House edge / rake.** 5% of spoils on every successful raid, 2% on interceptable caravan exits, 4% on safe courier exits, 0% on deposits. Players tolerate it for the same reason poker players tolerate rake: it is charged on the pot they just won, it is visible in the transaction, and it is not charged on the money they brought. Critically the rake is NOT charged per tick — a player who sits quietly and never fights pays nothing, so the world does not bleed out passive holders and the pool shrinks only through actual play. Effective drag on a player making 30 raids a season is about 1.5% of their Granary per raid won, which is well under the variance they are already accepting.

**Bots.** Assume 40% of Holds are botted by month two and design for it. A bot can compute an equilibrium Blotto mix and time its ticks perfectly — and it still cannot exploit anyone, because equilibrium play in Blotto is unexploitable but also non-exploiting: it wins nothing against another equilibrium player and loses the rake. The exploitative edge lives in reading a specific opponent's mixing bias, which is exactly what a naive bot cannot do and a good human or a good bot-with-a-model can. So botting raises the floor, not the ceiling, and I would publish the state-read RPC helpers myself. The genuinely dangerous vector is not botting but sybil-collusion: twenty accounts feeding losses to one. It is defused structurally — because there is no emission, a sybil ring has nothing to farm; feeding losses to yourself just pays 5% rake to me on your own money. The only real collusion play is coordinated ganking of a rich player, which is politics, which is the game.

**Cold start plan.** Week one launches at map radius 4 — 61 tiles, everyone within 3 hexes of everyone, no empty middle. With 8 players the map is ~45 neutral Freeholds and 8 Holds. Freeholds are deterministic PvE garrisons with no money at stake: they teach Blotto, they give Grain and tiles, and they mean a solo player at 3am always has something to do that costs a transaction and returns progress. The 8h tick means 8 players produce 24 meaningful world events a day, which is a readable feed. The map only grows when occupancy exceeds 70%, so density is maintained by construction rather than hoped for. The design is playable at 8, good at 60, and best at 150-400; above ~500 I shard into parallel realms rather than growing the map, because the fun requires knowing your neighbours' names.

**First session.** Player clicks a link from a Crypto Twitter post. Web page, no install. Detects Backpack/Phantom; if no wallet, embedded wallet via a passkey in 20 seconds. Funding: the page shows a QR/address and a Jupiter swap widget for anyone arriving with SOL — minimum 0.25 SOL, and I say plainly "this is money you can lose to other players." Minute 1: pick a hex on the frontier ring, one transaction, Hold founded, rent-exempt PDA (~0.004 SOL, refunded at season end). Minute 2: an interactive Blotto tutorial against a Freehold with fake Levy — three sliders, three walls, no money. Minute 3: real first raid on an actual Freehold, one tx, commit now, reveal auto-scheduled by the client 8h later (the client holds the nonce in local storage AND encrypted in the Hold PDA so a lost device doesn't forfeit). Minute 4: I show them the four richest Holds within reach, their Granary sizes, and the 20%-cap arithmetic on what a breach would pay. Then I tell them to close the tab and come back in 8 hours. That last instruction is the product.

**Viral mechanic.** Every successful breach mints a shareable settlement card — a CSS/emoji rendering of the three wall segments, both revealed allocations, and the exact SOL that changed hands, with the loser's handle on it. Losing publicly is the distribution engine; people repost their own defeats when the read was good. Secondly, Caravans are public: when someone launches a 3 SOL exit caravan the whole map sees a 48-hour countdown, and that is a spontaneous, unscheduled, all-hands event that generates its own Twitter thread every time it happens. A Blink for "escort this caravan" or "join the ambush" is the natural share unit — with the caveat from the brief that Blinks are a viral primitive, not an acquisition channel.

**Four-week MVP.** Week 1: Anchor program — Hold PDA, Tile PDA, lazy tick replay, claim/abandon, Levy production and superlinear upkeep. Week 2: Raid PDA with commit-reveal Blotto, segment resolution, spoils transfer with the 20%/2×-cap/poor-target rules, Jito bundle for reveal+settle. Week 3: Caravan and Courier exits, deposit/withdraw, Freehold NPC garrisons, season-end settlement and account closure. Week 4: client — CSS hex grid, emoji terrain, wallet connect, tick countdown, settlement cards, and the landing layer (Helius Sender with retry and a client-side nonce vault). EXPLICITLY CUT from MVP: Vassalage, Grain-to-Levy conversion buildings, map ring expansion (launch fixed at radius 4 and hand-expand it), the Solana Attestation Service integration, alliances of any kind, mobile app, and any market for anything. Ship 61 tiles, one combat system, one exit path.

**Biggest risk.** The world is zero-sum minus rake, so the median player loses money, and a persistent world takes weeks to teach — which means the median player loses money slowly over three weeks and then has to decide whether that felt like entertainment. Poker survives this; most games do not, because poker gives you a hand every 90 seconds and I am giving you a raid every two days. If the 8h tick makes losses feel like erosion rather than drama, retention dies at day 12 and no economic tuning saves it. The mitigation is that Freeholds and the public map have to be genuinely interesting with play chips — if I can't get people to check the map twice a day when nothing is at stake, the money is a casino skin and I should stop.

## TENURE
*Lens: persistent-stakes-world*

**Pitch.** Every parcel of land in this world is permanently for sale at whatever price its owner claims it's worth — and they pay 2% of that claim per day for the privilege, so lying about it costs them either money or the land.

**Core loop.** The world is 120 parcels on a 12×10 grid, each with one of five terrains (Ore, Coal, Timber, Water, Clay) assigned once at genesis by a single Switchboard On-Demand draw. You own parcels under Harberger tenure: you post a self-assessed price in USDC, you pay 2%/day of that price into the Commons Pool (accrued per second, settled lazily whenever the parcel PDA is touched), and ANYONE may buy it from you at your posted price at any moment with no consent. Your 30-second loop is: check whether anyone has bid on your keystone parcels, check the tax burn against last tick's payout, and reprice something. Production ticks every 8 hours: raw parcels make 1 production point; a Foundry on an Ore parcel adjacent to a Coal parcel consumes 3 Ore + 2 Coal and makes Steel worth 4 pp; Timber+Water makes Plank at 2 pp; Clay+Coal makes Brick at 3 pp. The Commons Pool — everyone's tax — is paid out each tick pro-rata to production points, not to assessments. So you pay on what you claim it's worth and you earn on what you actually make with it. Buying is a two-phase act: you post a Bid locking the posted price, and 30 minutes later it executes unless the incumbent retains by matching +10% — which is paid to YOU as a break fee, and forces their assessment (and therefore their tax) up to the new number. A bid is never wasted: you either get the land or get paid for being right about it.

**Depth curve.** Day 1 you are learning that overpricing costs tax and underpricing loses the parcel. Day 100 you are running the holdout problem: recipes need ADJACENCY, so a contiguous cluster is worth several times the sum of its parcels, but a rival only has to pay the single-parcel price to buy the keystone in the middle of your Foundry chain and break it. Defending a keystone means overpricing it on purpose and paying real tax forever — a permanent, quantified extortion premium. Reading other people's assessments backwards to infer their private cluster value, building spite-clusters adjacent to someone's chain purely as a threat, cornering Coal so that every Foundry on the map starves — that is the day-100 game and it is EVE-grade economic warfare in about 600 lines of Anchor. Anti-snowball is structural and needs no rule: a monopolist holding 60 parcels pays 2%/day on all of it, while payout is capped by recipe throughput (a Foundry eats exactly 3 Ore/tick, one Foundry per parcel), so the 14th Ore parcel you own produces nothing and taxes you daily. Over-accumulation is mathematically tax-negative. The week-six newcomer has the best answer in this whole category: land is NEVER unavailable. Every parcel on the map has a posted price and can be bought this second. A newcomer with 40 USDC buys the cheapest parcel in one transaction and is immediately playing — and can immediately threaten a large holder's chain, which is high-leverage at low capital.

**Skill vs chance.** ~85% skill, and I want to be honest that this is a cost, not just a virtue. The only randomness is a ±20% per-tick production jitter from hash(parcel_pubkey, tick_index) — unmanipulable, since no player controls when the hash inputs change. Low variance means weak players bleed steadily instead of occasionally getting lucky, which is worse for retention than a noisier design. I accept that trade because the mastery here is pricing under threat, and pricing games are ruined by noise: if production were random, an assessment couldn't be read as a signal, and reading assessments is the entire social layer. Expected edge: a player who correctly prices keystones and assembles a 6-parcel matched cluster earns roughly 2.5× the Commons share of a player holding 14 scattered parcels with the same capital, and the strong player nets +25-40% on deployed capital per 60-day cycle while the bottom half runs -15 to -25%.

**Why on-chain.** Harberger tenure is the rare mechanic that is strictly better with a program than a server, because its whole credibility rests on "the owner genuinely cannot refuse the sale." On a server that is a promise; in an Anchor program it is an instruction anyone can call. The same goes for foreclosure: when tax funding lapses, anyone may call foreclose and collect a 5% bounty, so enforcement is permissionless and does not depend on me running a cron. And the exit story — that a player can always liquidate by lowering their own price until someone takes it — is only trustworthy if the buy instruction is public and unstoppable. Take those away and Harberger degrades into "a landlord who says he'll sell."

**Solana primitives.**
  - One Parcel PDA per parcel (owner, price, tax_paid_through_ts, terrain, building) — 120 independent writable accounts, so the 12M CU/writable-account/block ceiling is a non-issue and the local fee market on each parcel means a contested keystone gets its own priority auction, which is thematically perfect
  - Lazy tax and production accrual against Clock::unix_timestamp — no crank, no keeper, no global tick account
  - USDC (SPL Token) treasuries per player; explicitly no native token, per the economic record
  - Permissionless foreclosure with a 5% bounty, replacing an off-chain job with an incentive
  - Switchboard On-Demand once at genesis for terrain assignment; nothing else needs randomness
  - Jito bundles for the bid-execute-or-retain resolution so the transfer and the tax settlement can never split
  - Account close and rent refund (~6,960 lamports/byte) when a cycle ends and parcels dissolve
  - Token-2022 transfer hook as a v2 path: enforcing that a parcel NFT cannot move except through the Harberger instruction, making the tenure rule unrevocable even if wrapped

**Money flow.** Money enters as USDC deposits into a player Treasury. It moves three ways: player-to-player on every Harberger purchase and break fee, player-to-Commons via the 2%/day tax, and Commons-to-player each tick pro-rata to production. There is no emission and no faucet — the Commons Pool is funded entirely by land tax, which is a consumption sink (holding scarce land), not by new entrants. That is the specific structural difference between this and every dead play-to-earn economy: payout does not require growth, it requires that people hold land. If deposits stop entirely, the world keeps functioning at a smaller scale, redistributing from hoarders to producers indefinitely. Dev revenue: 4% skimmed off the tax inflow to the Commons, 1% of every parcel transfer, 1% on withdrawal. With 120 parcels averaging a 90 USDC assessment, the map taxes 216 USDC/day, so 8.6 USDC/day to the house from tax plus transfer fees at maybe 15 transfers/day × 90 × 1% = 13.5 USDC/day. Roughly $650/month at 120 parcels and ~80 players. Map expands to 240 parcels at 80% occupancy; revenue scales with assessed value, which scales with player competition, not player count.

**House edge / rake.** 4% of the tax stream (i.e. 0.08%/day of assessed value — 2% of your assessment goes out, 1.92% comes back to the pool) plus 1% on transfers and 1% on withdrawal. Players tolerate it because the dominant cost they feel is the tax itself, which they set, and which they get back as Commons yield if they are producing efficiently. The rake is a rounding error against the strategic decisions — nobody rage-quits over 0.08%/day when they just lost a keystone parcel to a rival for reasons entirely of their own pricing. The honest risk is the opposite one: the tax is so salient that players blame the game for a cost they chose.

**Bots.** Bots are welcome and actively good here — a bot that sniffs underpriced parcels is an arbitrageur that makes assessments honest, which is exactly what the mechanism wants. Day one, someone runs a scanner that bids on anything priced below its production-implied value; within a week assessments across the map tighten. The failure mode is humans never getting a chance, and the 30-minute shout window plus the retain-at-+10% right fixes it precisely: the human owner always gets a half-hour and a way out, and the bot that was RIGHT still profits from the break fee. So speed converts into a smaller, fairer return rather than into confiscation. Sybils have nothing to farm — the Commons pays pro-rata to production, so splitting across ten wallets splits your production ten ways and multiplies your tax bookkeeping. Wash-trading parcels between your own wallets to fake a price signal costs 1% per round trip and signals nothing anyone has to believe.

**Cold start plan.** With 8 players and 120 parcels, most land is unowned, which is fine because unowned parcels sit in a permanently repeating 30-day Dutch auction descending from 50 USDC to 5 USDC — there is always something cheap and the world is never gated. At 8 players the game plays as a satisfying single-player builder: find a matched Ore/Coal pair, build a Foundry, watch the Commons share arrive every 8 hours. The Commons Pool is small in absolute terms but payout is proportional, so 8 players still see meaningful shares — a player holding 20% of map production gets 20% of the tax, whether that tax is 30 USDC/day or 3,000. The PvP layer switches on organically the first time two people want the same Coal parcel. Playable at 8, good at 40, best at 100-300; above 300 I expand the map to 240 parcels rather than shard, because a bigger board with the same competitive density is strictly more interesting here.

**First session.** Link from Crypto Twitter, web page, no install. Wallet connect or passkey-embedded wallet in 20 seconds; funding via an on-page swap widget, minimum 25 USDC. Minute 1: the map loads as a 12×10 CSS grid with emoji terrain, every parcel labelled with its posted price. I do not explain Harberger with a paragraph; I show one parcel with a price tag, a BUY button, and the words "this is also true of everything you own." Minute 2: buy the cheapest parcel — one transaction, and the confirm dialog makes you set YOUR price on it before it completes, which teaches the mechanic by forcing the decision. The dialog shows live arithmetic: "at 60 USDC you pay 1.20/day and anyone can take it for 60." Minute 3: the client highlights every parcel adjacent to yours that would complete a recipe, with the exact production-point delta. Minute 4: you buy or bid on one of them, and I show you your projected Commons share at the next 8h tick. Minute 5: a standing notification — "someone bid on your parcel" — is the only push this game ever sends, and it is the entire retention hook.

**Viral mechanic.** The Assessment Board: a public leaderboard not of wealth but of the biggest gaps between what someone pays tax on and what their cluster is provably worth, computed from public adjacency and production. It is a hit list, published by the game, updated every tick — "these four people are underpaying for what they hold." Sharing it is free advertising and joining is one transaction. Secondly, every hostile keystone purchase generates a share card showing the cluster before and after the cut, with the production points destroyed. Breaking someone's Foundry chain for 90 USDC is a very screenshot-able act of violence with no violence in it.

**Four-week MVP.** Week 1: Parcel PDA, Treasury PDA, set_price, buy with the 30-minute shout window and the retain-at-+10% break fee, lazy tax accrual, permissionless foreclosure with bounty. Week 2: terrain genesis via Switchboard, production points, three recipes, adjacency checks, lazy per-parcel production accrual. Week 3: Commons Pool accounting and pro-rata tick payout (computed lazily on claim, not pushed — critical, since a pushed payout to 120 accounts is a crank and a CU disaster), deposit/withdraw, cycle settlement. ## ASSAY
*Lens: variance-strategy*

**Pitch.** An 8-minute prospecting run where you decide, twelve times, whether to bank what you've dug up or borrow against it and dig deeper — and your score that day is ranked against everyone else who ran, with the pot split top-heavy.

**Core loop.** One round = ~40 seconds. Five claims are on the board, each showing three public tells (depth band 1-5, grade rumor A-E, hazard 0-9) that map to one of 8 hidden yield archetypes (payout multipliers ranging from 0.0x to 9x on capital committed, with wildly different shapes: 'Shelf' is 0.8x-1.4x tight, 'Chimney' is 0.0x 84% of the time and 14x otherwise). You do three things: (1) spend 40-120 credits on an assay to shrink your posterior on one claim from 8 archetypes to 2-3, (2) allocate working capital across claims, (3) set leverage 1x-6x on borrowed credits at 12% compounding per round. Then resolve. Two buttons matter more than everything else: SHIP banks any fraction of current equity permanently at a 15% shipping tax and it can never be lost; HOLD keeps it compounding and exposed. If equity falls below debt you are liquidated: run over, score = banked only. Run ends at round 12; score = banked + surviving equity. Start 1,000 credits, median finish ~2,400, 90th percentile ~11,000, 99th ~40,000. Per-round equity sd is ~12% unlevered, ~35% at 3x, ~68% at 6x — you choose your own variance every 40 seconds. Entry is $5 USDC into that day's contest; you may enter up to 5 runs/day.

**Depth curve.** Day 1 you are learning what a Chimney looks like from its tells. Day 10 you can price a claim from the tells alone and you have stopped over-assaying. Day 100 you are not playing the game, you are playing the payout curve: in the top-heavy GPP the correct line is to deliberately refuse to ship, run 5x into round 10, and accept a 60% chance of zero, because a median finish pays nothing there; in the Double-Up you ship early and never touch leverage. The mastered skill is variance targeting against a known field distribution — the same skill that separates DFS pros from DFS players, and it does not saturate because the field's aggregate behavior shifts weekly and the live archetype table rotates (8 of 24 active per week, parameters redrawn). Honest caveat: the *inference* layer (bandit posteriors from tells) IS solvable and a strong human will approach optimal on it in ~200 runs. What stays unsolved is the contest-relative decision, because it depends on what 300 other people did that day, which no solver can precompute.

**Skill vs chance.** A single run is roughly 65% variance / 35% skill — the correlation between true skill and single-run percentile is about 0.35. That ratio is correct here because the product is the *contest*, not the run: if a run were 90% skill the leaderboard would be a fixed ladder and everyone below the top 20 would quit in week two. High per-run variance is what lets a bad player win Tuesday, which is what makes them enter Wednesday. The design forces skill to show up in the aggregate instead. Numbers: in the Double-Up (top 45% paid ~2.09x, 6% rake), break-even is a 47.9% cash rate; a strong player cashes ~56% for +17% ROI, per-contest ROI sd ~104%, so n = (2 × 104 / 17)^2 ≈ 150 contests ≈ 20 hours of play before edge clears two standard errors. In the top-heavy GPP a strong player hits top-5% at ~14% vs 5% baseline (2.8x) but per-contest sd is ~320%, needing ~450 contests. That is why both contest types ship on day one: the Double-Up is where skill becomes legible fast, the GPP is where the money and the screenshots are.

**Why on-chain.** Weak-to-medium, and I will not oversell it. Three things genuinely break as a normal web server: (1) the RNG. Every payout is a function of a seed I generate; players with $5 on it have no reason to believe me. The commit-reveal makes every one of the day's runs recomputable by anyone from the revealed daily secret — 'check my run yourself' is an actual feature, and it is the only reason a stranger on Crypto Twitter sends a first $5. (2) Instant global sub-cent payouts to 300 people at contest close, with no payment processor that will touch a skill-contest business. (3) A bonded prize vault players can inspect, so the pool is provably not my runway. The gameplay itself does not need a chain and I would not pretend otherwise.

**Solana primitives.**
  - Per-run PDA (~400 bytes, ~0.0028 SOL rent, closed and refunded at settlement) — state sharded per player, so the 12M CU per writable account per block ceiling is never touched by gameplay
  - MagicBlock Ephemeral Rollup: delegate the RunState PDA, play all 12 rounds at ~10ms slots with 0 SOL base fee per tx, then commit + undelegate — total ~0.0004 SOL (~$0.03) per run against a $5 entry
  - Commit-reveal randomness: house posts H(s_day) before the contest opens, s_day itself anchored to an ORAO VRF draw from the previous day so it cannot be ground; per-run seed = hash(s_day, player_pubkey, entry_slot, player_nonce); per-round draw = hash(runSeed, round, actionHash). Zero per-round VRF cost, full post-hoc verifiability, 5 SOL bond slashed to the pool on non-reveal
  - Local fee markets: per-player PDAs see near-zero priority fees, so a 12-round run never competes with a memecoin launch for blockspace
  - SPL USDC vault PDA per contest, sharded every 500 entries so entry txs (~50k CU each) never saturate a single writable account
  - Solana Attestation Service for a one-human credential capping GPP entries at 5 per attested identity (post-MVP)

**Money flow.** Value enters only as USDC contest entries. It exits only as USDC prizes. 6% rake, taken off the top of the pool before payout, is the entire business — there is no token, no asset sale, no emission. At 100 entries/day: $500 pool, $30/day rake, $470 paid out, ~$900/month. At 1,000 entries/day: $9,000/month. Costs: ~$0.03/run in Solana fees (0.4% of entry, absorbed by the rake), an RPC/landing-layer bill of ~$200/month (Helius Sender or a staked connection — naive sendTransaction visibly drops entries at contest deadline and that is a refund event), and a $50/day overlay guarantee for the first 28 days (~$1,400) to make small contests worth entering. Break-even at ~170 entries/day. This is a real but small business until it isn't.

**House edge / rake.** 6% rake on the entry pool, disclosed on the contest card in plain numbers ('100 entries × $5 = $500 · house takes $30 · $470 to players'). The house never takes a position in a run — every credit paid out came from another entry, so I cannot be exploited by a strong player and I never need to ban a winner, which is the failure mode that kills every house-banked skill game. Players tolerate 6% because it is under the ~10% DFS standard and roughly half of a slot machine, because a strong player's +17% Double-Up ROI clears it by 3x, and because they can see the vault balance.

**Bots.** Day one a bot will play ASSAY, because it is a solitaire optimization problem and those get solved. I am not going to pretend a captcha stops that. What a bot extracts: near-optimal assay/allocation decisions, worth maybe +8% ROI over a good human in the Double-Up. What it does not extract: the contest-relative variance decision in the GPP, which requires modeling the live field. What actually stops damage: (a) the weekly archetype rotation forces a solver to re-derive 24 distributions from scratch every Monday, (b) rake is charged per entry so a bot farm is a paying customer, not a leak — a bot entering 500 contests pays me $150/week, (c) the real abuse is multi-accounting the top-heavy GPP, where extra entries are extra lottery tickets, so GPP entries are capped at 5 per attested identity and the Double-Up (where entry spam is EV-neutral) stays uncapped. I will publish a bot API in month three and take their rake.

**Cold start plan.** ASSAY needs zero concurrent players. It needs eight people over twenty-four hours, which is a different and much easier problem, and it is the whole reason this design exists. Week one, 8 players entering 3 runs each = 24 entries = a $120 pool. That is a real contest. To keep it from feeling thin: every contest is a GUARANTEED prize pool with dev overlay — a $150 guaranteed Double-Up with 24 entries means I eat $30, and overlay is the single most reliable acquisition tool DFS ever found because a +EV contest is worth posting about. Free practice runs with a public leaderboard run permanently alongside, so a stranger can play the whole game before funding a wallet. And the contest is 24 hours long, so a player in Manila and a player in Berlin are in the same field without ever being awake together.

**First session.** Minute 0: click a link from Crypto Twitter, land on a text/CSS page, hit 'Practice run'. No wallet, no signup — you play a full 12-round run against the previous day's revealed seed in 8 minutes. Minute 8: your run card shows you finished at the 31st percentile of yesterday's real field and names the mistake (you shipped at round 4 and had no exposure left when the Chimney hit). Minute 9: 'Enter today's $5 Double-Up' — connect Phantom or Backpack, or take the embedded wallet. Minute 10: fund. This is the real drop-off and I will not paper over it — the flow is a Coinbase/MoonPay USDC on-ramp for a $20 minimum, or a one-click Jupiter swap if SOL is already there; expect to lose half the funnel here. Minute 12: one transaction escrows $5, creates the run PDA, and delegates it to the Ephemeral Rollup. Minute 13-21: you play. Every round is a free ER transaction, so nothing costs anything and nothing drops. Minute 21: score committed to L1. Payout lands automatically at 00:00 UTC when the daily secret is revealed. Total fees paid: about 3 cents.

**Viral mechanic.** The run card, and specifically the ghost link. Every finished run produces a shareable text/emoji ledger — the banked-vs-exposed curve as a sparkline, the round you got greedy, the final percentile — plus a link that lets ANYONE replay your exact seed for free, seeing the same claims and the same tells, and find out whether they would have shipped. 'I lost 40k on round 11, here's my seed, you try it' is a post that costs a stranger nothing to accept and drops them straight into the practice funnel. Contest entry itself is a Blink for the wallet-extension crowd — a viral primitive, not an acquisition channel, and I will not budget it as one.

**Four-week MVP.** Ships: the Anchor program (contest account, per-run PDA, commit-reveal with bonded reveal, USDC vault, rank-and-payout instruction), MagicBlock ER delegation for the 12-round loop, a plain HTML/CSS/emoji client with no framework, 8 vein archetypes, both contest types (Double-Up and GPP), the practice mode against yesterday's revealed seed, and the run card. Explicitly cut: the ghost-replay link (week 5), Solana Attestation Service identity caps (week 6, uncapped GPP entries until then with a 5-per-wallet honor cap), archetype rotation (fixed table for the first month — solvers need longer than a month), seasons and leaderboards beyond a 7-day rolling table, mobile layout, and any notion of persistent progression. No accounts, no profiles, no cosmetics.

**Biggest risk.** That the solitaire loop is not fun enough to survive contact with someone who isn't paid to like it. ASSAY lives or dies on whether SHIP-vs-HOLD produces the Balatro feeling of a decision you regret in real time, and the only way to know is the free practice mode. The kill test is concrete: if median practice sessions per unique visitor is under 2.5 in week one, the loop is not fun and no rake percentage fixes that. Secondary risk: the top-heavy GPP needs ~450 contests for skill to become visible, which is long enough that good players may conclude it is a lottery and leave before the sample lands — the Double-Up is the mitigation and it needs to be the default, not the sidebar.

- **Bot extraction:** Week one, ~$1,800, of which $1,400 is the overlay budget. Mechanism: scrape the full archetype table from free practice in 10 minutes (160 parameters, 12,000 free labelled samples), then max-enter every guaranteed contest that is underfilled. Concretely at week-one volume: 8 humans × 3 entries = 24 entries = $120/day of human money. A 40-wallet operator adds 200 entries. The Double-Up now pays top 45% of 224 = 101 places; the bot's 200 entries at a ~60% cash rate claim ~120 of them, exceeding the paid slots outright. Human cash rate goes to approximately zero. Steady-state Double-Up ROI for the solver is 0.60 × 2.09 − 1 = +25.4%, or $1.27 per $5 entry; the binding constraint is not the bot's edge, it is that the pool is too small to feed it. The correct way to state the damage: within days, the bot farm IS the field, the 6% rake is paid mostly by the bot to itself, and the ~$120/day of human deposits plus the entire $1,400 overlay flows to one operator. The designer's 'a bot farm is a paying customer, not a leak' is true for the Double-Up (EV-neutral entry spam) and false for the GPP and false for guaranteed overlay, which is the acquisition tool the whole cold-start plan rests on.
- **Prior art:** Heavily precedented, twice over, and both precedents ended badly. (1) The SHIP/HOLD core is a crash game — bustabit shipped it in 2014 with 99% RTP and HMAC-SHA256 commit-reveal, and there are multiple live Solana clones (solanacrash.io, solanacrash.com, open-source Anchor implementations on GitHub) plus crash-tournament leaderboards as an established format. The 'provably fair, check the seed yourself' pitch is a 12-year-old commodity in this exact audience, not a differentiator that earns a first $5. (2) The contest wrapper is DFS — GPP/double-up/overlay are DraftKings-FanDuel primitives with a documented outcome: ~1.3% of players took 91% of profits, requiring permanent recreational inflow. (3) The direct crypto instance is Fantasy.top: daily top-heavy contests aimed at exactly the Crypto Twitter audience this design targets, peaked May 2024 at 21,500 users and $924k/day in fees, then 93% revenue decline and 80% user decline, announced shutdown for end of June 2026 with the team stating publicly the model 'was never built for crypto.' It paid out $20M+ and still could not retain. That is three months old and it is the closest comparable that exists.
- **Suggested fix:** Four changes, in order. (1) Kill the house-held daily secret. Request a per-run VRF at run start (Switchboard On-Demand or ORAO), or generate the run seed inside a MagicBlock Private ER so neither the operator nor the player can see it. This costs one callback and 1-2 slots of latency per run and it is the only version where the on-chain pitch is true. (2) Fix the two dead buttons: make the SHIP tax decay with round (25% at r1 → 5% at r12) so shipping late is a real option, and set the borrow rate BELOW the arithmetic mean archetype return by a published margin so leverage has a genuine interior Kelly optimum instead of being a knife-edge on an undisclosed table. Publish the archetype table — the solvers get it free anyway, and publishing converts a secret into a design constraint you are forced to balance. (3) Replace flat overlay with rake-funded overlay capped at 40% of that contest's collected rake, and enforce per-identity entry caps at day one, not week six — SAS is a week of work and it is load-bearing, not a nice-to-have. (4) Rate-limit practice to N runs per IP/day against the PREVIOUS day's seed only, which is already the design, but make the returned data show outcomes, not archetype labels.

## ATTRITION
*Lens: variance-strategy*

**Pitch.** You write sixteen if-then rules for a warlord house, then go to bed; four times a day your doctrine fights three others in a 120-turn war simulation, and you can bet on it — or on someone else's.

**Core loop.** There is no real-time loop; the loop is authoring and reading. A doctrine is an ordered list of up to 16 rules, each rule = (condition, action, magnitude) drawn from a fixed vocabulary of 24 predicates and 12 verbs — 'IF my_levies > 1.4 × strongest_neighbor THEN raid magnitude 70', 'IF turn > 80 AND holdings < 5 THEN offer_alliance magnitude 100'. Sixteen rules × 4 bytes = 64 bytes, fits in one transaction with 1,168 bytes to spare. The simulation: 4 houses, a radius-2 hex map (19 hexes), 120 turns. Income scales with hexes held and fortification level; levies cost upkeep; combat resolves stochastically — attacker wins with p = A/(A+D) softened toward 0.5, and casualties are drawn from a heavy-tailed distribution, so a 60/40 attack lost at turn 55 is frequently a run-ender. Alliances are a two-step mechanical protocol (offer, accept) with a betrayal action available every turn, which makes the alliance predicates the highest-skill part of the vocabulary. Entry $3 USDC, tables of 4, brackets fire every 6 hours. $12 pot, 8% rake, $11.04 paid: 1st $8.28, 2nd $2.76. Sessions are 3 minutes of doctrine editing and 90 seconds of reading a replay, four times a day if you want, or once a week if you don't.

**Depth curve.** This is the concept whose day-100 answer is strongest, because the content is the population. Day 1 you fork a template doctrine and change two numbers. Day 10 you notice 70% of the field opens with a turn-15 to turn-25 rush, so you write a doctrine that fortifies through turn 30 and counter-punches into their spent levies, and you start winning. Day 25 the field has turtled in response and rushing is +EV again. Day 40 you are reading the published replay archive — every simulation is on-chain and every doctrine that placed is public — and computing what the current field actually does at turn 40, then building the counter. Day 100 you maintain a portfolio of four doctrines across four entries per bracket, hedged against different field compositions, and you can name what won last Tuesday and why. It is rock-paper-scissors at population scale with a full data trail, which is the deepest and most durable retention structure in competitive gaming (see: any card game metagame ever). The weekly rotation of 6 of the 24 predicates guarantees the meta cannot ossify. And crucially, you accumulate competitive sample while asleep — 4 brackets/day × 4 entries = 16 tables/day of results without sitting at a screen.

**Skill vs chance.** A single 4-house table is about 60% variance / 40% skill: the combat draws genuinely decide games, and a top-decile doctrine loses to a template doctrine roughly a third of the time. That ratio is correct because a doctrine is a fixed strategy — with low variance, the single best doctrine would win nearly always, the field would converge on it in a week, and the game would be dead. High combat variance is what keeps a 30%-win-rate doctrine viable long enough for its author to iterate, and it is what makes the parimutuel betting layer interesting rather than a formality. Numbers: a top-decile doctrine finishes 1st ~38% (vs 25% baseline) and 2nd ~30%, giving EV = 0.38 × $8.28 + 0.30 × $2.76 = $3.98 on a $3 entry, +32% ROI against a field average of -8%. Per-table ROI sd ≈ 110%. n = (2 × 110 / 32)^2 ≈ 47 tables — and because the sim runs autonomously at 4 brackets a day with up to 4 entries each, a committed player clears 47 tables in about 3 days of wall-clock time without playing a single minute. That is the fastest skill-resolution-per-hour-of-attention of anything in this document.

**Why on-chain.** Two real things and one honest concession. Real thing one: the simulation is deterministic given (seed, 4 doctrines), the seed comes from a committed Switchboard On-Demand draw, and both are on chain — so anyone can re-execute a table and confirm the result. When your money rides on a war you did not watch, resolved by dice you did not roll, 'you can check the RNG' is not a feature, it is the entire basis for participating. Real thing two: the parimutuel side pool is a prediction market on a verifiable outcome, which is the single category with unambiguous product-market fit on this chain, and it works precisely because the operator cannot both run the sim and take the losing side. The concession: yes, I could build this as a Postgres app. Nobody would put $3 into it, because a server that runs the simulation, holds the escrow, and books the bets is asking for a level of trust that no solo developer has ever been granted.

**Solana primitives.**
  - 64-byte doctrine PDA per author per bracket — trivially inside the 1,232-byte transaction limit, and per-player PDAs mean near-zero priority fees via local fee markets
  - Deterministic sim executed inside a MagicBlock ER: 120 turns × 4 houses at 0 SOL base fee per tx and ~10ms slots resolves a full table in ~2 seconds; batching turn steps across ER txs keeps every instruction under the 200k CU default and well inside the 1.4M CU per-transaction cap
  - Per-table PDA sharding: a single global bracket account would hit the 12M CU per writable account per block ceiling at roughly 40 concurrent tables — the bracket is a list of independent table PDAs, written in parallel
  - Commit-reveal for map generation and combat rolls, with the bracket seed anchored to one Switchboard On-Demand VRF draw per 6-hour bracket (one callback, ~1-2 slots latency, negligible cost amortized over 100+ tables)
  - State compression / Bubblegum concurrent merkle tree for the replay archive — a year of 120-turn replays across thousands of tables for a few dollars, which is what makes the metagame data trail affordable
  - SPL USDC vault PDAs: one per table for entries, one per bracket for the parimutuel side pool
  - Solana Attestation Service for a portable doctrine-author ELO credential (post-MVP), which is the composability hook — a third party can gate on 'has beaten 1600 in ATTRITION'
  - Match/table accounts sized to be closed and rent-refunded at settlement (~6,960 lamports/byte recovered)

**Money flow.** Two USDC streams, no token, nothing sold. Entries: 8% rake on $3 tables. Parimutuel side pool: 5% rake on wagers backing houses before a bracket fires. Week one at 8 players × 4 brackets × 1 entry: $96/day in entries = $7.68 rake, side pool at ~3x entry volume = $288 × 5% = $14.40, total ~$22/day. At 400 doctrine-entries/day plus a proportional side pool: ~$1,100/day. The side pool is the actual business — it scales with audience rather than with players, which is the only revenue line here that does not require growing the hard-to-grow number. Costs: one Switchboard VRF callback per bracket (4/day, cents), ~0.0004 SOL per table in ER session+commit fees, RPC ~$200/month, and $0 in prize subsidy because NPC houses forfeit their share back into the pot rather than being funded.

**House edge / rake.** 8% on entry pots, 5% on the parimutuel side pool, both printed on the bracket card as arithmetic. The house takes no side of any bet — parimutuel means backers are paid from other backers, which is why this layer is safe for a solo developer to operate with no bankroll at all and no possibility of a house loss. 8% on entries is high relative to poker but the entry buys an autonomous unit that plays four times a day; a top-decile author's +32% clears it fourfold. 5% parimutuel is under the ~15-20% takeout of horse racing, which is the comparison the audience will actually make, and the pool sizes and payouts are visible on chain before you commit.

**Bots.** The bot problem inverts completely: in ATTRITION, the bot IS the player. What you submit is a program. Automation of doctrine-authoring — someone running a genetic algorithm over the rule space against a local copy of the simulator — is not cheating, it is the highest form of play, and I will ship the simulator as a wasm module in week five specifically so people can do it. That is the good news. The bad news is collusion, and it is the one real attack: one person entering two doctrines on the same table could ally-then-never-betray to hand a win to one of them, converting $6 of entries into an $8.28 return. Three mechanical defenses, all of which change what the game IS: (1) table assignment is randomized from the global entry pool at bracket-fire time by the committed seed, so you cannot choose your table, (2) doctrines cannot identify each other — the predicate vocabulary can only reference observable board state (holdings, levies, fortification, turn), never a house identity or author, so a colluding pair cannot recognize each other reliably, (3) an author's multiple entries in one bracket are hard-partitioned across tables. Entry spam is otherwise harmless here, unlike a top-heavy contest: every entry pays its own rake into a 4-player table and has no lottery-ticket externality.

**Cold start plan.** Best of the three, and it is a design property rather than a growth tactic. A player's presence in this game is a 64-byte doctrine, not a body in a chair — so I need 4 doctrines per table, not 4 humans awake at once. Twelve open-source reference doctrines ship as NPC houses (Turtle, Rusher, Opportunist, Kingmaker, and nine more), they fill any table short of four entries, they are staked from the pot rather than from a bankroll, and if an NPC house wins, its share returns to the prize pool and the human placements shift up. So a bracket with ONE human entry is a valid, fully-populated, fully-paying 4-house war. With 8 players in week one entering 2 doctrines each into 4 brackets a day, that is 64 doctrine-entries/day across 16 tables of real competition, plus a replay archive accumulating from day one that gives the metagame something to eat. The reference doctrines being open-source is also the onboarding path — everyone starts by forking Turtle and changing one number.

**First session.** Minute 0: land on a text page showing last bracket's replay as a scrolling turn-by-turn battle report with hex maps drawn in CSS grid and unicode. Minute 1: 'Fork this doctrine' on the winning entry — the editor opens with its 16 rules in plain English dropdowns. No wallet yet. Minute 2-6: you change three rules and hit 'Test' — the simulator runs in your browser as wasm (the exact same Rust crate compiled for BPF, so the local result is bit-identical to the on-chain result), 200 tables against the 12 reference doctrines in under two seconds, and you see your win rate. This is the hook and it is completely free. Minute 7: you iterate twice more and get to 31%. Minute 9: 'Enter next bracket, $3' — connect Phantom or Backpack, fund USDC (the honest funnel-killer; $20 minimum on-ramp or a Jupiter swap). Minute 11: one transaction writes your 64-byte doctrine PDA and escrows $3. Minute 12: you close the tab. Up to 6 hours later the bracket fires, your table resolves in about 2 seconds inside the ER, and USDC settles to your wallet on L1 with a push link to the replay. Total on-chain cost to you: about 3 cents.

**Viral mechanic.** The battle report, and the fork button. Every table produces a shareable turn-by-turn text report with the pivotal moment auto-detected and headlined — 'TURN 74: House Ash broke a 40-turn alliance and took three hexes in one turn' — with named houses and CSS hex maps that read fine in a screenshot. Betrayals are inherently narrative and they cost me no art budget. Underneath every report is 'Fork the winner', one click into the browser simulator with a stranger's doctrine loaded and no wallet required, which is the single cheapest possible funnel entry: you arrive to gloat at someone's loss and leave having tested a build. The parimutuel layer is the second engine — backing a house is a Blink, so 'I'm on House Ash at 3.2x, bracket fires in 40 minutes' is a postable position, and positions are what a trading population actually shares.

**Four-week MVP.** Ships: the deterministic simulator as one Rust crate compiled to both BPF and wasm (this is the highest-leverage decision in the whole build — identical local and on-chain results, and the browser tester is free), 19-hex map, 120 turns, 24 predicates, 12 verbs including the alliance/betrayal protocol, 12 open-source reference doctrines, the Anchor program (doctrine PDA, bracket, table sharding, committed-seed randomness, USDC escrow, settle-and-pay), MagicBlock ER execution of tables, a dropdown doctrine editor and CSS-grid replay viewer, 4 brackets/day. Explicitly cut: the parimutuel side pool (week 6 — it is the business but it is worth nothing at 8 players, and shipping it into an empty book looks worse than not having it), multi-round elimination brackets (flat single-table brackets only), Solana Attestation Service ELO credentials, compressed replay archival (last 48 hours in plain accounts, compression in week 7), predicate rotation (fixed vocabulary for month one), and any map larger than radius 2. Weeks 1-2 are the simulator and its balance, not the chain — the chain part is a week.

**Biggest risk.** Balance. One developer tuning a 4-player war simulation in two weeks will ship a dominant doctrine without knowing it, and the field will find it in 72 hours, at which point every table is a mirror match and the game is over. This is a much sharper version of the risk in the other two concepts because the strategy space is finite and fully enumerable — 24 predicates × 12 verbs × 16 slots is large but a genetic algorithm over a wasm simulator eats it. Mitigations, in order of importance: (1) run a free play-chip ladder for two full weeks before any money touches it, and treat any doctrine exceeding a 45% win rate as a balance bug rather than a great player, (2) ship the wasm simulator publicly on day one and explicitly ask people to break it — the solvers are free QA, (3) reserve the weekly predicate rotation as a live balance lever that does not require redeploying the program. Second risk, smaller but real: 'write 16 rules' is a programmer's idea of accessible, and the addressable audience for a doctrine editor may be a few thousand people worldwide. That is survivable for a solo dev's revenue target and fatal for anything larger.

### Hostile review

- **Verdict:** buildable-with-caveats
- **Solo-dev estimate:** 13 weeks
- **Degenerate strategy:** Genetic-algorithm best-response against a fully public field, converging rather than cycling. The design publishes (a) a bit-identical simulator, (b) every placing doctrine, (c) the full replay archive. That is a complete, machine-readable, zero-noise specification of the population's strategy plus a perfect response oracle. Sim cost: 120 turns × 4 houses of integer arithmetic ≈ 20μs/table native, so one core does 50,000 tables/sec. A 64-core spot box for one week = 1.9 × 10^12 table-evaluations for ~$500. A GA with population 10,000 × 10,000 generations × 1,000 evaluations per candidate = 10^11 — comfortably inside that budget with 19x to spare. Card-game metagames stay alive because decklists are partially hidden, the game is not runnable offline at 10^11 games/week, and human pilots introduce execution variance. Here there is no pilot, the field is published, and the response function is computable. It converges. The designer's top-listed balance mitigation — 'ship the wasm simulator on day one and ask people to break it' — is the mechanism that hands the solver its oracle, and it is the correct decision for balance QA and the fatal one for competitive longevity. Secondary degeneracy, smaller: the winner-take-all payout means the answer is always 'rush at the timing that beats the current modal rush,' a one-dimensional search.
- **Technical blockers:**
  - Bit-identical wasm/BPF determinism is achievable but the plan has not named the constraint that makes it hard: no floating point anywhere. 'Attacker wins with p = A/(A+D) softened toward 0.5' and 'casualties drawn from a heavy-tailed distribution' are both float-shaped specifications. Solana BPF has no hardware FP; emulated float behaviour has historically differed from native and is expensive in CU. Every probability, softening curve, and casualty draw must be fixed-point integer math with explicit wrapping semantics, and HashMap iteration order must never touch simulation state. A single f32 in the combat softening function silently desyncs the browser tester from the chain — which is not a bug report, it is a payout dispute with someone who tested 200 tables and got a different answer.
  - CU budget is tight but fits, and the design's own estimate is far too conservative. 120 turns × 4 houses × 16 rules = 7,680 rule evaluations per table at ~60 CU each (predicate lookup, compare, branch) = ~460k CU, plus ~100 combat resolutions at ~500 CU (hash draw + fixed-point division) = 50k, plus hex adjacency on 19 hexes and pairwise alliance state (4 houses = 6 pairs) = maybe 100k. Total ~600-900k CU, which fits in ONE 1.4M-CU transaction. So 'resolves a full table in ~2 seconds inside the ER' is off by three orders of magnitude — it is one instruction. Good news, but it means the ER is barely needed for the sim itself; you could run it in a single L1 tx. That undercuts a chunk of the solanaPrimitives narrative and is worth knowing before spending a week on ER integration you may not need.
  - One Switchboard On-Demand VRF callback per 6-hour bracket is a single point of failure for every table in that bracket plus all escrowed USDC. Callback fails or arrives late → bracket stalls with player money locked. There is no timeout/refund/fallback-seed path in the plan. Four brackets/day means ~1,460 callbacks/year; at three-nines that is one stalled bracket per year with every entrant's $3 frozen and no instruction to unfreeze it. Budget the refund path.
  - 64-byte doctrine is correct and comfortable (1,168 bytes of headroom in a 1,232-byte tx). Per-table PDA closure and rent refund is correct. Bubblegum replay archival is the right primitive and correctly deferred. These parts are genuinely well-specified — this concept has the cleanest chain design of the three.
  - The doctrine editor is deceptively large: 24 predicates × 12 verbs × magnitudes, ordered 16-deep, with an alliance protocol, rendered as plain-English dropdowns that must be unambiguous to a non-programmer. That is 1.5 weeks of UI, not a weekend, and it is the entire onboarding surface.
- **Economic holes:**
  - THE PAYOUT CURVE MAKES SECOND PLACE A LOSS AND THEREFORE DESTROYS THE METAGAME. Entry $3, 1st = $8.28, 2nd = $2.76. Second place pays LESS than the entry fee. So finishing 2nd is worth −$0.24, identical to finishing 4th. Only 1st is profitable, which means the payout is effectively winner-take-all and every rational doctrine must maximise P(1st), not survival. The 'day 10 you notice the field rushes, so you fortify through turn 30 and counter-punch' story requires turtling to be rewarded — but turtling into 2nd loses money. Rock-paper-scissors needs three archetypes with a payoff cycle; a winner-take-most payout collapses that to a single axis (aggression timing) with a scalar parameter, and scalar parameters get grid-searched. The metagame the entire depth curve rests on cannot exist under these numbers.
  - NPC-FILLED TABLES MATHEMATICALLY GUARANTEE THE HUMAN LOSES. NPC houses are 'staked from the pot rather than from a bankroll.' A 1-human/3-NPC table has a pot of $3 (only the human paid), rake 8% = $0.24, payout pool $2.76. The human's BEST possible outcome is $2.76 on a $3 entry: −8%, guaranteed, regardless of skill. The bracket card advertises $8.28 for 1st; on any table containing an NPC that number is not payable. Two humans + two NPCs: pot $6, pool $5.52, humans collectively lose 8% no matter what. So the cold-start mechanism — the concept's proudest structural property — is a machine that converts new-player deposits into rake at a 100% loss rate until tables are 4-human. This must be fixed before money touches it: either fund NPC seats from a real float, or scale the advertised prize to actual entries and say so on the card.
  - The parimutuel side pool — explicitly 'the actual business' — is a bet against a party with strictly superior information, and there is no mechanism against it. A doctrine author knows their own doctrine's true win rate to ±0.5% by running 10^6 local sims; the backing public prices a brand-new doctrine at the 25% baseline. Deploy a doctrine with true P(1st)=0.55, get ~4x odds, EV = 0.55 × 4 − 1 = +120% per unit staked, repeatable on every new deployment, capped only by pool depth. Parimutuel is 'safe for the operator' in the sense that the house cannot lose — which is true and irrelevant, because the losses land entirely on the recreational backers who are the only reason the side pool exists. It is a first-time-out horse with a stopwatch in the owner's pocket, forever.
  - Median-human ROI is brutal once the field contains one solver. If the solver holds P(1st)=0.55, everyone else averages P(1st)≈0.15-0.20, giving EV = 0.20 × 8.28 + 0.28 × 2.76 − 3 = −$0.57 = −19% ROI per table. That is worse than a slot machine, it is structural rather than a tuning artefact, and it is fully visible to any player who tracks 50 tables — which the design makes trivially easy by publishing everything.
  - Revenue at scale requires an audience that does not exist. The $1,100/day figure only works at ~400 entries PER BRACKET = 1,600 doctrine-entries/day = ~400 daily active paying authors of a rule-scripting game. Battlecode, the largest and longest-running competition of this exact shape, draws a few hundred TEAMS PER YEAR. The design's own secondary risk — 'the addressable audience for a doctrine editor may be a few thousand people worldwide' — is correct and is probably generous, and 400 concurrent paying authors would be a meaningful fraction of the global population for this hobby.
- **Cold start:** ALIVE — decisively the best of the three, and it is a genuine design property rather than a growth tactic. A player's presence is a 64-byte doctrine, not a body in a chair, so brackets need 4 doctrines, not 4 humans awake at once. At exactly 8 players entering 2 doctrines each into 4 brackets/day: 16 doctrines per bracket = 4 fully-human tables per bracket = 64 doctrine-entries and 16 tables of real competition daily, with a replay archive accumulating from day one for the metagame to eat. That is a functioning competitive game at 8 users, which is a genuinely rare and valuable property and the single strongest thing in this document. The caveat is the one above: BELOW that threshold, NPC-filled tables cap the human at −8% by construction, so the range 1-7 players is a trap that looks like a game. Fix the NPC funding and this concept's cold start is solved in a way the other two are not.
- **Bot extraction:** Week one: a ~$500 compute spend converts to roughly 100% of the human entry pool, ~$180/day at the design's own week-one volume, plus effectively unlimited take from the parimutuel once it ships. Mechanism: one week on a 64-core box runs 10^11 simulated tables against the 12 published reference doctrines and every published human doctrine, producing a near-best-response. Assume P(1st)=0.55 (conservative against forked templates and open-source NPCs; 0.75+ against 3 reference NPCs). EV per entry = 0.55 × 8.28 + 0.25 × 2.76 − 3 = +$2.24 on a $3 entry = +75% ROI. Entries are limited only by hard-partitioning across tables within a bracket, and identities are free wallets: 16 entries/day/identity × 50 identities × $2.24 = $1,800/day of theoretical extraction against a pool of $192/day, so the binding constraint is again that the bot becomes the field. The real money is the side pool: a solver that has simulated its own doctrine 10^6 times knows its win probability to three decimals while the market prices it at the 25% baseline, giving ~+120% per new deployment. The design's framing — 'the bot IS the player, and that is the highest form of play' — is philosophically defensible and commercially fatal the moment there is a prize pool, because the humans forking Turtle and changing one number are the liquidity.
- **Prior art:** Two lineages, both instructive, both ending in the same place. (1) Programming-game competitions: Battlecode (MIT, running since 2000, autonomous players, cash prizes, final tournament), Screeps/Screeps Arena (Steam, JS and wasm), Gladiabots (rule-based bot squads, online ranked and tournaments — the closest mechanical match to a 16-rule doctrine editor), Bot Land, CodinGame. Consistent documented outcomes: small dedicated audience measured in hundreds of teams, complete dominance by the top handful of solvers, and — critically — none of them ever converted spectating into revenue. (2) The money analogue: Zed Run, autonomous simulated racing with betting, processed $400M+ in horse transactions and bred 420,000 horses at peak, was discontinued in favour of Zed Champions (April 2025), whose final season ended 24 May 2026, after which the IP was rebooted inside the online casino Clutch as continuous racing with live betting and pack openings. That is the precise arc this design risks: once the sim's skill layer is solved, only the gambling layer retains value, and you end up operating a casino with extra steps. Note the counter-example the grounded facts cite as a success, Photo Finish LIVE ($9.7M in track earnings), works on the OPPOSITE principle — the skill sits in breeding and training and the race resolution is deliberately opaque, so there is no offline oracle to grind against.
- **Suggested fix:** Four changes, and the first two are non-negotiable before money. (1) Flatten the payout so more than one place is profitable: 1st 50% / 2nd 30% / 3rd 20% of the $11.04 pool = $5.52 / $3.31 / $2.21, making 2nd clearly profitable and 3rd nearly break-even. This is what restores three viable archetypes and therefore restores the entire rock-paper-scissors depth claim; under the current curve the metagame is arithmetically impossible. (2) Fund NPC seats from a rake-financed float so a 1-human table pays a real 4-way prize, or refuse to fire tables with fewer than 4 human entries and run brackets less often (2/day instead of 4/day) so they fill. A guaranteed −8% ceiling for the newest players is the worst possible first-session outcome and it is invisible to them. (3) Delay the parimutuel until doctrines are commit-revealed and betting resolves on author-ELO rather than on unseen new doctrines, or take the side pool off the table entirely — it is described as the business, and as designed it is a transfer from recreational backers to whoever owns the doctrine, which will end the product's reputation faster than any balance bug. (4) Treat the wasm simulator as adversarial infrastructure from day one: run a public solver bounty during the free play-chip ladder, pay for exploits in cash, and reserve the weekly predicate rotation as a live balance lever — but understand that rotation buys weeks, not permanence, against a GA that re-solves in one overnight run. Weeks 1-2 being simulator-and-balance rather than chain is exactly the right call; budget 4-6 weeks for it, not 2.

## TAPE
*Lens: variance-strategy*

**Pitch.** Six people trade one made-up asset for eight minutes; each of you privately knows a little about where it's going, everyone can see everyone's position but not their reason, and you can borrow eight times your money until it kills you.

**Core loop.** 96 ticks of 5 seconds each = 8 minutes. One synthetic asset, price starts at 1,000. The price is a hidden 3-state Markov drift (bull +0.40%/tick, flat 0%, bear -0.40%/tick, 4% transition probability per tick) plus N(0, 0.6%) noise plus market impact: net order flow of 1,000 notional moves price 0.05%. Every 8 ticks each seat privately receives a read on the CURRENT regime, correct with p=0.72, drawn independently per player. So six people hold six independent 72% reads, and the aggregate of five other reads is worth far more than your own — but the only way to observe someone's read is to watch them trade on it, and the only way to trade on yours is to leak it. That is the entire game, and it is the Kyle/Glosten-Milgrom information structure, which is the same reason poker is deep. Every seat starts with 10,000 equity, can lever to 8x, maintenance margin 12%, per-tick order cap 25% of equity in notional. Liquidation is automatic and forced-sells into the book, which moves price further against you and directly into whoever is positioned opposite — so hunting a visibly over-levered neighbor is a real and correct strategy, and everyone's position size is public. At tick 96 everything marks to final price. Buy-in $10 USDC, six seats, $60 pot, 4% rake, payout 60/30/10 of the $57.60. Bust early and you are ranked by bust order.

**Depth curve.** Day 1 you trade your own signal and get liquidated by tick 40. Day 5 you learn that leverage is a timer, not a multiplier. Day 15 you start reading flow — you notice seat 3 bought into a falling tape and you update harder on their conviction than on your own read. Day 30 you learn to lie: you buy small and visibly into your own bear read to induce two seats to lean long, then reverse into their liquidation cascade. Day 60 you are managing which of six seats you can actually read and folding the games where you can't. Day 100 you are running opponent-specific exploits, sizing by table composition, and moving between the $2 / $10 / $50 tiers on bankroll rules. This is poker's depth curve and I am claiming it on purpose: the information structure — public actions, private signals, endogenous prices — is provably rich, and nobody has solved it in the forty years economists have had it. There is no content to run out of because the content is the other five people.

**Skill vs chance.** About 55% chance / 45% skill in a single game. The regime chain can simply go against you for 96 straight ticks with a correct read, and a strong player will be liquidated by a coin flip several times a session. That ratio is right because leverage is the mechanic and leverage means ruin, and ruin has to be real or the fear that drives the whole thing evaporates — a version where good reads always pay is a spreadsheet, not a market. Numbers: a strong player runs +22% ROI per game against a field average of -4% (the rake), with per-game ROI sd ~145%. n = (2 × 145 / 22)^2 ≈ 174 games to clear two standard errors, which at 8 minutes a game is about 23 hours of play — the fastest skill-resolution of the three concepts, because you get 96 consequential decisions per session instead of 12. Heads-up (2 seats, $10 each, winner takes 96%) resolves even faster: edge +28%, sd ~110%, n ≈ 62 games.

**Why on-chain.** This is the strongest of the three and it is not a fig leaf. The game requires per-player hidden information — six different private regime reads — with real money on them. Off-chain that means a server that generates the signals, runs the price process, AND holds the escrow, and there is no reason on earth for a stranger to believe that server didn't feed the house seat a better read. MagicBlock **Private Ephemeral Rollups are TEE-backed**, which means the signal distribution and the price process execute inside an attested enclave: players get genuinely hidden information with a settlement anyone can verify afterward, and I never see the signals either. That is a capability that did not exist for a solo developer two years ago and it is the specific thing that makes a trust-heavy hidden-information wagering game shippable by one person. Add instant sub-cent global payout on a 6-way pot and the case is complete.

**Solana primitives.**
  - MagicBlock Private ER (TEE-backed) holding the hidden Markov regime, the per-seat signal draws, and the order book — genuinely hidden state, not obfuscated state
  - MagicBlock ER ~10ms slots: 5-second ticks with sub-tick order submission, 0 SOL base fee per tx so a player can send 200 orders in a game for nothing; the 64 KB ER transaction limit means the whole book fits in one instruction
  - Per-match PDA holds all six seats' state — sharding per match is mandatory, since a single global market account would hit the 12M CU per writable account per block ceiling at about 60 concurrent matches
  - Delegation program: seats delegate their escrow-bearing PDA at join, undelegate at settlement; ~0.0003 SOL session + 0.0001 SOL commit ≈ $0.03 per six-player match, ~0.05% of the pot
  - Jito bundles (up to 5 txs, atomic, same slot) for join+escrow+delegate so a seat is never half-registered when a table fills
  - SPL USDC escrow PDA per table; 5,000 lamport base fee per signature is the only L1 cost a player sees
  - Local fee markets: per-seat PDAs see near-zero priority fees even when the network is busy — critical, because a dropped order at tick 70 is a refund ticket

**Money flow.** USDC in as buy-ins, USDC out as prizes, 4% rake, no token, no asset. $10 table: $60 pot, $2.40 to me, $57.60 to players. 200 matches/day = $480/day = $14.4k/month, but that requires ~1,200 player-sessions/day and I should be honest that at 8 players playing 6 matches each I get 8 matches/day and $19/day. The economics of this concept are entirely a function of concurrency, which is also its biggest risk. Costs: ~$0.03/match on-chain, a Private ER is priced the same as a standard ER at these volumes, RPC/landing layer ~$200/month, plus the NPC market-maker bankroll — 2-4 algorithmic seats staked from a $2,000 dev float. Those seats are designed to be edge-zero, but 'designed to be' is not 'is', and I should budget for bleeding $500 of that float while tuning them.

**House edge / rake.** 4% of the pot, taken at settlement, disclosed on the table card. The house takes no position against players in the money game — the NPC market-maker seats are separately funded, publish their exact strategy and parameters, and pay no rake and collect none. 4% on an 8-minute six-way game is roughly 0.5% per player-minute, well under a poker room's effective rake and an order of magnitude under a slot. Players tolerate it because the alternative is no game, because a strong player's +22% clears it six times over, and because they can read the settlement math on chain. Rake is capped at $4 per table so the $50 tier is 1.6%, which is what keeps serious players in the top tier rather than driving them to a rakeless private arrangement.

**Bots.** Day one someone writes a bot that scalps the impact function, and by week two it will beat median humans. I am not going to fight this, because the fight is unwinnable and because bots pay rake. What I do instead is remove the axes where a bot's advantage is uninteresting: the 25%-of-equity per-tick order cap kills size-based steamrolling, the 5-second tick with 10ms ER slots means a human has 500 slots of headroom to act so latency is not an edge, and impact is stochastic (±20% around the stated coefficient) so pure arbitrage of the price function has real variance. What is left for a bot is inference — modeling five opponents' signal-conditional behavior — which is genuinely hard and is also exactly what a strong human is doing. The market-maker seats are house bots by design, so the game already assumes machine participants. Structural answer: three stake tiers, a published bot API at the $50 tier, and honesty in the UI that the top tier is a solver arena. The failure mode I actually guard against is one operator seating four of six seats and colluding on flow to farm the other two — countered by randomized seat assignment from a global pool at table-fill time, never letting a player choose a table, and a per-wallet seat cap of one per table.

**Cold start plan.** Concurrency is this design's only real enemy and it gets three structural answers, none of them marketing. First: **heads-up is a first-class mode, not a degenerate one.** Two players, $10 each, winner takes 96%. With 8 players online you always have four heads-up tables, and heads-up is arguably the deepest form because your entire read is one opponent's flow. Second: **house-seated NPC market makers fill empty chairs.** They run a published mean-reversion strategy with published parameters, staked from a $2,000 dev float; because their strategy is public they are exploitable and therefore edge-negative-to-zero, and they collect no rake. A two-human table with four NPC seats has real depth, real impact, and real liquidations — the market does not feel empty at 8 players, which is the failure that kills every solo-dev multiplayer game. Third: **scheduled bells.** Tables at 12:00 and 20:00 UTC with escrow-to-register and full refund on no-show. Eight people who all show up at 20:00 UTC is a full six-seat table plus a heads-up. Coordinated concurrency beats hoped-for concurrency.

**First session.** Minute 0: land from a Crypto Twitter post on a monospace-and-CSS terminal UI. 'Sit at a free table' — no wallet needed, you're seated with five NPCs and play-chips. Minute 1-9: full 8-minute game, free, with a persistent right-hand panel that explains exactly two things as they happen — your maintenance margin and what a liquidation cascade looks like. Minute 9: you were liquidated at tick 62 and the panel shows you were levered 6x on a correct read that arrived one regime-change too late. Minute 10: 'Sit at $2' — connect Phantom or Backpack. Minute 11: funding, and this is the honest drop-off point — USDC via an on-ramp with a $20 minimum, or a Jupiter swap. Minute 13: one Jito bundle registers, escrows $2, and delegates your seat PDA into the Private ER, atomically. Minute 13-14: queue for a table (heads-up fills instantly, six-seat waits up to 90 seconds or fills with NPCs). Minute 14-22: you play, every order free and instant at 10ms ER slots. Minute 22: settlement commits to L1, USDC hits your wallet within a slot or two. About 3 cents in fees.

**Viral mechanic.** The liquidation cascade replay. When someone gets blown up, the last 20 ticks render as a 6-second animated ASCII tape — price, the victim's position bleeding out, and the seat that was leaning on them — with a permalink that anyone can watch without a wallet. 'seat 4 held 7x through three regime flips' is a clip, and clips are how a trading-population audience discovers anything. Second-order: a live public feed of the biggest open positions across all tables right now, which is a page people idle on, and every position on it links to the table it's on. Table invites are Blinks, which is a decent viral primitive for the wallet-extension crowd and not an acquisition channel.

**Four-week MVP.** Ships: Anchor program (table account, seat PDAs, USDC escrow, settle-and-rank), the price engine and signal distribution running inside a MagicBlock Private ER, the 5-second tick loop, market/limit orders with the 25% cap, leverage to 8x with automatic liquidation, heads-up and six-seat modes, two published NPC market makers, a monospace CSS client with an ASCII tape chart, and free play-chip tables. Explicitly cut: order book depth beyond a single mid-price with impact (no real limit book in v1 — market orders and impact only; the limit book is week 6), multiple assets, the $50 tier and bot API, the public open-position feed, the liquidation replay clip (week 5, and it hurts to cut because it's the viral engine), tournaments, and anything resembling a profile or progression system. If the Private ER integration eats more than 8 days, v1 ships with signals in a standard ER and a public disclosure that signals are not yet TEE-hidden — the game still works, it just requires trusting me, and the free tables don't care.

**Biggest risk.** Concurrency, and it is not close. TAPE is the only one of these three that needs bodies in chairs at the same moment, and the NPC market makers are load-bearing for a problem that has killed more solo-dev multiplayer games than every other cause combined. If the NPC seats feel like playing against a wall — if humans can tell within two games that four of six seats are dumb — the cold-start solution collapses and the game needs 30 concurrent players it will not have. Secondary and nearly as bad: the NPC bankroll is a house position, and a house position is exactly the thing I said I would never take; a mispriced market maker bleeds the $2,000 float in a weekend and I have to either stop seating them (killing cold start) or subsidize them (a running cost that scales with volume). The kill test: by end of week 2, do two-human-four-NPC tables show the same liquidation rate and price-path entropy as six-human tables? If not, cut to heads-up-only and accept a smaller game.

### Hostile review

- **Verdict:** needs-redesign
- **Solo-dev estimate:** 14 weeks
- **Degenerate strategy:** Two, stacked, and the second is worse. (1) THE CLOSING RAMP. 'At tick 96 everything marks to final price' plus self-marking permanent impact means the last three ticks are free money: hold 80,000 notional at 8x, push 2,500 notional in your own direction on ticks 94/95/96, move the mark 0.125% per tick, harvest 80,000 × 0.375% = $300 = 3% of equity, risk-free at the buzzer. Everyone discovers this in game two, so the close becomes a pure flow war won by whoever has the biggest position — the game resolves on capital size, not information. Real exchanges use randomised closing auctions specifically to kill this. (2) NEVER BE THE MOST-LEVERED SEAT. Positions, equity, and the 12% maintenance margin are all public, so every seat's liquidation price is a publicly computable number. Five seats pushing 2,500 notional each = 12,500 = 0.625%/tick. A 7x seat has only 2.29% of adverse room (equity 10,000, notional 70,000, maintenance 8,400, so 1,600 of loss = 2.29% price move) — dead in 3.7 ticks, about 18 seconds, with no communication needed because mobbing is the obvious +EV play for all five independently. Then the forced sale of 70,000 notional moves price another 3.5% into the mobbers' hands: five seats holding 20,000 opposite each collect ~$700 of in-game equity on a $60 pot. Solved play is therefore: run 2-3x, never lead the leverage table, wait for someone else to, mob them. It is discoverable in about six games, it makes the 8x dial dead above 4x, and it makes the private signal layer — the TEE, the entire whyOnChain argument, three weeks of build — nearly irrelevant to outcomes.
- **Technical blockers:**
  - The market impact coefficient is mis-scaled by roughly 5-10x against the signal, and this single number breaks the game. Signal edge: three-state drift ±0.40%/tick at p=0.72 gives conditional expected drift ≈ 0.44 × 0.40% = 0.18%/tick against 0.60% noise (per-tick Sharpe ≈ 0.30). Player flow: 25% of 10,000 equity = 2,500 notional/tick, impact 0.05% per 1,000 = 0.125%/tick. So ONE seat's maximum order carries 0.7x the entire information edge, and five seats acting together carry 0.625%/tick = 3.5x the drift. The Kyle/Glosten-Milgrom structure the design correctly cites works because informed flow is SMALL relative to market depth; here aggregate levered capacity is 6 × 80,000 = 480,000 notional against a book whose depth is 1,000 notional per 5bp (≈2M notional per 100%). The participants are 24% of the entire market. Prices are determined by flow, not information, and the private signals — the whole reason for the TEE, the whole on-chain justification — become decoration.
  - Intra-tick ordering is a total last-mover advantage and the design's defence is backwards. 5-second ticks on 10ms ER slots = ~500 slots per tick. If orders apply sequentially at arrival, the order submitted in the final 50ms observes all five other seats' flow and trades against a price it already knows. That is worth up to 0.625%/tick of known movement, every tick, with zero inference. The design says '500 slots of headroom so latency is not an edge' — latency is not an edge for reaction time, it is a decisive edge for SEQUENCE. The known fix is a frequent batch auction: collect all orders in a tick, clear at one uniform price, arrival-order-blind (Budish/Cramton/Shim 2015). That is a mechanism change, not a parameter.
  - Nobody is specified to run the tick. 96 price-process advances per match must be cranked. If the crank is permissionless, a player can time it around the 4%/tick regime-transition draw. If it is the operator's server, every table has a centralised liveness dependency and a missed crank at tick 70 with a levered position is a refund event and a Twitter post. Same for liquidation triggering — it must be inside the crank, not player-invoked, or the last-mover problem compounds into who calls the liquidation.
  - ER transactions cost 0 SOL base fee and there is no per-seat order-count cap specified — only a notional cap. A bot submits 500 probe orders per tick for free. The rate limiter is the ER validator, i.e. someone else's infrastructure. Needs an explicit per-seat per-tick order count cap enforced in-program.
  - The MVP fallback is not a degraded product, it is a non-product. 'If Private ER integration eats more than 8 days, v1 ships with signals in a standard ER and a public disclosure that signals are not TEE-hidden — the game still works, it just requires trusting me.' No. In a standard ER the delegated account's contents are readable; all six seats' private regime reads are visible to anyone who reads the account. Every player has perfect information about every other player's signal. That is not 'trust me,' that is the game being over. This fallback should be deleted from the plan, and PER integration should be week 1, not a contingency.
  - The sharding rationale is confused (correct conclusion, wrong reason): the 12M CU per writable account per block ceiling is an L1 constraint, and in-match order flow lives in the ER where matches are separate delegated accounts. L1 only sees one commit per match at settlement. Per-match PDAs are right, but 'a global market account hits the ceiling at ~60 concurrent matches' is not the reason and suggests the ER execution model is not fully internalised yet.
  - The liquidation engine is a small margin exchange and it is always underestimated. Forced-sell into own impact, negative-equity holes when the forced sale moves price past the liquidation price, partial liquidations, and the accounting invariant that the six seats' equity always sums to 60,000 minus nothing. Getting this right without a hole a player can drain is 2 weeks on its own.
- **Economic holes:**
  - The NPC market-maker float has a ~50-table lifespan, and it is the load-bearing cold-start mechanism. NPCs run a PUBLISHED mean-reversion strategy in a market where impact is permanent (no reversion process is specified), against a genuine ±0.40%/tick regime drift. A mean-reverting maker with no reversion to revert to is short every trend by construction — it is not 'edge-zero,' it is structurally negative. Four NPC seats at $10 each = $40 of real buy-in per table. If humans reliably beat them, $2,000 float / $40 = 50 tables ≈ 8 hours at the target volume. The design budgets '$500 of bleed while tuning.' The bleed rate is $40/table, not a one-time tuning cost, and it scales linearly with the only growth number that matters.
  - This is also a house position, which is the thing the design explicitly promises never to take, and the design says so out loud in biggestRisk. It is the classic 'payout funded by a subsidy pool rather than by consumption' structure — kill-list item #1 wearing a market-maker costume. The countdown is 50 tables long.
  - Revenue requires concurrency the design cannot manufacture. $14.4k/month = 200 matches/day = ~1,200 player-sessions/day = ~200 DAU each playing six 8-minute games. The design admits week one is 8 matches/day = $19/day. There is no intermediate state described between $19/day and $480/day, and every mechanism that bridges the gap (NPCs) costs $40/table.
  - The $4 rake cap makes the $50 tier 1.6%, which the design frames as retaining serious players. It also means the top tier — explicitly described as 'a solver arena' with a published bot API — is the lowest-margin product. You are subsidising the tier where humans have the least reason to sit.
  - Maximum leverage is 8x but usable leverage is ~4x (see degenerate strategy), so the headline mechanic — 'borrow eight times your money until it kills you' — is 50% decoration.
- **Cold start:** Dead as designed, and the two survival mechanisms each destroy something essential. At 8 concurrent players you get four heads-up tables. But heads-up removes the design's own stated premise — 'the aggregate of five other reads is worth far more than your own' — and with two seats and public positions, heads-up collapses immediately into the leverage duel above: lever less than your opponent, push, done. It is a coin flip on who blinks, not a Kyle model. The other mechanism, NPC seats, is a $2,000 float that funds 50 tables. So the honest answer is: TAPE at 8 concurrent players is a leverage-chicken game against a bleeding house bot, and the kill test the designer proposes (do 2-human/4-NPC tables match 6-human liquidation rate and price entropy by end of week 2?) is the right test and will almost certainly fail, because a published-strategy maker is deterministic and six humans are not. Also note the scheduled-bells plan is the correct idea and is the only one that does not cost money — it should be the primary answer, not the third.
- **Bot extraction:** Week one, approximately 100% of all human money in the system, which at week-one volume is only ~$280/day but is the entire deposit base. The bot needs none of the private signals. It needs: the public position book, the deterministic liquidation price of every seat, and the last 50ms of each 5-second tick. Sniping intra-tick sequence is worth up to 0.625%/tick (5 other seats × 0.125% impact) of already-observed price movement, versus a 0.18%/tick information edge — a 3.5:1 advantage requiring zero inference, available 96 times per game. Add mechanical liquidation-hunting: the bot computes every seat's kill price exactly and pushes whenever a seat is within 4 ticks of it. Against five humans that bot wins ~90%+ of tables it sits at, taking 0.60 × $57.60 = $34.56 per win; at the design's own week-one volume of 8 matches/day that is ~$280/day, i.e. all of it. A well-capitalised operator does not scale this by capital, they scale it by seats — the per-wallet cap of one seat per table means they need N wallets to occupy N seats, and wallets are free, so the real ceiling is how many humans exist to sit opposite. That is the point: the bot's absolute take is small only because the game is small, and its take is ~100% of the game.
- **Prior art:** The mechanic is well-explored and the money version keeps not working. Figgie (Jane Street, invented 2013, free official iOS/Android app, 4-5 players, private information about the goal suit, public trading actions) is the canonical multiplayer trading game and is the direct free competitor — and it already has an established bot arena (codeclash.ai/arenas/figgie, 0xDub/figgie-auto on GitHub) whose answer to 'do bots beat humans' is unambiguously yes. Market-making interview simulators (marketmakinggames.com, tradermath.org) own the free real-time multiplayer version for the exact quant-adjacent audience TAPE would recruit from. Crypto Trading Simulator on Steam ships the leverage/liquidation fantasy as single-player. Most directly: Rush Trade (rush.trade) is LIVE on Solana on MagicBlock right now — 1000x leverage, 10-second positions, one-tap, fully on-chain — targeting the identical audience on the identical infrastructure, and it is player-vs-house, so it has no concurrency problem at all. That is the competitor that gets to 8 users and still works. Academically, this is Kyle (1985) and Glosten-Milgrom (1985); the designer is right that the structure is rich, but linear permanent impact plus Gaussian noise plus informed traders is precisely the case with a known closed-form equilibrium, so a solver can precompute considerably more than 'nobody has solved it in forty years' implies.
- **Suggested fix:** Change the microstructure, not the marketing. (1) Frequent batch auction: all orders in a tick clear at one uniform price, arrival-order-blind. This kills intra-tick sniping outright and is a two-day change. (2) Cut the impact coefficient by 5x (1,000 notional → 0.01%) so one seat's max order is ~0.025%/tick against a 0.18%/tick signal edge. Information should dominate flow, not the reverse; that is what makes it a Kyle model instead of a shoving match. (3) Randomise the end tick uniformly over 90-102 AND settle on a 20-tick VWAP rather than the last mark. Kills the closing ramp. (4) Cut max leverage to 3x and make liquidation partial (close 50% of position, not all), which removes the public-execution-date property that makes mobbing deterministic. (5) Fund NPC seats from accumulated rake with a hard daily stop-loss and publish that stop-loss, or delete NPCs and make scheduled bells (12:00/20:00 UTC, escrow-to-register, refund on no-show) the sole cold-start answer — it is free, it is the one mechanism in the plan that manufactures concurrency rather than faking it, and eight people showing up at 20:00 UTC is a real six-seat table. (6) Delete the 'ship without the TEE' fallback from the plan entirely.
