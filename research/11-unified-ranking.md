# Unified cross-wave ranking
All 45 concepts from all three waves, re-scored by three judges on one common rubric.
This supersedes the per-wave rankings. Wave 1's original ranking was built on a truncated
judge list that covered only 7 of its 21 concepts; wave 3 had never been judged at all.
Every judge here scored all 45 and self-verified coverage before returning.

**Judges.** A hardcore strategy gamer who does not care about crypto; a solo indie developer who has shipped four games and abandoned nine; a professional advantage player who breaks money games for a living.

**Rubric.** Eight dimensions, 0-10 each, 80 maximum. Legal exposure is excluded — it was dropped as a design constraint partway through the exercise.

| | dimension | what it asks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `depth` | depthAndFun | Strip out the money. Is the game still interesting? |
| `skill` | skillCeiling | Does a strong player reliably beat a weak one over 100 rounds? |
| `tech` | technicalFeasibility | Does this actually run on Solana in 2026 at real player counts? |
| `chain` | onChainNecessity | Is the blockchain load-bearing, or decorative? |
| `econ` | economicDurability | Does it survive month 6 without new money entering? |
| `cold` | coldStartResilience | Does it work with 8 players online? |
| `ship` | soloShippability | Real MVP in 4-8 weeks by one person? |
| `orig` | originality | Genuinely new, or a reskin? |

## Wave comparison

| wave | n | mean | best | worst |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — broad, seven lenses | 21 | **34.0** | 39.7 | 24.0 |
| 2 — solo-dev targeted | 12 | **46.2** | 52.3 | 42.7 |
| 3 — asymmetric randomness allowed | 12 | **36.6** | 46.7 | 29.0 |

Wave 2 dominates decisively — it takes 11 of the top 16 places and beats wave 1 by more than
12 points on average. The lesson is about targeting, not luck: wave 1 generated against a broad
brief and produced concepts for a builder who does not exist, while wave 2 generated against
the actual constraints (one developer, weeks not months, no art, strategy depth, asynchronous).
Every wave-1 concept ranks 17th or lower.

Wave 3 was the experiment in removing the compliance gate to recover asymmetric randomness.
It beat wave 1 but not wave 2, and the reason is visible in the scores: its best concepts
(ASSAY, MYCELIA) score highest in the set on `cold` and `chain` but collapse on `econ` —
ASSAY::market-native-sim scores 1.7/10 on economic durability. Freeing up variance produced
sharper mechanics and worse businesses.

## Where the judges converged

### advantage-player

The set gets one thing right consistently and one thing wrong consistently. Right: a real minority of these designs (SALVO, DOCTRINE, MILLWRIGHT, LOCKMAKER, Foundry) understand that a durable money game either makes bots non-adversarial (they pay the same rake, or they ARE the content) or makes the equilibrium strategy unprofitable against the rake -- that is the only way a solo dev's bankroll survives contact with people like me. Wrong: the overwhelming majority of the other 40 concepts hand a professional an exploit that isn't a skill edge at all, it's an arithmetic bug -- a dominant strategy, a free option, an impossible-to-lose branch, or a solved MDP -- and the attached critiques found one in nearly every design, usually within minutes of reading the payout formula. Push-your-luck and crash-game reskins (ASSAY, DELVE, Fuse) are the worst offenders: they are solved casino games with an unconvincing coat of on-chain paint, and the 'verifiable RTP' pitch doesn't matter if the RTP is worse than the incumbent (Stake/Bustabit already run 1% edges; several of these run 3-8%). The strategy-duel concepts (SALVO, DOCTRINE, Entente, Foundry) are the most economically sound because skill genuinely compounds over a season and the house never takes a position -- but almost every one of them has a cold-start problem that is worse than its designers admit, because the interesting metagame needs a population the solo dev will not have in week one. And a wide swath of the set (Wild Type, Callout, Hunch, Commons, BULWARK) has so little real money in the loop that the whole premise of this review -- can a pro drain it -- doesn't even apply; they are fine free games wearing a wallet.

**Would build:** DOCTRINE::wagered-strategy-a. The daily hidden field means there is no fixed strategy to converge on -- the metagame has to keep re-forming every 24 hours, which is the same property that kept Core War and Axelrod's tournaments alive for decades -- and the near-miss refund curve (ranks 25-40 get roughly their entry back) is the cheapest, smartest retention mechanic in the entire set for keeping losing players at the table instead of chasing them off.

**Easiest to break:** NINE FRONTS::house-and-oracle. The Colonel Blotto payout rule (won troops return 1.10x, lost troops vanish) has a corner-solution dominant strategy -- stack everything on one front and force the house's counter-strategy to match you exactly there -- and combined with the self-calibrating, backward-looking payout ladder being trivially poisonable by a single wallet at low volume, the critique's own estimate is that a bot operator drains the entire house bankroll inside 48 hours, something the design's own risk model claims has a 3x10^-7 chance of happening.

### solo-shipper

Across all 45, the pattern is consistent: nearly every concept's stated '2-4 week solo MVP' is a fantasy once you weigh the attached critique. Where wave 3 gives an explicit critic 'weeks' estimate, it lands 7-16 weeks against an implied 4-5 week plan -- a 2-4x miss, exactly the range I'd have predicted before reading a word of these docs. Wave 1's flashier, more cinematic concepts (real-time action, market-mechanism novelty) are consistently the worst offenders on cold start: they need 16-60 simultaneous humans in week one, which a solo dev will never have, and several critiques concede as much in their own biggestRisk sections. The concepts that actually survive scrutiny share one property almost without exception: a player's 'turn' is a file, a doctrine, or a standing order submitted asynchronously, not a body that has to be in a chair at the same moment as seven strangers. DOCTRINE, ATTRITION, REDOUBT (both), MILLWRIGHT, LOCKMAKER, STRAIN, MYCELIA, and the two best ASSAY variants all get this right, and it shows directly in their coldStartResilience scores. The other consistent failure mode is a formula error masquerading as a business model -- sink math off by four or five orders of magnitude, a countdown that mathematically can't reach zero, a house edge (DELVE's 3%, ASSAY market-native's b=5) that caps lifetime revenue under a dollar a day -- errors that are individually one-line fixes but collectively suggest nobody ran the numbers before writing the pitch. The genuinely best-matched-to-this-builder concepts are the two Zachtronics-lineage puzzle games (MILLWRIGHT, LOCKMAKER): a proven-fun genre independent of money, an honest and real onChainNecessity case (a leaderboard nobody can fake, royalties nobody can strip), and MVPs that show actual engineering restraint -- chunked ticks, byte-budgeted transactions -- instead of a hand-waved 'Week 3: wire it all up.' If I have one broad complaint about the set, it's that the wagering layer is bolted onto almost every concept whether or not the design needs it; the ones that would be better games with the money simply deleted (Wild Type, Crashout, Dead Drop, Callout) are not rare exceptions, they're most of wave 1.

**Would build:** MILLWRIGHT::pve-and-coop -- it's the one concept in the set that's already a great game with the money deleted (Zachtronics leaderboards are the best-documented long-tail retention curve in the medium), needs zero concurrent players to be complete, and its four-week plan is the only one in 45 that shows someone actually budgeted CU and transaction bytes instead of hoping they'd fit.

### strategy-player

Across all 45, real strategic depth and on-chain necessity are almost never the same axis. The handful of concepts with genuine strip-the-money-out depth (DOCTRINE, MILLWRIGHT, Entente, Foundry, LOCKMAKER, ASSAY market-native-sim, MYCELIA, TENURE) mostly don't need a blockchain for the interesting part — the chain buys them trustless escrow, unforgeable timestamps, or credible commitment, real but modest wins bolted onto designs that were already good. The reverse failure is far more common and far more instructive: concepts where the on-chain mechanic IS the whole idea (Vaultbreak's atomicity, Frontrun's ordering auction, Dead Drop's public-ledger surveillance, Longshot's price-as-rules, NINE FRONTS's histogram-house) are the most original ideas in the entire set and every single one collapses the moment you check the arithmetic — degenerate dominant strategies, impossible byte budgets, bots clearing the board in one slot. A large fraction of both wave 1 and wave 3 are gambling primitives wearing strategy-game vocabulary: crash games (Fuse), Mines clones (DELVE), DFS wrappers (ASSAY variance-strategy) that do not survive 'is this still fun with the money stripped out' for even a minute — DELVE's own critique says outright there is no meta and no opponent, which is disqualifying for anyone who plays for the game rather than the wager. The hostile critiques earned their keep: nearly every stated MVP timeline is 2-3x optimistic by its own review's estimate, and several 'novel' mechanics (Harberger taxes, Vickrey auctions with free losing bids, deterministic solo-optimization assault phases) have already been tried in exactly this space and failed for reasons the critiques correctly name. The genuinely encouraging pattern: PvE/solo-shippable, asynchronous designs (MILLWRIGHT, MYCELIA, ASSAY persistent-stakes-world, Commons) score highest on average precisely because removing the requirement for synchronous strangers also removes most of the failure modes — snowballing, collusion, dead lobbies — that kill the PvP concepts.

**Would build:** MILLWRIGHT::pve-and-coop — it's Opus Magnum with a verifiable on-chain leaderboard and a real economy for trading optimized solutions, meaning it's a proven, decades-deep genre rather than a novel mechanic hoping to be fun. It's also the rare concept here that doesn't need anyone else online to be worth playing on day one, which is the actual test of whether something is a game or just a market wearing a game's clothes.

## Per-concept panel takes

**1. MILLWRIGHT** (pve-and-coop, wave 2) — 52.3/80

> A weekly engineering puzzle where you build a machine on a grid, the chain runs it and stamps the exact cycle count nobody can fake — and if your machine is good, other players pay to bolt a sealed copy of it into theirs.

- [advantage-player] The strongest economic model in the set: bots are the content pipeline, not a threat, revenue is subscription plus module royalties rather than adversarial wagering, and the Zachtronics genre has a decade-proven long tail.
- [solo-shipper] The best-engineered MVP in all 45 concepts -- Zachtronics-proven fun, a real verifiable-leaderboard case for the chain, works solo at N=1, and the tx-chunked CU budget shows someone actually did the math.
- [strategy-player] This is Opus Magnum with a verifiable leaderboard and a real module economy — one of the deepest, most proven genres in the medium — and it barely needs other players online to be worth playing.

**2. LOCKMAKER** (unused-primitives, wave 2) — 50.0/80

> A puzzle game where players build tiny signal-routing locks and everyone else pays a few cents to try to pick them — and the person who designed the lock gets paid every time, forever, including a cut to whoever's lock they remixed.

- [advantage-player] Smart bot-tolerant design (attempts cost money whether or not the solver succeeds, so bots are paying customers), but it's a two-sided marketplace with a classic cold-start problem: nobody authors locks when there's no audience yet.
- [solo-shipper] TIS-100-as-a-marketplace with ancestry royalties is a genuinely great, well-scoped idea -- the UGC chicken-and-egg problem (nobody authors without an audience) is the honest risk, and it's the right one to worry about.
- [strategy-player] TIS-100-style signal-routing golf with an ancestry-royalty marketplace on top is genuinely deep puzzle design; the marketplace needs authors and authors need an audience that doesn't exist yet.

**3. DOCTRINE** (wagered-strategy-a, wave 2) — 48.7/80

> Every day you get 20 minutes and a rulebook to design an autonomous commander, then every design in the world fights every other design head to head, and the top fifth of the field splits the pot.

- [advantage-player] Best-designed anti-solve mechanism in the set: the field is hidden until the daily deadline and shifts every day, so there's no fixed strategy to converge on, and bots are the intended, non-adversarial audience.
- [solo-shipper] Async doctrine-authoring tournament where a player IS a file, not a body in a chair -- one of the best cold-start answers in the set, if the sandbox UI doesn't eat the timeline.
- [strategy-player] The best idea in the whole set for someone who actually likes strategy: author rules, fight the entire hidden field daily, adapt to a real evolving metagame — Battlecode/Screeps done right, if it ever gets enough entrants to have a meta at all.

**4. Entente** (wagered-strategy-b, wave 2) — 47.0/80

> Five players carve up a map through negotiation and betrayal, everyone posts a bond so nobody can rage-quit, and the pot pays out the moment the last army moves.

- [advantage-player] The deepest game in the whole set on pure design merit (it's Diplomacy with bonds fixing the abandonment problem), but a 3-week cadence is a hard sell to a crypto-native audience that churns in days.
- [solo-shipper] Diplomacy with bonded bad-faith insurance finally fixes the genre's actual disease (abandonment), but a 3-week correspondence game targets an audience that churns in 3 days.
- [strategy-player] This is just Diplomacy, one of the deepest strategy games ever designed, with a forfeit bond bolted on to fix the abandonment problem that's plagued every free correspondence server for decades — genuinely smart use of real stakes.

**5. STRAIN** (unused-primitives, wave 2) — 47.0/80

> Every Monday a verifiable coin flip nobody can rig — not even me — publishes the week's environment, and you spend five days breeding a creature from the genes you actually own to survive it better than anyone else's.

- [advantage-player] Real optimization depth in the fitness landscape, but the designer's own risk section admits the epistasis could be shallow enough that the community maps the whole landscape and collapses to one genome by week six.
- [solo-shipper] Weekly VRF-revealed breeding puzzle with real epistatic depth; the author self-corrects to 5-6 weeks and hand-tunes the fitness function for days, which is the kind of honesty that should be rewarded.
- [strategy-player] Breeding against a hidden, weekly-revealed fitness landscape is real optimization depth, comparable to competitive breeding metagames, contingent entirely on the landscape being interesting enough not to get mapped in a month.

**6. ASSAY** (market-native-sim, wave 3) — 46.7/80

> A market where the events being bet on are generated by a hidden formula instead of by the news — you profit by figuring out the formula faster than everyone else, and your bot is allowed to play.

- [advantage-player] Best-engineered whyOnChain in the wave (verifiable resolution against a house-committed hidden model), but the LMSR liquidity parameter caps total possible revenue under $1/day forever, at any player count -- it can never grow into a business.
- [solo-shipper] The cleanest technical execution and the best skill/variance ratio of any concept here, capped by one tunable constant (b=5) that caps the entire business under a dollar a day forever -- an easy fix, which is rare.
- [strategy-player] Model a hidden data-generating process better than everyone else is a genuinely legitimate skill game, Numerai-shaped and durable — capped at under a dollar a day of extractable value at any player count, which is a business problem, not a depth problem.

**7. Foundry** (wagered-strategy-b, wave 2) — 46.0/80

> A month-long industrial economy where a hundred firms set production and prices once a day, the market clears from what everybody actually did, and the best operators split the entry pool.

- [advantage-player] Genuinely strong market-integrity design (on-chain clearing prevents front-running) with real behavioral-modeling depth, but a solo dev's actual week-one liquidity is eight firms, not the hundred the game needs to have a real market.
- [solo-shipper] The deepest market-sim of the batch and the most honest about the one thing that kills it: at 8 players the clearing price is just whatever the two most active people did.
- [strategy-player] A real market simulation with time-priority execution and population-level reasoning — genuinely EVE-market-adjacent — that needs a hundred active firms to not just be two guys setting the price.

**8. BLIGHTWATER** (pve-and-coop, wave 2) — 45.7/80

> Two hundred players farm one shared watershed that dies if everyone takes as much as they want, and the only thing that makes a promise not to overharvest actually credible is that you locked fifty real dollars behind it.

- [advantage-player] The cleanest case for real money as a costly, credible signal in the whole set -- bonded pacts among strangers is a genuinely chain-native mechanic -- but a single early defector can flip belief and race the whole commons to the bottom.
- [solo-shipper] The strongest true onChainNecessity claim in wave 2 (bonded, automatic slashing between strangers) -- but it's explicitly a 200-player design, and 8 players produces no tragedy and no commons.
- [strategy-player] Bonded cartels managing a shared, decaying resource is real political-economy depth, EVE-null-sec-adjacent, but it needs a defection cascade to not happen in week two, which is asking a lot of strangers.

**9. MYCELIA** (market-native-sim, wave 3) — 44.7/80

> You breed microbial strains whose genetics are secret even from you, sell breeding rights to strangers, and collect a cut of every descendant forever — enforced by the token itself, not by a promise.

- [advantage-player] The only genuinely non-Ponzi money flow in wave 3 -- payouts funded entirely by consumption -- but the flagship transfer-hook royalty mechanic doesn't actually work as specified, and 600 USDC buys a permanent, un-revocable claim on all future fee flow.
- [solo-shipper] The most economically honest design in the set (payouts funded purely by consumption, no faucet) and works solo at N=1, but its flagship differentiator -- a transfer hook taking a cut of sale price -- is technically false as specified.
- [strategy-player] Breeding under noise with real epistasis discovery and a genuine secondary market for the results is deep and consumption-funded rather than ponzi-shaped — the flagship royalty mechanism just doesn't work the way it's described.

**10. ASSAY** (persistent-stakes-world, wave 3) — 44.7/80

> A hidden ore field nobody has seen — not even me — where you pay a dollar a hole to learn what's underground, and the only way to cash in your knowledge is to dig it up in public where everyone can watch.

- [advantage-player] Best cold-start design of any concept reviewed -- the antagonist is the hidden map, not other players, so it's a real game at N=8 -- but the dominant strategy is to never drill and just snipe adjacent claims once someone else reveals a body.
- [solo-shipper] The best cold-start design of all 45 -- hidden geology means the antagonist is the map, not other players -- with one real unsolved infra dependency (60-day TEE seed persistence) standing between it and a genuine solo ship.
- [strategy-player] Hidden geology you deduce Bayesian-style, plus a real lemons market where silence is a signal, is genuinely Dark-Forest-shaped with the best cold start of any wagering concept here — undercut by an incentive structure where drilling funds the pool and not drilling wins it.

**11. REDOUBT** (wagered-strategy-a, wave 2) — 44.0/80

> You and one opponent each build a fortress on the identical map, then each of you gets one shot at cracking the other's — whoever solves the enemy puzzle better takes the pot, and neither of you is ever waiting for the other to be online.

- [advantage-player] Defense retains real opponent-modeling skill, but the assault half is a fully-specified public optimization problem that a weekend solver cracks -- half the game gets professionalized fast.
